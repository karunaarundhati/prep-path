Conditional expectation and variance

Conditional expectation is the average of a random quantity once you already know some extra information. The law of total expectation and the law of total variance split a hard average or spread into smaller, easier pieces, and GATE DA questions often ask you to do exactly that.

  • Law of total expectation
  • Conditional variance
  • Law of total variance and a checked example

The idea in plain words

Imagine guessing the marks a student will score in a test. Before you know anything about the student, your best guess is the average mark of the whole class. Now suppose you learn that the student studied for ten hours. Your guess should change. You are now averaging only the students who studied ten hours. That narrower average is a conditional expectation.

Take the words one at a time. Expectation means the long-run average of a random quantity. Conditional means "given some information". So a conditional expectation is "the long-run average, given that I know something".

Why does this matter for GATE? Data Science and AI questions often ask about a quantity that depends on another quantity, such as the average rainfall given the season or the average error of a model given an input group. Conditional expectation is the tool for these. The two laws in this page then combine the pieces back into the overall answer.

Key terms

Read these once now, and come back whenever a word feels unfamiliar. Each entry gives the plain meaning first and the precise definition second.

Random variable
A number that depends on the result of a random experiment. Precise definition: a rule that assigns a real number to each outcome, written with a capital letter such as X or Y.
Joint distribution
The probabilities for two random variables at the same time, usually shown as a table. Precise definition: P(X = x, Y = y).
Marginal probability
The probability of one variable on its own, found by adding a row or column of the joint table. Precise definition: P(Y = y) = sum over x of P(X = x, Y = y).
Conditional probability
The probability of one event once you know another has happened. Precise definition: P(X = x | Y = y) = P(X = x, Y = y) / P(Y = y).
Expectation
The long-run average of a random variable. Precise definition: E[X] = sum over x of x times P(X = x).
Conditional expectation
The long-run average of X when you know Y = y. Precise definition: E[X | Y = y] = sum over x of x times P(X = x | Y = y).
E[X | Y]
A random variable that takes the value E[X | Y = y] whenever Y takes the value y, so it changes with the outcome.
Variance
How far values usually sit from the average. Precise definition: Var(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2.
Conditional variance
The spread of X inside one group. Precise definition: Var(X | Y = y) = E[X^2 | Y = y] - (E[X | Y = y])^2.
Law of total expectation
The overall average built from the group averages and the group sizes. Precise definition: E[X] = E[E[X | Y]].
Law of total variance
The overall spread split into two parts. Precise definition: Var(X) = E[Var(X | Y)] + Var(E[X | Y]).

Conditional expectation: an average given some information

A small example you can picture

Roll a fair six-sided die. Let X be the number that shows. Each face 1 to 6 has probability 1/6, and the overall average is 3.5.

Now let Y = 1 if the number is even, and Y = 0 if it is odd. Suppose you are told Y = 1. Then X can only be 2, 4 or 6, each with probability 1/3. The conditional expectation is the average of those three values:

E[X | Y = 1] = (2 + 4 + 6) / 3 = 4

If Y = 0, X can only be 1, 3 or 5, so E[X | Y = 0] = (1 + 3 + 5) / 3 = 3. The overall average was 3.5. Knowing the number is even moved the guess up to 4, and knowing it is odd moved it down to 3. The extra information changed the answer. This is exactly what conditioning does.

The formula, symbol by symbol

E[X | Y = y] = sum over x of x times P(X = x | Y = y)
  • E[ ... ] means "the average of".
  • X | Y = y means "X, given that Y equals y".
  • sum over x means "add up for every possible value x of X".
  • P(X = x | Y = y) is the probability that X equals x once we know Y = y.

It is a weighted average. Each value x is multiplied by its conditional probability, and the results are added. For the die, each even value has weight 1/3, so the sum is 2 times 1/3 plus 4 times 1/3 plus 6 times 1/3, which is 4.

Getting the conditional probabilities from a table

In most GATE questions you are given a joint table, not the conditional probabilities. Use this rule:

P(X = x | Y = y) = P(X = x, Y = y) / P(Y = y)

Take the cell for the pair (x, y) and divide by the total of its row (or column) for y. This is the only division you need.

Key ideaA conditional expectation is a weighted average over only the outcomes where the condition holds. Restrict to the condition, re-scale the probabilities so they add to 1, then average.

TrapE[X | Y = y] is one number for each y. E[X | Y] is a random variable, because its value depends on which y actually occurred.

Self-check: if X and Y are independent, what is E[X | Y = y]?

If Y tells you nothing about X, the conditional probabilities of X are the ordinary ones. So E[X | Y = y] = E[X] for every y.

Self-check: a fair die shows an odd number. What is the conditional expectation of X?

The odd values are 1, 3 and 5, each with probability 1/3. The average is (1 + 3 + 5) / 3 = 3.

Law of total expectation

The idea

Suppose you know the average inside each group, and how big each group is. The law of total expectation combines them. Think of two sections of a class: with each section's average mark and size, you can find the class average without re-reading every mark.

E[X] = sum over y of E[X | Y = y] times P(Y = y)

Here E[X] is the overall average, E[X | Y = y] is the average inside group y, and P(Y = y) is the size of that group as a fraction of everything. Compactly, the same law is E[X] = E[E[X | Y]].

A full worked example with a joint table

Let X take the values 1, 2 or 3, and let Y take the values 0 or 1. Each cell gives P(X = x, Y = y).

Joint probabilityX = 1X = 2X = 3Row total P(Y = y)
Y = 00.10.20.10.4
Y = 10.10.30.20.6
Column total P(X = x)0.20.50.31.0

Step 1: the group sizes are P(Y = 0) = 0.4 and P(Y = 1) = 0.6. They add to 1.

Step 2: inside Y = 0, divide the row by 0.4. That gives P(X = 1 | Y = 0) = 1/4, P(X = 2 | Y = 0) = 1/2 and P(X = 3 | Y = 0) = 1/4. They add to 1, as they should.

E[X | Y = 0] = 1 times 1/4 + 2 times 1/2 + 3 times 1/4 = 2

Step 3: inside Y = 1, divide the row by 0.6. That gives 1/6, 1/2 and 1/3, which add to 1.

E[X | Y = 1] = 1 times 1/6 + 2 times 1/2 + 3 times 1/3 = 13/6

Step 4: combine the groups with the law of total expectation:

E[X] = 2 times 0.4 + (13/6) times 0.6 = 0.8 + 1.3 = 2.1

Step 5: check from the column totals. E[X] = 1 times 0.2 + 2 times 0.5 + 3 times 0.3 = 0.2 + 1.0 + 0.9 = 2.1. The two methods agree.

TrapDo not take the simple average of the group means. (6 + 4) / 2 = 5 is only right when both groups are equally large. If 90 per cent of the calls came from the group with mean 6, the answer is 5.8.

Self-check: E[X | Y = 0] = 10, E[X | Y = 1] = 20 and P(Y = 0) = 0.75. What is E[X]?

P(Y = 1) = 1 - 0.75 = 0.25. Then E[X] = 10 times 0.75 + 20 times 0.25 = 7.5 + 5 = 12.5.

Conditional variance

Spread, in plain words

Variance measures how far values sit from the average. The conditional variance measures that spread inside one group, once you know Y = y.

Var(X | Y = y) = E[X^2 | Y = y] - (E[X | Y = y])^2
  • X^2 is X multiplied by itself, so 3 becomes 9.
  • E[X^2 | Y = y] is the average of X squared, inside the group Y = y.
  • (E[X | Y = y])^2 is the square of the group average.

In words: find the average of the squares, then subtract the square of the average.

Worked example using the same table

Inside Y = 0, the probabilities are 1/4, 1/2 and 1/4 for X = 1, 2, 3, and E[X | Y = 0] = 2.

E[X^2 | Y = 0] = 1^2 times 1/4 + 2^2 times 1/2 + 3^2 times 1/4 = 1/4 + 2 + 9/4 = 9/2
Var(X | Y = 0) = 9/2 - 2^2 = 9/2 - 4 = 1/2

Inside Y = 1, the probabilities are 1/6, 1/2 and 1/3, and E[X | Y = 1] = 13/6.

E[X^2 | Y = 1] = 1 times 1/6 + 4 times 1/2 + 9 times 1/3 = 1/6 + 2 + 3 = 31/6
Var(X | Y = 1) = 31/6 - (13/6)^2 = 186/36 - 169/36 = 17/36

So the spread is 1/2 inside Y = 0 and 17/36, about 0.472, inside Y = 1.

Key ideaA variance is never negative. A negative answer means an arithmetic slip, usually a forgotten square or an unconditional probability used by mistake.

TrapE[X^2 | Y = y] is the average of the squares, not the square of the average. The average of the squares is always at least as large as the square of the average.

Self-check: X is 2 or 4 with equal probability inside a group. What is the conditional variance?

The mean is 3. The average of the squares is (4 + 16) / 2 = 10. So Var = 10 - 3^2 = 1.

Self-check: can the spread inside one group be larger than the overall spread?

Yes. A small, very spread-out group can sit inside a large, nearly constant population. Practice problem 8 gives a concrete case where the conditional variance is 2500 but the overall variance is 49.75.

Law of total variance and a checked example

The idea

If you know the spread inside each group and how the group averages differ, you can get the total spread. The law of total variance says:

Var(X) = E[Var(X | Y)] + Var(E[X | Y])
  • E[Var(X | Y)] is the average spread inside the groups, weighted by group size. This is the within-group part.
  • Var(E[X | Y]) is the spread of the group averages. This is the between-group part. It is zero when every group has the same average.

Key ideaVariance is not a simple average of the group variances. You must add the between-group part. Forgetting it is the most common error in this topic.

Step by step with the running example

The pieces are: Var(X | Y = 0) = 1/2, Var(X | Y = 1) = 17/36, P(Y = 0) = 2/5, P(Y = 1) = 3/5, E[X | Y = 0] = 2, E[X | Y = 1] = 13/6 and E[X] = 21/10.

Within-group part:

E[Var(X | Y)] = 1/2 times 2/5 + 17/36 times 3/5 = 1/5 + 17/60 = 29/60

Between-group part. E[X | Y] equals 2 with probability 2/5 and 13/6 with probability 3/5, and its mean is 21/10:

Var(E[X | Y]) = 2/5 times (2 - 21/10)^2 + 3/5 times (13/6 - 21/10)^2 = 2/5 times 1/100 + 3/5 times 1/225 = 1/250 + 1/375 = 1/150

Add the two parts:

Var(X) = 29/60 + 1/150 = 145/300 + 2/300 = 147/300 = 49/100 = 0.49

Check directly: E[X^2] = 4.9, so Var(X) = 4.9 - (2.1)^2 = 0.49. The two methods agree.

GATE loves thisA question may give E[Var(X | Y)] and Var(E[X | Y]) and ask for Var(X), which is just addition.

Self-check: E[Var(X | Y)] = 3 and Var(E[X | Y]) = 5. What is Var(X)?

Var(X) = 3 + 5 = 8. Add the two parts. Do not multiply them.

Self-check: Var(X) = 10 and E[Var(X | Y)] = 6. What is Var(E[X | Y])?

Var(E[X | Y]) = 10 - 6 = 4.

A Python check of the whole example

Here is a standard-library program that uses exact fractions, so there is no rounding. Each function mirrors one step of the hand calculation.

from fractions import Fraction as F

# joint probabilities P(X=x, Y=y), stored as exact fractions
joint = {
    (1, 0): F(1, 10), (2, 0): F(2, 10), (3, 0): F(1, 10),
    (1, 1): F(1, 10), (2, 1): F(3, 10), (3, 1): F(2, 10),
}

def p_y(y):
    # group size
    return sum(p for (x, yy), p in joint.items() if yy == y)

def cond_mean(y):
    return sum(x * p for (x, yy), p in joint.items() if yy == y) / p_y(y)

def cond_second(y):
    return sum(x * x * p for (x, yy), p in joint.items() if yy == y) / p_y(y)

def cond_var(y):
    return cond_second(y) - cond_mean(y) ** 2

for y in (0, 1):
    print(f"P(Y={y}) = {p_y(y)}")
    print(f"E[X|Y={y}] = {cond_mean(y)}")
    print(f"Var(X|Y={y}) = {cond_var(y)}")

ey = sum(cond_mean(y) * p_y(y) for y in (0, 1))
ex = sum(x * p for (x, y), p in joint.items())
print(f"E[X] by total expectation = {ey}")
print(f"E[X] directly = {ex}")

ev = sum(cond_var(y) * p_y(y) for y in (0, 1))
vm = sum(p_y(y) * (cond_mean(y) - ey) ** 2 for y in (0, 1))
print(f"E[Var(X|Y)] = {ev}")
print(f"Var(E[X|Y]) = {vm}")
print(f"Law of total variance = {ev + vm}")

ex2 = sum(x * x * p for (x, y), p in joint.items())
print(f"Var(X) directly = {ex2 - ex ** 2}")

P(Y=0) = 2/5
E[X|Y=0] = 2
Var(X|Y=0) = 1/2
P(Y=1) = 3/5
E[X|Y=1] = 13/6
Var(X|Y=1) = 17/36
E[X] by total expectation = 21/10
E[X] directly = 21/10
E[Var(X|Y)] = 29/60
Var(E[X|Y]) = 1/150
Law of total variance = 49/100
Var(X) directly = 49/100

The last two lines agree, which confirms the law of total variance for this table.

Solved GATE-style problems

These are original, GATE-style exercises written for practice. They are not past exam questions. Try each one on paper before reading the answer.

Overall mean from two groups

E[X | Y = 0] = 4 and E[X | Y = 1] = 10. P(Y = 0) = 0.75. Find E[X]. (Give a decimal.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The overall mean from two group means and the group sizes.
  2. Which idea applies. The law of total expectation.
  3. Work it out. P(Y = 1) = 1 - 0.75 = 0.25. Then E[X] = 4 times 0.75 + 10 times 0.25 = 3 + 2.5 = 5.5.
  4. Check. 5.5 lies between 4 and 10, and it is closer to 4, which is right because that group is bigger.

Answer: 5.5

Spread of two equally likely values

Inside one group, X is 2 with probability 1/2 and 4 with probability 1/2. Which is the conditional variance of X inside this group?

  • (A) 0
  • (B) 1
  • (C) 2
  • (D) 3

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The spread of X inside one group.
  2. Which idea applies. Var = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2, computed inside the group.
  3. Work it out. E[X] = 2 times 1/2 + 4 times 1/2 = 3. E[X^2] = 4 times 1/2 + 16 times 1/2 = 10. So Var = 10 - 3^2 = 1.
  4. Check. Each value is 1 away from the mean 3, so the squared distances are both 1.

Answer: (B) 1

Mean from a small joint table

X takes the values 0 and 1, and Y takes the values 1 and 2. P(X = 0, Y = 1) = 0.1, P(X = 1, Y = 1) = 0.4, P(X = 0, Y = 2) = 0.3 and P(X = 1, Y = 2) = 0.2. Find E[X | Y = 1] and E[X]. (Give decimals.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The mean inside Y = 1, then the overall mean.
  2. Which idea applies. Conditional probability, then the law of total expectation.
  3. Work it out. P(Y = 1) = 0.5. So P(X = 1 | Y = 1) = 0.4 / 0.5 = 0.8, and E[X | Y = 1] = 0.8. Similarly P(Y = 2) = 0.5 and P(X = 1 | Y = 2) = 0.2 / 0.5 = 0.4. Then E[X] = 0.8 times 0.5 + 0.4 times 0.5 = 0.6.
  4. Check. Directly, P(X = 1) = 0.4 + 0.2 = 0.6, and for a variable taking only 0 and 1, the mean equals P(X = 1).

Answer: E[X | Y = 1] = 0.8 and E[X] = 0.6

Which statements about conditional expectation are true?

Select all that apply.

  • (A) If X and Y are independent, E[X | Y] equals E[X] for every value of Y.
  • (B) E[E[X | Y]] = E[X] for any X and Y.
  • (C) A conditional variance Var(X | Y = y) can never be negative.
  • (D) If X takes only whole-number values, then E[X | Y = y] must be a whole number.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Judge each statement. More than one may be true.
  2. Which idea applies. Meaning of independence, the law of total expectation, and the fact that a variance is an average of squares.
  3. Work it out. (A) True: independence means Y tells nothing about X. (B) True: this is the law of total expectation. (C) True: a variance is an average of squared distances. (D) False: the average of 1 and 2 with equal weights is 1.5.
  4. Check. The counterexample for (D) is X equal to 1 or 2 with equal chance. Its mean is 1.5.

Answer: (A), (B) and (C). Select all that apply.

Total spread from two groups

A population is split into two equally large groups. In group 0, the conditional variance of X is 4 and the conditional mean is 1. In group 1, the conditional variance is 8 and the conditional mean is 5. Find Var(X). (Give a whole number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The overall variance, from group information.
  2. Which idea applies. The law of total variance.
  3. Work it out. Within-group part: 0.5 times 4 + 0.5 times 8 = 6. The overall mean of the group means is 0.5 times 1 + 0.5 times 5 = 3. Between-group part: 0.5 times (1 - 3)^2 + 0.5 times (5 - 3)^2 = 2 + 2 = 4. Total: 6 + 4 = 10.
  4. Check. The total must be at least the within-group part, 6, and it is.

Answer: 10

Variance from a joint table

X takes the values 0, 1 and 2, and Y takes the values 0 and 1. P(X = 0, Y = 0) = 0.2, P(X = 1, Y = 0) = 0.2, P(X = 2, Y = 0) = 0.1, P(X = 0, Y = 1) = 0.1, P(X = 1, Y = 1) = 0.3 and P(X = 2, Y = 1) = 0.1. Find Var(X). (Give a decimal.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The overall variance of X.
  2. Which idea applies. Group sizes, group means and variances, then the law of total variance. A direct check is also useful.
  3. Work it out. Group sizes are 0.5 and 0.5. Inside Y = 0 the probabilities are 0.4, 0.4 and 0.2, so the mean is 0.8, E[X^2] = 1.2 and Var = 1.2 - 0.64 = 0.56. Inside Y = 1 they are 0.2, 0.6 and 0.2, so the mean is 1.0, E[X^2] = 1.4 and Var = 0.4. Within-group part: 0.5 times 0.56 + 0.5 times 0.4 = 0.48. The overall mean is 0.9, so the between-group part is 0.5 times 0.01 + 0.5 times 0.01 = 0.01. Total: 0.49.
  4. Check. Directly, E[X] = 0.9 and E[X^2] = 0.5 times 1.2 + 0.5 times 1.4 = 1.3. So Var(X) = 1.3 - 0.81 = 0.49.

Answer: 0.49

Random number of coin tosses

Y is equally likely to be 1 or 2. Given Y = y, X counts the heads in y fair coin tosses. Find Var(X). (Give a decimal.) Verify your answer with the Python program in the solution.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The overall spread of X, where the number of tosses is random.
  2. Which idea applies. The law of total variance.
  3. Work it out. For y = 1, X is 0 or 1 with probability 1/2 each, so E = 1/2 and Var = 1/2 - 1/4 = 1/4. For y = 2, X is 0, 1, 2 with probabilities 1/4, 1/2, 1/4, so E = 1 and Var = 3/2 - 1 = 1/2. Overall mean: 3/4. Within-group part: 1/2 times 1/4 + 1/2 times 1/2 = 3/8. Between-group part: 1/2 times (1/2 - 3/4)^2 + 1/2 times (1 - 3/4)^2 = 1/16. Total: 3/8 + 1/16 = 7/16.
  4. Check. Directly, P(X = 0) = 3/8, P(X = 1) = 1/2, P(X = 2) = 1/8, so E[X^2] = 1 and Var = 1 - 9/16 = 7/16.
from fractions import Fraction as F
from math import comb

def binom_pmf(n, k):
    # probability of k heads in n fair tosses
    return F(comb(n, k), 2 ** n)

p_y = F(1, 2)
means, variances = {}, {}
for y in (1, 2):
    probs = {k: binom_pmf(y, k) for k in range(y + 1)}
    m = sum(k * p for k, p in probs.items())
    s2 = sum(k * k * p for k, p in probs.items())
    means[y] = m
    variances[y] = s2 - m * m
    print(f"Y={y}: E[X|Y]={m}, Var(X|Y)={variances[y]}")

ex = sum(means[y] * p_y for y in (1, 2))
ev = sum(variances[y] * p_y for y in (1, 2))
vm = sum(((means[y] - ex) ** 2) * p_y for y in (1, 2))
print(f"E[X]={ex}")
print(f"E[Var(X|Y)]={ev}")
print(f"Var(E[X|Y])={vm}")
print(f"Var(X)={ev + vm}")

Y=1: E[X|Y]=1/2, Var(X|Y)=1/4
Y=2: E[X|Y]=1, Var(X|Y)=1/2
E[X]=3/4
E[Var(X|Y)]=3/8
Var(E[X|Y])=1/16
Var(X)=7/16

Answer: 7/16, which is 0.4375

Testing the two laws

Select all that apply. X and Y have finite variances.

  • (A) Var(X) is always greater than or equal to E[Var(X | Y)].
  • (B) If Var(E[X | Y]) = 0, then E[X | Y] is the same constant for every outcome, and Var(X) = E[Var(X | Y)].
  • (C) Var(X | Y = y) can be larger than Var(X) for some value y.
  • (D) E[Var(X | Y)] is always equal to Var(X).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements hold for every pair of random variables.
  2. Which idea applies. The law of total variance, and a counterexample to test a claim.
  3. Work it out. (A) True: the second part is a variance, so it is at least 0. (B) True: the between-group part is zero. (C) True: take P(Y = 0) = 0.01 with X equal to 0 or 100 with equal chance, and P(Y = 1) = 0.99 with X = 0. Then Var(X | Y = 0) = 2500, but E[X] = 0.5, E[X^2] = 50 and Var(X) = 49.75. (D) False: equality needs Var(E[X | Y]) = 0.
  4. Check. The counterexample settles statement (C).

Answer: (A), (B) and (C). Select all that apply.

Exam-level 1: Faults from three batches (NAT, 2 marks)

A factory makes items in three production batches, numbered Y = 1, 2 or 3. A randomly chosen item comes from batch y with probability P(Y = 1) = 0.2, P(Y = 2) = 0.5 and P(Y = 3) = 0.3. Given that the item comes from batch y, the number of faults X on that item has mean E[X | Y = y] = 2y and variance Var(X | Y = y) = y + 1. Find Var(X) for a randomly chosen item. Give a decimal.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The overall variance of X, when the batch Y is not known.
  2. Which idea applies. The law of total variance: Var(X) = E[Var(X | Y)] + Var(E[X | Y]).
  3. Work it out. The within-batch part is E[Var(X | Y)] = 0.2 times 2 + 0.5 times 3 + 0.3 times 4 = 3.1. The batch number has E[Y] = 0.2 times 1 + 0.5 times 2 + 0.3 times 3 = 2.1 and E[Y^2] = 0.2 + 2 + 2.7 = 4.9, so Var(Y) = 4.9 - 2.1^2 = 0.49. Since E[X | Y] = 2Y, the between-batch part is Var(2Y) = 4 times 0.49 = 1.96. Add the two parts: Var(X) = 3.1 + 1.96 = 5.06.
  4. Check. The total must be at least the within-batch part, and 5.06 is larger than 3.1 by exactly the between-batch part 1.96.

Answer: Var(X) = 5.06

from fractions import Fraction as F
p = {1: F(2, 10), 2: F(5, 10), 3: F(3, 10)}
EY = sum(p[y] * y for y in p)
EY2 = sum(p[y] * y * y for y in p)
VarY = EY2 - EY ** 2
EVar = sum(p[y] * (y + 1) for y in p)
VarE = 4 * VarY
print(f"E[Var(X|Y)] = {float(EVar)}")
print(f"Var(E[X|Y]) = {float(VarE)}")
print(f"Var(X) = {float(EVar + VarE)}")
E[Var(X|Y)] = 3.1

Exam-level 2: Retries from two job types (NAT, 2 marks)

A cloud server handles jobs of two types, labelled Y = 0 (light) or Y = 1 (heavy). X is the number of retries a job needs, and X can be 0, 1 or 2. The joint probabilities are P(X = 0, Y = 0) = 0.1, P(X = 1, Y = 0) = 0.2, P(X = 2, Y = 0) = 0.2, P(X = 0, Y = 1) = 0.1, P(X = 1, Y = 1) = 0.1 and P(X = 2, Y = 1) = 0.3. Find the variance of X for a randomly chosen job, working through each job type first. Give a decimal.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Var(X) over all jobs, built from the two job types.
  2. Which idea applies. Conditional means and variances for each Y, then the law of total variance.
  3. Work it out. P(Y = 0) = 0.5 and P(Y = 1) = 0.5. Then E[X | Y = 0] = (0.2 + 0.4) / 0.5 = 1.2 and E[X | Y = 1] = (0.1 + 0.6) / 0.5 = 1.4, so E[X] = 1.3. Next E[X^2 | Y = 0] = (0.2 + 0.8) / 0.5 = 2.0, so Var(X | Y = 0) = 2.0 - 1.44 = 0.56. Also E[X^2 | Y = 1] = (0.1 + 1.2) / 0.5 = 2.6, so Var(X | Y = 1) = 2.6 - 1.96 = 0.64. Within-type part: 0.5 times 0.56 + 0.5 times 0.64 = 0.6. Between-type part: 0.5 times (1.2 - 1.3)^2 + 0.5 times (1.4 - 1.3)^2 = 0.01. Total: Var(X) = 0.6 + 0.01 = 0.61.
  4. Check. Directly, E[X^2] = 0.5 times 2.0 + 0.5 times 2.6 = 2.3, and 2.3 - 1.3^2 = 2.3 - 1.69 = 0.61.

Answer: Var(X) = 0.61

from fractions import Fraction as F
joint = {(0, 0): F(1, 10), (1, 0): F(2, 10), (2, 0): F(2, 10),
         (0, 1): F(1, 10), (1, 1): F(1, 10), (2, 1): F(3, 10)}
pY = {y: sum(p for (x, t), p in joint.items() if t == y) for y in (0, 1)}
mean = {y: sum(x * p for (x, t), p in joint.items() if t == y) / pY[y] for y in (0, 1)}
sq = {y: sum(x * x * p for (x, t), p in joint.items() if t == y) / pY[y] for y in (0, 1)}
within = {y: sq[y] - mean[y] ** 2 for y in (0, 1)}
EX = sum(pY[y] * mean[y] for y in (0, 1))
between = sum(pY[y] * (mean[y] - EX) ** 2 for y in (0, 1))
EVar = sum(pY[y] * within[y] for y in (0, 1))
print(f"E[X|Y=0] = {float(mean[0])}, E[X|Y=1] = {float(mean[1])}")
print(f"E[X] = {float(EX)}")
print(f"E[Var(X|Y)] = {float(EVar)}, Var(E[X|Y]) = {float(between)}")
print(f"Var(X) = {float(EVar + between)}")
EX2 = sum(x * x * p for (x, t), p in joint.items())
print(f"Check: E[X^2] - E[X]^2 = {float(EX2 - EX ** 2)}")

E[X] = 1.3
E[Var(X|Y)] = 0.6, Var(E[X|Y]) = 0.01
Var(X) = 0.61

Exam-level 3: Call-centre total time (NAT, 2 marks)

A call centre gets N calls in an hour, where N = 2 or N = 4, each with probability 1/2. Each call has a random length. Given N = n, the total call time T (in minutes) for that hour has mean E[T | N = n] = 3n and variance Var(T | N = n) = n. Find Var(T) for a randomly chosen hour, using the law of total variance. Give a whole number.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The spread of the total call time T over all hours.
  2. Which idea applies. The law of total variance, plus the variance of a two-point variable.
  3. Work it out. E[N] = 3 and Var(N) = (2 - 3)^2 times 1/2 + (4 - 3)^2 times 1/2 = 1. The within-hour part is E[Var(T | N)] = E[N] = 3. Since E[T | N] = 3N, the between-hour part is Var(3N) = 9 times Var(N) = 9. So Var(T) = 3 + 9 = 12.
  4. Check. The mean call time is E[T] = 3 times 3 = 9, and the between-hour part 9 is exactly the spread of 3N, which is 3 times the spread of N.

Answer: Var(T) = 12

from fractions import Fraction as F
p = {2: F(1, 2), 4: F(1, 2)}
EN = sum(p[n] * n for n in p)
VarN = sum(p[n] * (n - EN) ** 2 for n in p)
EVar = sum(p[n] * n for n in p)
VarE = 9 * VarN
print(f"E[N] = {EN}")
print(f"Var(N) = {VarN}")
print(f"E[Var(T|N)] = {EVar}")
print(f"Var(E[T|N]) = {VarE}")
print(f"Var(T) = {EVar + VarE}")

Exam-level 4: Which statements about the two laws hold (MSQ, 2 marks)

Let X and Y be random variables with finite variances. Select all that apply.

  • (A) If E[X | Y] equals the same constant c for every value of Y, then Cov(X, Y) = 0.
  • (B) If Var(X | Y = y) = 0 for every value y, then X is a function of Y.
  • (C) Var(X) is always at least Var(E[X | Y]).
  • (D) If E[X | Y] = Y, then X = Y for every outcome.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which of the four statements hold for every pair of random variables.
  2. Which idea applies. The law of total expectation, the law of total variance, and a counterexample to test a claim.
  3. Work it out. (A) True: E[XY] = E[Y E[X | Y]] = cE[Y] and E[X] = c, so Cov(X, Y) = cE[Y] - cE[Y] = 0. (B) True: zero conditional variance means X does not vary once Y is known. (C) True: Var(X) = E[Var(X | Y)] + Var(E[X | Y]), and the first term is never negative. (D) False: take Y equal to 0 or 1 with equal chance, and X = Y + Z, where Z is -1 or 1 with equal chance and independent of Y. Then E[X | Y] = Y, but X differs from Y half of the time.
  4. Check. The only wrong option is (D), and the counterexample in the Python block shows why. The computed conditional means equal Y, while P(X = Y) is 0.

Answer: (A), (B) and (C). Select all that apply.

from fractions import Fraction as F
prob = F(1, 4)
pairs = [(y, z) for y in (0, 1) for z in (-1, 1)]
for y in (0, 1):
    xs = [(y + z, prob) for (yy, z) in pairs if yy == y]
    pyy = sum(q for _, q in xs)
    cond = sum(x * q for x, q in xs) / pyy
    print(f"E[X | Y={y}] = {cond}")
pXeqY = sum(prob for (y, z) in pairs if y + z == y)
print(f"P(X = Y) = {pXeqY}")
E[X | Y=0] = 0
E[X | Y=1] = 1
P(X = Y) = 0

Summary

Conditional expectation
The average of X once you know Y = y. A weighted average over only the outcomes in that group.
Conditional probability
P(X = x | Y = y) = P(X = x, Y = y) / P(Y = y).
Group size
P(Y = y), the chance of landing in group y, read from the table.
Law of total expectation
E[X] = sum of E[X | Y = y] times P(Y = y), also written E[X] = E[E[X | Y]].
Conditional variance
Var(X | Y = y) = E[X^2 | Y = y] - (E[X | Y = y])^2, the spread inside one group.
Within-group spread
E[Var(X | Y)], the average of the conditional variances, weighted by group size.
Between-group spread
Var(E[X | Y]), the spread of the group averages.
Law of total variance
Var(X) = E[Var(X | Y)] + Var(E[X | Y]).
Variance shortcut
Var(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2.
Independence
When Y tells you nothing about X, so every conditional mean equals E[X].
Equal group averages
If all group averages are equal, the between-group part of the spread is zero.

Remember these

  1. To condition on Y = y, divide the row (or column) by its total, then average.
  2. The overall mean is the weighted average of the group means, with group sizes as weights.
  3. Conditional variance uses the shortcut: average of the squares, minus the square of the average.
  4. The law of total variance has two parts, and you must add both.
  5. If the group averages are all equal, the between-group part is zero.
  6. Check every total a second way, directly from the table.
