Mean, median, mode, SD, expectation (recap)

Mean, median, mode, standard deviation and expectation are the summary numbers behind almost every GATE RA statistics question. This recap gives the formulas, what each symbol means, and the two rules for transforming a random variable, which GATE uses constantly.

  • Variance and standard deviation
  • Expectation of discrete and continuous variables
  • Linearity of expectation and variance under a linear transform

The idea in plain words

A data set has a centre and a spread. The centre tells you where most values sit. The spread tells you how far they wander. Think of the heights in a class: the centre is "about 160 cm" and the spread is "most people are within 8 cm of that".

Expectation is the same idea for a random variable. It is the long-run average you would get if you repeated the experiment many times. If a die is rolled many times, the average of the results settles near 3.5, even though 3.5 never shows on a die. That long-run average is the expectation.

Why it matters for GATE RA: sensor readings, noise and random outcomes are all summarised by these numbers. Questions often transform a variable, such as Y = 3X + 2, and ask for the new mean and variance. The rules in Part 4 make that quick.

Key terms

Mean (average)
Plain meaning: add everything and divide by how many there are. Precise definition: the sum of the n values divided by n, written x̄ for a sample.
Median
Plain meaning: the middle value after sorting. Precise definition: the middle value of the sorted data, or the average of the two middle values when n is even.
Mode
Plain meaning: the most common value. Precise definition: the value that occurs most often in the data set.
Variance
Plain meaning: the average squared distance from the mean. Precise definition: Var(X) = E[(X − μ)²] for a random variable.
Standard deviation (SD)
Plain meaning: the typical distance from the mean, in the original units. Precise definition: the square root of the variance.
Expectation E[X]
Plain meaning: the long-run average of a random variable. Precise definition: Σ x × P(X = x) for a discrete variable, or ∫ x f(x) dx for a continuous one.
Probability density function (PDF)
Plain meaning: the height of the curve for a continuous variable. Precise definition: f(x) ≥ 0 with total area 1. Probabilities are areas, not heights.
Linearity
Plain meaning: expectation respects adding and scaling. Precise definition: E[aX + b] = aE[X] + b for constants a and b.

Part 1: Mean, median and mode

Take the data 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. There are 8 values.

Mean = (sum of values) / n

Mean. The sum is 2 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 5 + 5 + 7 + 9 = 40. Divide by n = 8, so the mean is 40/8 = 5.

Median. The data are already sorted. The middle two values are the 4th and 5th, which are 4 and 5. The median is their average, (4 + 5)/2 = 4.5. The median does not have to be a value in the data.

Mode. The value 4 appears three times, more than any other, so the mode is 4.

TrapFor the median you must sort the data first. Taking the middle of the list as it was written gives the wrong answer.

What is the median of 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11?

The middle two values are 5 and 7. Their average is 6.

Which measure of centre is better when one value is huge, as in 1, 2, 3, 100?

The median, which is 2.5. The mean, 26.5, is pulled up by the single large value.

Part 2: Variance and standard deviation

The mean gives the centre. The variance gives the spread. We measure each value's distance from the mean, square it so that positive and negative distances do not cancel, and average the squares.

Population variance = Σ (x − μ)² / N
  • x is one value and μ is the mean.
  • N is the number of values.

For the same data, μ = 5. The squared deviations are (2 − 5)² = 9, (4 − 5)² = 1 three times, (5 − 5)² = 0 twice, (7 − 5)² = 4, and (9 − 5)² = 16. Their sum is 9 + 3 + 0 + 4 + 16 = 32. So the population variance is 32/8 = 4, and the population SD is √4 = 2.

Sample variance = Σ (x − x̄)² / (n − 1)

When the data are a sample from a larger population, divide by n − 1 instead of n. This corrects a small bias. For our data, the sample variance is 32/7 ≈ 4.5714 and the sample SD is √(32/7) ≈ 2.1381.

import statistics

data = [2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9]
print("Mean:", statistics.mean(data))
print("Median:", statistics.median(data))
print("Mode:", statistics.mode(data))
print("Population variance:", statistics.pvariance(data))
print("Population SD:", statistics.pstdev(data))
print("Sample variance:", round(statistics.variance(data), 4))
print("Sample SD:", round(statistics.stdev(data), 4))
Mean: 5
Median: 4.5
Mode: 4
Population variance: 4
Population SD: 2.0
Sample variance: 4.5714
Sample SD: 2.1381

Key ideaUse N when the question describes the whole population, and n − 1 when it describes a sample. GATE usually says which one applies.

Why do we square the deviations?

Without squaring, positive and negative deviations cancel, and the average deviation is always zero. Squaring keeps every term positive.

What is the SD when the variance is 49?

SD = √49 = 7.

Part 3: Expectation of a random variable

Expectation is a weighted average. Each value is weighted by how likely it is.

Discrete variables

E[X] = Σ x × P(X = x)

A fair die has faces 1 to 6, each with probability 1/6. So E[X] = (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6)/6 = 21/6 = 3.5.

Another example: X is 0 with probability 0.2, 1 with probability 0.5, and 2 with probability 0.3. Then E[X] = 0 × 0.2 + 1 × 0.5 + 2 × 0.3 = 0 + 0.5 + 0.6 = 1.1.

Continuous variables

E[X] = ∫ x f(x) dx

Here f(x) is the density and the integral sign ∫ replaces the sum. Take X uniform on [0, 2], so f(x) = 1/2 on that interval. Then E[X] = ∫ from 0 to 2 of (x/2) dx = [x²/4] from 0 to 2 = 4/4 = 1.

The shortcut for variance

Var(X) = E[X²] − (E[X])²

For the die, E[X²] = (1 + 4 + 9 + 16 + 25 + 36)/6 = 91/6, and (E[X])² = (7/2)² = 49/4. So Var(X) = 91/6 − 49/4 = 182/12 − 147/12 = 35/12.

from fractions import Fraction

faces = range(1, 7)
EX = sum(Fraction(x, 6) for x in faces)
EX2 = sum(Fraction(x * x, 6) for x in faces)
print("E[X] for a die:", EX)
print("Var(X) for a die:", EX2 - EX ** 2)

# uniform on [0, 2] by a fine Riemann sum
steps = 200000
dx = 2 / steps
xs = [(i + 0.5) * dx for i in range(steps)]
mean_u = sum(x * (1 / 2) * dx for x in xs)
print("E[X] uniform [0,2]:", round(mean_u, 3))
E[X] for a die: 7/2
Var(X) for a die: 35/12
E[X] uniform [0,2]: 1.0

TrapE[X²] is not (E[X])². Square the values first, then average. Mixing these up is the most common error in variance questions.

A variable takes 1 with probability 0.4 and 3 with probability 0.6. What is E[X]?

E[X] = 1 × 0.4 + 3 × 0.6 = 0.4 + 1.8 = 2.2.

Part 4: Linearity and linear transforms

A linear transform is a new variable Y = aX + b, where a and b are fixed numbers. Two rules cover almost every GATE question on this.

E[aX + b] = aE[X] + b
Var(aX + b) = a² Var(X)
  • a scales the values. It multiplies the mean by a, but it multiplies the variance by a², because variance is built from squares.
  • b shifts the values. It moves the mean but leaves the variance unchanged, because shifting does not change the spread.

Worked example: X has E[X] = 3.5 and Var(X) = 35/12, the fair die. Let Y = 3X + 2. Then E[Y] = 3 × 3.5 + 2 = 12.5. And Var(Y) = 3² × 35/12 = 9 × 35/12 = 315/12 = 26.25. Note that the +2 has no effect on the variance.

Key ideaLinearity of expectation holds even when the variables are dependent. The variance rule for aX + b, however, is only the simple form shown here for a single variable.

TrapVar(aX + b) is a² Var(X), not a Var(X). The shift b disappears, and the scale appears squared. Taking the SD of Y gives |a| × SD(X).

If Var(X) = 4, what is Var(5 − 2X)?

Var(5 − 2X) = (−2)² × 4 = 4 × 4 = 16. The sign of a does not matter, because it is squared.

If E[X] = 10, what is E[4 − X]?

E[4 − X] = 4 − 10 = −6.

Solved GATE-style problems

These problems are original, written in GATE style, and are not past exam questions. Try each one before reading the answer.

Median of an even list

Find the median of the values 8, 2, 6, 4, 10, 12.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The median, which is the middle value after sorting.
  2. Which idea applies. Sort first. With an even count, the median is the average of the two middle values.
  3. Work it out. Sorted: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. There are 6 values, so the middle two are the 3rd and 4th: 6 and 8. Median = (6 + 8)/2 = 7.
  4. Check. Three values lie below 7 and three lie above it, so 7 is the middle.

Answer: 7.

Expectation of a simple variable

A random variable X takes the value 0 with probability 0.2, 1 with probability 0.5, and 2 with probability 0.3. Find E[X].

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The long-run average of X.
  2. Which idea applies. E[X] = Σ x × P(X = x).
  3. Work it out. 0 × 0.2 + 1 × 0.5 + 2 × 0.3 = 0 + 0.5 + 0.6 = 1.1.
  4. Check. The probabilities add to 1, and the answer lies between the smallest value 0 and the largest value 2.

Answer: 1.1.

Sample variance, to three decimals

The data are a sample: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. Find the sample variance (divide by n − 1), correct to 3 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The sample variance to 3 decimal places.
  2. Which idea applies. Sample variance = Σ (x − x̄)²/(n − 1).
  3. Work it out. The mean is 5. Squared deviations sum to 32. Divide by n − 1 = 7: 32/7 = 4.571428..., which is 4.571.
  4. Check. The sample variance must be a little larger than the population variance of 4, because we divide by a smaller number. 4.571 is larger than 4, as expected.

Answer: 4.571.

Which statements are correct?

A random variable X has E[X] = 4 and Var(X) = 9. Let Y = 2X − 3. Select all statements that are correct.

(A) E[Y] = 5.
(B) Var(Y) = 18.
(C) The standard deviation of Y is 6.
(D) E[Y] = 1.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Check each claim about E[Y] and Var(Y).
  2. Which idea applies. E[aX + b] = aE[X] + b, and Var(aX + b) = a² Var(X).
  3. Work it out. E[Y] = 2 × 4 − 3 = 5, so A is true and D is false. Var(Y) = 2² × 9 = 36, not 18, so B is false. SD(Y) = √36 = 6, so C is true.
  4. Check. The shift −3 does not change the variance. The scale 2 squares to 4, and 4 × 9 = 36.

Answer: (A) and (C).

A continuous expectation

A continuous random variable X has density f(x) = 2x for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1, and f(x) = 0 otherwise. Find E[X] as a fraction.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The mean of X, as a fraction.
  2. Which idea applies. E[X] = ∫ x f(x) dx over the support.
  3. Work it out. First check that the density is valid: ∫ from 0 to 1 of 2x dx = [x²] from 0 to 1 = 1. Good. Then E[X] = ∫ from 0 to 1 of x × 2x dx = ∫ from 0 to 1 of 2x² dx = [2x³/3] from 0 to 1 = 2/3.
  4. Check. The density puts more weight near 1, so the mean should be above 0.5. 2/3 is about 0.67, which fits.

Answer: 2/3.

Variance of a transformed continuous variable

Using the variable X from the previous problem, with density 2x on [0, 1], find Var(3X + 1) as a fraction.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The variance of a linear transform of X.
  2. Which idea applies. Var(aX + b) = a² Var(X), so first find Var(X).
  3. Work it out. E[X²] = ∫ from 0 to 1 of x² × 2x dx = ∫ from 0 to 1 of 2x³ dx = [x⁴/2] from 0 to 1 = 1/2. Var(X) = 1/2 − (2/3)² = 1/2 − 4/9 = 9/18 − 8/18 = 1/18. Then Var(3X + 1) = 3² × 1/18 = 9/18 = 1/2.
  4. Check. The +1 does not change the variance. The factor 9 is applied once, correctly.

Answer: 1/2.

Working backwards from a mean and variance

A random variable X has E[X] = 3 and Var(X) = 4. Find E[(X − 1)²].

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The expectation of a squared shifted variable.
  2. Which idea applies. Expand the square, then use linearity. Also use E[X²] = Var(X) + (E[X])².
  3. Work it out. E[X²] = 4 + 3² = 13. Expanding, (X − 1)² = X² − 2X + 1. So E[(X − 1)²] = E[X²] − 2E[X] + 1 = 13 − 6 + 1 = 8.
  4. Check. E[(X − 1)²] is always at least Var(X − 1) = Var(X) = 4, because the mean of the squared deviation from 1 is at least the mean's own spread. 8 is at least 4.

Answer: 8.

Exam-level practice

Exam-level 1: Variance of a linear combination (NAT, 2 marks)

X and Y have Var(X) = 4, Var(Y) = 9 and Cov(X, Y) = −2. Find Var(2X − 3Y + 5).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The variance of a linear combination.
  2. Which idea applies. Var(aX + bY + c) = a²Var X + b²Var Y + 2ab Cov(X, Y).
  3. Work it out. 4·4 + 9·9 + 2·2·(−3)·(−2) = 16 + 81 + 24 = 121.
  4. Check. The constant 5 does not change the spread.

Answer: 121.

Exam-level 2: Mean, median, mode after a change (MSQ, 2 marks)

Data: 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 10. The largest value 10 is changed to 40. Which statistics change? (A) Mean (B) Median (C) Mode (D) Standard deviation

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which summaries are sensitive to the extreme value.
  2. Which idea applies. The mean and SD use every value. The median and mode depend only on the middle and the most frequent values.
  3. Work it out. Mean goes from 5 to 10. Median stays (3 + 5)/2 = 4. Mode stays 3. SD grows.
  4. Check. The code prints both sets of statistics.
import statistics as st
for d in ([2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 10], [2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 40]):
    print(st.mean(d), st.median(d), st.mode(d), round(st.pstdev(d), 3))

5 4.0 3 2.769
10 4.0 3 13.515

Answer: (A) and (D).

Exam-level 3: Expectation of a discrete variable (NAT, 2 marks)

A fair die is rolled until a 6 appears. Let N be the number of rolls. Find E[N] and Var(N).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Mean and variance of a geometric variable.
  2. Which idea applies. N is geometric with p = 1/6: E[N] = 1/p and Var(N) = (1 − p)/p².
  3. Work it out. E[N] = 6. Var(N) = (5/6)/(1/36) = 30.
  4. Check. The code sums the series numerically.
p = 1 / 6
E = sum(k * (1 - p) ** (k - 1) * p for k in range(1, 2000))
E2 = sum(k * k * (1 - p) ** (k - 1) * p for k in range(1, 2000))
print("E = %.4f  Var = %.4f" % (E, E2 - E * E))

E = 6.0000  Var = 30.0000

Answer: E[N] = 6 and Var(N) = 30.

Summary

Mean
The sum of the values divided by how many there are.
Median
The middle value of the sorted data, averaging the two middle values when n is even.
Mode
The value that appears most often.
Population variance
The sum of squared deviations from the mean, divided by N.
Sample variance
The sum of squared deviations from the sample mean, divided by n − 1.
Standard deviation
The square root of the variance, in the original units.
Expectation, discrete
E[X] = Σ x P(X = x), a weighted average.
Expectation, continuous
E[X] = ∫ x f(x) dx.
Density
f(x) ≥ 0 with total area 1. Probabilities are areas under it.
Variance shortcut
Var(X) = E[X²] − (E[X])².
Linearity
E[aX + b] = aE[X] + b, for any random variable.
Variance of a linear transform
Var(aX + b) = a² Var(X). The shift b disappears.
Scale factor
The number a in aX + b, which multiplies the mean by a and the variance by a².

Remember these

  1. Sort the data before finding the median.
  2. Use n − 1 for a sample variance and N for a population variance.
  3. E[X²] is not (E[X])². Square first, then average.
  4. E[aX + b] = aE[X] + b, always.
  5. Var(aX + b) = a² Var(X). The shift b has no effect on the variance.
  6. For a continuous variable, expectation is an integral, and the density must integrate to 1.
