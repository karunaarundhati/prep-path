Functions of a single variable: limits & continuity

A function is a rule that turns an input into an output. A limit asks what the output gets close to as the input gets close to some value, and continuity asks whether the graph can be drawn without lifting your pen. These two ideas are the base of all calculus in GATE DA and RA.

The idea in plain words

Imagine walking towards a door, taking smaller and smaller steps. Even if you never touch the door, you can say exactly where your steps are heading. That is the big idea of a limit. A limit tells you where a function is heading, not necessarily what it equals at one exact point.

Now imagine drawing a graph with a pencil. If you can draw the whole graph without lifting the pencil, the function is continuous. If you must lift the pencil to jump to another height, or the graph shoots off to infinity, the function has a break. Continuity is simply "no breaks".

Why does this matter for GATE? Derivatives and integrals are both defined using limits. Optimisation uses derivatives, and machine learning uses optimisation. If a function has a break, many later tools stop working. In GATE Data Science and AI (DA) and Robotics and Automation (RA), you will meet questions such as "find the value of a limit", "find the constant that makes a function continuous", and "which statement about continuity or differentiability is true".

Key terms

Each term gets a plain meaning first, then a precise definition. Come back here whenever a word feels unfamiliar.

Function
Plain meaning: a machine that takes a number in and gives one number out. Precise definition: a rule f that assigns exactly one output f(x) to each input x in the domain.
Domain
Plain meaning: the inputs you are allowed to use. Precise definition: the set of all x for which f(x) is defined.
Range
Plain meaning: the outputs the function actually produces. Precise definition: the set of all values f(x) as x runs over the domain.
Limit
Plain meaning: the value the output heads towards as the input approaches a point. Precise definition: lim (x approaches a) of f(x) = L means f(x) can be made as close to L as we like by taking x close enough to a, with x not equal to a.
Left-hand and right-hand limits
Plain meaning: the values approached from smaller numbers and from bigger numbers. Precise definition: the limits of f(x) as x approaches a from below and from above.
Indeterminate form
Plain meaning: a substitution result that does not give an answer, such as 0/0. Precise definition: an expression whose limit cannot be decided from its form alone, such as 0/0, inf/inf, 0 times inf, inf minus inf, 1^inf, 0^0 or inf^0.
L'Hopital's rule
Plain meaning: for 0/0 and inf/inf, differentiate the top and the bottom separately. Precise definition: if the limit of f/g is 0/0 or inf/inf, it equals the limit of f'/g' whenever that limit exists.
Continuous at a point
Plain meaning: no break at that point. Precise definition: f(a) is defined, the limit as x approaches a exists, and the limit equals f(a).
Removable discontinuity
Plain meaning: a hole that one value can fill. Precise definition: the limit exists, but f(a) is missing or differs from the limit.
Jump discontinuity
Plain meaning: the graph steps from one height to another. Precise definition: both one-sided limits are finite but not equal.
Infinite discontinuity
Plain meaning: the graph shoots up or down near the point. Precise definition: at least one one-sided limit is plus or minus infinity.
Differentiable at a
Plain meaning: the graph has a clear slope at a. Precise definition: the limit of (f(a + h) minus f(a))/h as h goes to 0 exists.
Squeeze theorem
Plain meaning: a function trapped between two functions with the same limit must share that limit. Precise definition: if g at most f at most h near a, and g and h both tend to L, then f tends to L.

TipMost mistakes on this topic come from mixing up "the limit" and "the value at the point". They are different things, and the difference is the whole topic.

Domain and range: where the function lives

Before asking what happens near a point, find where the function is allowed to live. Think of the domain as the rooms in a building. You can only visit rooms that exist. The function 1/x has no room at x = 0, because division by zero is not allowed. Three common ways to lose a room are:

  • Division by zero. Example: 1/(x - 2) fails at x = 2.
  • Square root of a negative number. Example: sqrt(x - 3) needs x - 3 to be 0 or more, so x must be at least 3.
  • Logarithm of zero or less. For ln(x), x must be greater than 0.

Worked example. Find the domain of f(x) = sqrt(x - 3)/(x - 5).

  1. Step 1, the square root. x - 3 must be 0 or more, so x is at least 3.
  2. Step 2, the denominator. x - 5 must not be 0, so x is not 5.
  3. Step 3, combine. The domain is all x at least 3, except x = 5.

TrapA function can be defined near a point without being defined at that point. (x squared minus 4)/(x minus 2) has no value at x = 2, but it is defined for every x close to 2. Limits look at the neighbourhood, not at the point itself.

Quick check: what is the domain of 1/(x squared minus 9)?

The denominator is zero when x = 3 or x = -3. The domain is all real numbers except 3 and -3.

Quick check: is the range of f(x) = x squared all real numbers?

No. The range is [0, infinity), because squares are never negative.

What a limit means

Take f(x) = (x squared minus 4)/(x minus 2). Putting x = 2 gives 0/0, which has no meaning. So we look at numbers close to 2 and watch where f(x) goes.

x (close to 2)x squared minus 4x minus 2f(x)

1.99-0.0399-0.013.99

2.10.410.14.1

As x gets close to 2 from either side, f(x) gets close to 4. We write this as:

lim (x approaches 2) of (x squared minus 4)/(x minus 2) = 4

Here "lim" means limit, and "x approaches 2" means we use x values near 2 but never x = 2 itself. The number 4 is the value the function heads towards. Check the table with a short program.

def f(x):
    return (x * x - 4) / (x - 2)

for x in [1.9, 1.99, 1.999, 2.001, 2.01]:
    print(x, round(f(x), 4))
1.9 3.9
1.99 3.99
1.999 3.999
2.001 4.001
2.01 4.01

Key ideaThe limit depends only on values near a. The value f(a) can be anything, or may not exist at all, and the limit can still be a clear number.

One-sided limits. A point can be approached from the left (smaller numbers) or from the right (bigger numbers). The two-sided limit exists only when both one-sided limits exist and are equal.

GATE loves thisFor a piecewise function, always compute the left-hand and right-hand limits separately. If they differ, the two-sided limit does not exist.

Quick check: the left-hand limit at 3 is 5 and the right-hand limit is 7. Does the limit at 3 exist?

No. The two one-sided limits differ, so the two-sided limit does not exist.

Quick check: can a limit exist at a point where the function has no value?

Yes. The limit of (x squared minus 4)/(x minus 2) at x = 2 is 4, even though the function has no value at x = 2.

Evaluating limits step by step

Method 1: direct substitution. If the function is continuous at the point, just put the number in. Example: lim (x approaches 3) of (2x + 1). Substitute 3 to get 2 times 3 plus 1, which is 7.

Method 2: factor and cancel. If substitution gives 0/0, factor the top and bottom and cancel the common factor.

  1. Step 1. Substitute x = 3 into (x squared minus 9)/(x minus 3). We get 0/0.
  2. Step 2. Factor: x squared minus 9 = (x minus 3)(x plus 3). Check by expanding: x squared plus 3x minus 3x minus 9 = x squared minus 9.
  3. Step 3. Cancel (x minus 3). This is allowed, because x is not equal to 3 in a limit. The expression becomes x plus 3.
  4. Step 4. Substitute x = 3: 3 plus 3 = 6. The limit is 6.

Method 3: rationalise a square root. Find lim (x approaches 4) of (sqrt(x) minus 2)/(x minus 4).

  1. Step 1. Substitution gives 0/0.
  2. Step 2. Multiply top and bottom by (sqrt(x) plus 2). The top becomes x minus 4, because (a minus b)(a plus b) = a squared minus b squared.
  3. Step 3. Cancel (x minus 4) to get 1/(sqrt(x) plus 2).
  4. Step 4. Substitute x = 4: 1/(2 plus 2) = 1/4. The limit is 1/4.
import math

def g(x):
    return (math.sqrt(x) - 2) / (x - 4)

for x in [3.9, 3.99, 3.999, 4.001, 4.01]:
    print(x, round(g(x), 4))

print("1/(2+2) =", 1 / (2 + 2))
3.9 0.2516
3.99 0.2502
3.999 0.25
4.001 0.25
4.01 0.2498
1/(2+2) = 0.25

TipThe values on both sides heading to 0.25 confirm the answer. Always test both sides, because a one-sided pattern can mislead you.

Quick check: what is lim (x approaches 2) of (x cubed minus 8)/(x minus 2)?

Factor x cubed minus 8 as (x minus 2)(x squared plus 2x plus 4). Cancel (x minus 2) and substitute x = 2: 4 plus 4 plus 4 = 12.

Indeterminate forms and L'Hopital's rule

An indeterminate form such as 0/0 does not mean the answer is 0, and it does not mean infinity. It means more work is needed. Think of two runners who reach the finish line at the same moment. The finish does not tell you who was faster. You must look at their speeds just before the line. L'Hopital's rule looks at the "speeds" (derivatives) just before the finish.

The rule. If f(x)/g(x) has the form 0/0 or inf/inf as x approaches a, then:

lim (x approaches a) of f(x)/g(x) = lim (x approaches a) of f'(x)/g'(x)

Here f' and g' are the derivatives. Differentiate the top and the bottom separately, not the fraction as one piece.

TrapDo not use the quotient rule here. Students who do this get wrong answers. Differentiate top and bottom separately.

Worked example A: sin(x)/x. Find lim (x approaches 0) of sin(x)/x.

  1. Step 1. Substitution gives sin(0)/0 = 0/0.
  2. Step 2. Derivative of the top: cos(x). Derivative of the bottom: 1.
  3. Step 3. New fraction: cos(x)/1. Substitute x = 0: cos(0)/1 = 1.
  4. Answer. The limit is 1. This assumes x is in radians.

Worked example B: two rounds. Find lim (x approaches 0) of (1 minus cos(2x))/x squared.

  1. Step 1. Substitution gives (1 minus 1)/0 = 0/0.
  2. Step 2. Round one: derivative of the top is 2 sin(2x), derivative of the bottom is 2x. New fraction: 2 sin(2x)/(2x). Substituting x = 0 gives 0/0 again.
  3. Step 3. Round two: derivative of 2 sin(2x) is 4 cos(2x), derivative of 2x is 2. New fraction: 4 cos(2x)/2.
  4. Step 4. Substitute x = 0: 4 times 1 divided by 2 = 2. The limit is 2.

Key ideaApply the rule again only while the form is still 0/0 or inf/inf. Once you get an ordinary number, stop.

Quick check: is lim (x approaches 0) of x/x a 0/0 form?

It looks like 0/0, but x/x equals 1 for every x not equal to 0. So the limit is 1, and no rule is needed.

Quick check: what must be true before using L'Hopital's rule?

Direct substitution must give 0/0 or inf/inf. Differentiate the top and bottom separately, and repeat only if the form is still indeterminate.

Continuity at a point

A continuous road has no gaps, holes or sudden jumps. A function is continuous at a point a if its graph passes through a without a break. Mathematically there are three conditions, and all three must hold:

  1. f(a) exists. The function has a value at a.
  2. The limit exists. The left-hand and right-hand limits both exist and are equal.
  3. The limit equals the value. The limit of f(x) at a is exactly f(a).
f is continuous at a if f(a) is defined, lim (x approaches a) of f(x) exists, and that limit = f(a)

Example. Let f(x) = x squared when x is less than 2, and f(x) = 3x minus 2 when x is at least 2. Is f continuous at 2?

  1. Step 1, the value. x = 2 uses the second formula: 3 times 2 minus 2 = 4, so f(2) = 4.
  2. Step 2, the left limit. From below, x squared tends to 4.
  3. Step 3, the right limit. From above, 3x minus 2 tends to 4.
  4. Step 4, compare. Both one-sided limits are 4, and f(2) = 4. All three conditions hold, so f is continuous at 2.
def f(x):
    if x < 2:
        return x * x
    return 3 * x - 2

left = f(2 - 1e-9)
right = f(2 + 1e-9)
print("left limit near 2 is about", round(left, 6))
print("right limit near 2 is about", round(right, 6))
print("value f(2) is", f(2))
print("continuous at 2?", round(left, 6) == round(right, 6) == f(2))
left limit near 2 is about 4.0
right limit near 2 is about 4.0
value f(2) is 4
continuous at 2? True

TrapStudents often check only f(a). A function can have a value at a point and still be discontinuous there, because the limit is different. Always check all three conditions.

Quick check: f(0) = 1, and f(x) = x for every other x. Is f continuous at 0?

No. The limit at 0 is 0, but f(0) = 1, so the third condition fails.

Quick check: is every polynomial continuous everywhere?

Yes. Polynomials have no division and no square roots of negatives, so the limit always equals the value.

Types of discontinuity

When a function is not continuous, the type of break matters. GATE often asks you to classify one.

Removable. Like a small hole in paper. The limit exists, but f(a) is missing or wrong. Example: (x squared minus 4)/(x minus 2) with no value at 2. Setting f(2) = 4 removes the break.

Jump. Like a staircase. Both one-sided limits exist, but they are different. Example: the sign function, which has left limit minus 1 and right limit 1 at 0. No single value at 0 can fix it.

Infinite. Like a cliff. At least one one-sided limit is infinite. Example: f(x) = 1/(x minus 1) squared at x = 1. The values grow without limit from both sides.

def f(x):
    return 1 / (x - 1) ** 2

for x in [1.1, 1.01, 1.001, 0.999, 0.99]:
    print(x, round(f(x), 2))
1.1 100.0
1.01 10000.0
1.001 1000000.0
0.999 1000000.0
0.99 10000.0

Recipe. Find f(a), then the one-sided limits. If one is infinite, the break is infinite. If both are finite and different, it is a jump. If both are finite and equal but f(a) is missing or different, it is removable.

GATE loves thisOnly a removable break can be fixed by changing one value. A jump or an infinite break cannot.

Quick check: what type of break is sin(x)/x at x = 0?

Removable. The limit is 1, but the function is undefined at 0. Setting f(0) = 1 removes the break.

Differentiability implies continuity

A function is differentiable at a point if it has a well-defined slope there. Think of driving along the graph. A smooth road has one clear direction at each point. A sharp corner has no single steering direction. The slope is found from a limit:

f'(a) = lim (h approaches 0) of (f(a + h) minus f(a))/h

Here h is a tiny step away from a, f(a + h) minus f(a) is the rise, and dividing by h turns the rise into a slope.

The theorem. If f is differentiable at a, then f is continuous at a. Reason: multiply and divide by h to get f(a + h) minus f(a) = [(f(a + h) minus f(a))/h] times h. As h goes to 0, the first factor tends to the finite number f'(a) and the second tends to 0. So the product tends to 0, which means f(a + h) tends to f(a).

Key ideaDifferentiable implies continuous. Not every continuous function is differentiable.

The converse is false. The function f(x) = |x| is continuous everywhere, but it has a sharp corner at 0. The left slope is minus 1 and the right slope is 1.

def f(x):
    return abs(x)

h = 0.001
left_slope = (f(0) - f(0 - h)) / h
right_slope = (f(0 + h) - f(0)) / h
print("left slope at 0 is", round(left_slope, 6))
print("right slope at 0 is", round(right_slope, 6))
left slope at 0 is -1.0
right slope at 0 is 1.0

TrapA continuous function can still fail to be differentiable at a corner. But a function with a break can never be differentiable at that break.

Quick check: is f(x) = x squared differentiable at 0?

Yes. The quotient (h squared)/h = h tends to 0, so the slope at 0 is 0. The graph is smooth there.

Quick check: can a function be differentiable at a point where it is not continuous?

No. Differentiable at a implies continuous at a.

Limit of a rational function

Find the value of lim (x approaches 5) of (x squared minus 25)/(x minus 5).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A limit as x approaches 5.
  2. Which idea applies. Substitution gives 0/0, so factor and cancel.
  3. Work it out. x squared minus 25 = (x minus 5)(x plus 5). Cancel to get x plus 5, then substitute: 5 plus 5 = 10.
  4. Check. At x = 4.99 the expression is (24.9001 minus 25)/(minus 0.01) = 9.99, close to 10.

Answer: 10

Type of a break

Consider f(x) = 1/(x minus 4) at x = 4. Which type of discontinuity does f have? (A) removable, (B) jump, (C) infinite, (D) f is continuous at 4.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The type of break at x = 4.
  2. Which idea applies. Look at the one-sided limits.
  3. Work it out. Just above 4, x minus 4 is a tiny positive number, so 1/(x minus 4) is huge and positive. The right-hand limit is plus infinity, so the break is infinite. Option (D) is wrong because f is not defined at 4.
  4. Check. A vertical asymptote at x = 4 is the picture of an infinite break.

Answer: (C) infinite discontinuity

Piecewise function, select all that apply

Let f(x) = x + 1 for x less than 1, f(1) = 3, and f(x) = 2x for x greater than 1. Select all that apply: (A) the limit of f(x) as x approaches 1 exists; (B) f is continuous at x = 1; (C) f has a removable discontinuity at x = 1; (D) f has a jump discontinuity at x = 1.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements are true.
  2. Which idea applies. Compare the left limit, the right limit and f(1).
  3. Work it out. From the left, x + 1 tends to 2. From the right, 2x tends to 2. Both sides agree, so the limit exists and is 2: (A) is true. But f(1) = 3 is not 2, so (B) is false. The limit exists but the value is wrong, which is removable: (C) is true. A jump needs different one-sided limits, so (D) is false.
  4. Check. Setting f(1) = 2 would remove the break, matching the removable label.

Answer: (A) and (C)

Limit with a trig factor

Find lim (x approaches 0) of sin(5x)/(2x). Give your answer as a decimal number.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A limit, answered as a decimal.
  2. Which idea applies. Substitution gives 0/0, so L'Hopital's rule is allowed.
  3. Work it out. Derivative of the top is 5 cos(5x), derivative of the bottom is 2. New fraction 5 cos(5x)/2. At x = 0, cos(0) = 1, so the value is 5/2 = 2.5.
  4. Check. Write sin(5x)/(2x) = (5/2) times sin(5x)/(5x). The second part tends to 1, leaving 2.5.

Answer: 2.5

Finding a constant for continuity

Let f(x) = (x squared plus kx)/x for x not equal to 0, and f(0) = 3. Find the value of k that makes f continuous at 0.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The value of k.
  2. Which idea applies. Continuity at 0 needs the limit to equal f(0) = 3.
  3. Work it out. For x not equal to 0, cancel x: (x squared plus kx)/x = x plus k. As x approaches 0 this tends to k. So k must equal 3.
  4. Check. With k = 3 the limit at 0 is 3, which equals f(0).

Answer: k = 3

Differentiability and continuity

Which one of the following statements is TRUE for a real function f at a point a? (A) If f is continuous at a, then f is differentiable at a. (B) If f is differentiable at a, then f is continuous at a. (C) If f has a jump at a, then f is differentiable at a. (D) If f has a removable discontinuity at a, then f is differentiable at a.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The one true statement.
  2. Which idea applies. Differentiable implies continuous, not the converse.
  3. Work it out. (A) is the converse, and |x| at 0 is a counterexample. (B) is the theorem, so it is true. (C) and (D) describe breaks, and a function with a break cannot be differentiable there.
  4. Check. (B) matches the theorem proved in this topic.

Answer: (B)

A limit with an oscillating factor

Find lim (x approaches 0) of x squared times sin(1/x). Substitution and L'Hopital's rule are not useful here, because sin(1/x) keeps swinging. Use the squeeze theorem.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A limit where one factor keeps swinging.
  2. Which idea applies. The squeeze theorem: trap the function between simpler functions.
  3. Work it out. For every x not equal to 0, minus 1 is at most sin(1/x), which is at most 1. Multiply by x squared, which is never negative: minus x squared is at most x squared times sin(1/x), which is at most x squared. Both outer functions tend to 0, so the middle one does too.
  4. Check. At x = 0.01 the value is at most 0.0001 in size, as the squeeze predicts.

Answer: 0

Limit at infinity of a ratio

Find lim (x approaches infinity) of (3x squared plus 5x)/(2x squared minus 7). Give your answer as a decimal number.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The behaviour for very large x.
  2. Which idea applies. The form is inf/inf, so L'Hopital's rule can be used twice.
  3. Work it out. Round one: (6x plus 5)/(4x), still inf/inf. Round two: 6/4 = 1.5.
  4. Check. Dividing top and bottom by x squared gives (3 plus 5/x)/(2 minus 7/x squared), which tends to 3/2. The program below shows the same trend.
for x in [10, 100, 1000]:
    value = (3 * x * x + 5 * x) / (2 * x * x - 7)
    print("x =", x, "value =", round(value, 4))
x = 10 value = 1.8135
x = 100 value = 1.5255
x = 1000 value = 1.5025

Answer: 1.5

Exam-level 1: two limits that fix two constants (NAT, 2 marks)

A sensor model uses two functions. The first is f(x) = (x2 - 2x - 3)/(x - 3) for every x not equal to 3, and f(3) = k, where the constant k is chosen so that f is continuous at x = 3. The second is g(x) = (1 - cos 2x)/x2 for every x not equal to 0, and g(0) = m, where the constant m is chosen so that g is continuous at x = 0. Angles are in radians. Find the value of k + m, correct to 2 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The sum k + m, where each constant is the value that makes one function continuous at its special point.
  2. Which idea applies. A function is continuous at a point when its limit there exists and equals its value there. Both limits have the form 0/0, so factor the first numerator and use a standard trigonometric identity for the second.
  3. Work it out. The numerator of f factors as (x - 3)(x + 1), so f(x) = x + 1 for x not equal to 3. The limit as x approaches 3 is 4, so k = 4. For g, the identity 1 - cos 2x = 2 sin2 x gives g(x) = 2(sin x / x)2. Since sin x / x approaches 1, the limit is 2, so m = 2. Therefore k + m = 4 + 2 = 6.
  4. Check. The Python block evaluates both functions near their special points. The values approach 4 and 2, so the constants are right and the sum is 6.00.
import math
def f(x):
    return (x*x - 2*x - 3)/(x - 3)
def g(x):
    return (1 - math.cos(2*x))/(x*x)
for h in [0.1, 0.01, 0.001]:
    print("h =", h, "f(3+h) =", round(f(3+h), 4), "g(h) =", round(g(h), 4))
k = 4.0
m = 2.0
print("k + m =", round(k + m, 2))
h = 0.1 f(3+h) = 4.1 g(h) = 1.9933
h = 0.01 f(3+h) = 4.01 g(h) = 1.9999
h = 0.001 f(3+h) = 4.001 g(h) = 2.0
k + m = 6.0

Answer: k + m = 6.00

Exam-level 2: one-sided limits of a sign function (MSQ, 2 marks)

Let p(x) = |x - 2|/(x - 2) for every x not equal to 2, and let p(2) = 0. The function acts like a switch that reports which side of 2 an input lies on. Select all that apply. (A) The left-hand limit of p at x = 2 is -1. (B) The right-hand limit of p at x = 2 is 1. (C) The two-sided limit of p as x approaches 2 exists. (D) The function p is continuous at x = 2.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which of the four statements about p near x = 2 are true. Each one is about a one-sided limit, the two-sided limit, or continuity.
  2. Which idea applies. For x less than 2, the number x - 2 is negative, so |x - 2| = -(x - 2). For x greater than 2, |x - 2| = x - 2. Simplify on each side, then compare the two one-sided limits. The two-sided limit exists only if they agree, and continuity also needs p(2) to match.
  3. Work it out. For x less than 2, p(x) = -(x - 2)/(x - 2) = -1, so the left-hand limit is -1 and (A) is true. For x greater than 2, p(x) = 1, so the right-hand limit is 1 and (B) is true. The two one-sided limits differ, so the two-sided limit does not exist, which makes (C) false. A function cannot be continuous where its limit does not exist, so (D) is false. Also p(2) = 0 matches neither one-sided limit.
  4. Check. The Python block evaluates p just to the left and just to the right of 2. The output shows -1 on the left and 1 on the right, which matches the algebra.
import math
def p(x):
    return abs(x - 2) / (x - 2)
for h in [0.1, 0.001, 0.00001]:

Exam-level 3: classifying discontinuities of a rational function (MCQ, 2 marks)

A process yield is modelled by r(x) = (x3 - 8)/(x2 - 4). The formula has zero denominator at x = 2 and at x = -2, so r is not defined there by the formula alone. Which option correctly describes the behaviour of r at these two points? (A) r has a removable discontinuity at x = 2 whose limit is 3, and an infinite discontinuity at x = -2. (B) r has a jump discontinuity at x = 2, and a removable discontinuity at x = -2. (C) r has an infinite discontinuity at x = 2, and a removable discontinuity at x = -2. (D) r is continuous at both points.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The type of discontinuity at x = 2 and at x = -2.
  2. Which idea applies. Factor the numerator and the denominator. A factor that cancels gives a removable discontinuity, where the limit exists but the function value is missing. A factor that remains in the denominator, while the numerator stays nonzero, gives an infinite discontinuity.
  3. Work it out. The numerator is x3 - 8 = (x - 2)(x2 + 2x + 4), and the denominator is (x - 2)(x + 2). After cancelling x - 2, r(x) = (x2 + 2x + 4)/(x + 2) for x not equal to 2 or -2. At x = 2 the limit is (4 + 4 + 4)/(2 + 2) = 12/4 = 3, so the discontinuity is removable. At x = -2 the factor x + 2 stays in the denominator, and the numerator is 4 - 4 + 4 = 4, which is not zero. So r grows without bound near -2, an infinite discontinuity. Option (A) is correct.
  4. Check. The Python block samples r on both sides of each point. Near 2 the values approach 3. Near -2 the values are large, positive on one side and negative on the other.
def r(x):
    return (x**3 - 8) / (x**2 - 4)
for h in [0.01, 0.0001]:
    print("r(2 + h) =", round(r(2 + h), 4), " r(2 - h) =", round(r(2 - h), 4))
for h in [0.01, 0.0001]:
    print("r(-2 + h) =", round(r(-2 + h), 1), " r(-2 - h) =", round(r(-2 - h), 1))
r(2 + h) = 3.0075  r(2 - h) = 2.9925
r(2 + h) = 3.0001  r(2 - h) = 2.9999

r(-2 + h) = 39998.0  r(-2 - h) = -40002.0

Answer: (A)

Exam-level 4: a removable 0/0 form plus a limit at infinity (NAT, 2 marks)

A signal's value is the sum of two limits. The first is L1 = lim (x - sin x)/x3 as x approaches 0, with x in radians. The second is L2 = lim (3x2 + x)/(x2 - 5) as x approaches infinity. Find L1 + L2, correct to 2 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The sum of two limits, rounded to 2 decimal places at the end.
  2. Which idea applies. The first limit has the form 0/0 as x approaches 0, so use the Taylor series of sin x, or L'Hopital's rule. The second is a rational function as x grows large, so divide every term by the highest power of x in the denominator.
  3. Work it out. The Taylor series is sin x = x - x3/6 + x5/120 - ..., so x - sin x = x3/6 - x5/120 + .... Dividing by x3 gives 1/6 - x2/120 + ..., which tends to 1/6, so L1 = 1/6, about 0.1667. For the second limit, divide the top and bottom by x2 to get (3 + 1/x)/(1 - 5/x2). The fractions 1/x and 5/x2 go to 0, so L2 = 3. The sum is 1/6 + 3 = 3.1667, which is 3.17 to 2 decimal places.
  4. Check. The Python block evaluates the first expression at two small values of x, where it is close to 0.1667, and the second at a very large x, where it is close to 3.
import math
def a(x):
    return (x - math.sin(x)) / x**3
def b(x):
    return (3*x*x + x) / (x*x - 5)
print("(x - sin x)/x^3 at x = 0.01:", round(a(0.01), 4))
print("(x - sin x)/x^3 at x = 0.001:", round(a(0.001), 4))
print("(3x^2 + x)/(x^2 - 5) at x = 10^6:", round(b(10**6), 4))
total = 1/6 + 3
print("sum to 2 dp:", round(total, 2))

Summary

Domain
Plain meaning: the inputs allowed. Precise definition: the set of x where f(x) is defined.
Range
Plain meaning: the outputs produced. Precise definition: the set of all values f(x) for x in the domain.
Limit
Plain meaning: where the output heads. Precise definition: f(x) gets as close to L as wanted when x is close to a but not equal to a.
One-sided limit
Plain meaning: the value approached from one side. Precise definition: the limit taken only from below or only from above.
Indeterminate form
Plain meaning: a form that needs more work. Precise definition: 0/0, inf/inf, 0 times inf, inf minus inf, 1^inf, 0^0 or inf^0.
L'Hopital's rule
Plain meaning: differentiate top and bottom. Precise definition: for 0/0 or inf/inf, lim f/g = lim f'/g' when the right side exists.
Squeeze theorem
Plain meaning: trapped functions share the limit. Precise definition: if g at most f at most h near a, and g and h tend to L, then f tends to L.
Continuous at a
Plain meaning: no break at a. Precise definition: f(a) exists, the limit exists, and the limit equals f(a).
Removable discontinuity
Plain meaning: a hole. Precise definition: the limit exists but f(a) is missing or wrong.
Jump discontinuity
Plain meaning: a step. Precise definition: both one-sided limits are finite and different.
Infinite discontinuity
Plain meaning: a cliff. Precise definition: a one-sided limit is plus or minus infinity.
Differentiable at a
Plain meaning: a clear slope at a. Precise definition: the limit of (f(a + h) minus f(a))/h as h goes to 0 exists.

Remember these

  1. A limit looks at values near the point, not at the point itself.
  2. The limit exists only if the left and right limits are equal.
  3. Continuity needs three things: the value exists, the limit exists, and they are equal.
  4. L'Hopital's rule is only for 0/0 and inf/inf, and you differentiate top and bottom separately.
  5. Only a removable break can be fixed by changing one value.
  6. Differentiable at a implies continuous at a. The converse is false, because |x| is continuous but not differentiable at 0.
