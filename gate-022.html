Definite and improper integrals

An integral adds up tiny pieces. A definite integral gives the total amount between two points, such as an area, and an improper integral does the same when the range runs to infinity or the function blows up. Convergence tests decide whether the total is a finite number, which is a common GATE question.

The idea in plain words

Imagine you want the area under a curve, but the shape is curved, so you cannot use a simple rectangle. Cut the area into many thin vertical strips. Each strip is almost a rectangle, so its area is easy: height times width. Add all the strips together. If you make the strips thinner and thinner, the total gets closer and closer to the true area. That limit of sums is the integral.

Integrals are not only about area. If a robot's speed is known at every moment, the integral of speed gives the distance travelled. If a rate of energy use is known, the integral gives the total energy. Whenever you add up a changing quantity, you need an integral.

Why does this matter for GATE? Robotics and probability questions often need a definite integral. Some questions ask you to evaluate one. Others ask whether an improper integral converges, meaning whether its total is a finite number. Both skills are tested in GATE RA and DA.

Key terms

Each term gets a plain meaning first, then a precise definition.

Integral
Plain meaning: a total built from many small pieces. Precise definition: the limit of sums of products of function values and small widths.
Definite integral
Plain meaning: the total between two fixed points a and b. Precise definition: the number written as the integral from a to b of f(x) dx, when it exists.
Integrand
Plain meaning: the expression being integrated. Precise definition: the function f(x) inside the integral sign.
Antiderivative
Plain meaning: a function whose slope is the given function. Precise definition: a function F with F'(x) = f(x).
Improper integral
Plain meaning: an integral where a limit runs to infinity or the function blows up. Precise definition: an integral defined as a limit of definite integrals.
Convergent
Plain meaning: the total is a finite number. Precise definition: an improper integral whose limit exists and is a finite number.
Divergent
Plain meaning: the total is infinite, or has no limit. Precise definition: an improper integral whose limit is infinite or does not exist.
p-integral
Plain meaning: a standard integral of 1 over x to a power. Precise definition: the integral of 1/x to the power p over an infinite or near-zero range, whose behaviour depends on p.

TipThe symbol dx says which variable you are adding up over. Always write it, because the answer depends on it.

Area as a limit of sums

Let us find the area under f(x) = x squared from 0 to 1. Divide the interval into n equal strips, each of width 1/n. Use the height at the left end of each strip. The sum of the rectangle areas is called a Riemann sum.

Riemann sum = sum over i from 0 to n minus 1 of f(i/n) times (1/n)

Each symbol means:

  • n is the number of strips.
  • i/n is the position of the left end of the i-th strip.
  • f(i/n) is the height of the strip, and (1/n) is its width.
  • The sum adds all the rectangle areas.

As n grows, the sum approaches the true area. The notation for the limit is the integral:

integral from 0 to 1 of x squared dx = lim (n goes to infinity) of the Riemann sum

The next program uses 1000 strips and compares the result with the exact answer, which is 1/3.

n = 1000
left = sum(((i / n) ** 2) * (1 / n) for i in range(n))
print("left Riemann sum of x^2 on [0,1] with n = 1000:", f"{left:.4f}")
print("exact value 1/3 is about", f"{1/3:.4f}")
left Riemann sum of x^2 on [0,1] with n = 1000: 0.3328
exact value 1/3 is about 0.3333

Key ideaA definite integral is a limit of sums of small rectangles. You do not need to compute the sum directly, because the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus gives a short method.

Quick check: what does the integral of a positive function over [a, b] represent?

The area between the graph of the function, the x-axis, and the vertical lines x = a and x = b.

Quick check: what happens to the Riemann sum if you use more strips?

The approximation gets closer to the true area, because each rectangle becomes a better match for the curve.

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Derivatives and integrals are opposites. Differentiating an antiderivative gives back the original function. This is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC), and it gives the most useful method for definite integrals.

If F'(x) = f(x), then integral from a to b of f(x) dx = F(b) minus F(a)

The steps are:

  1. Find an antiderivative F, which means a function whose derivative is f.
  2. Evaluate F at the upper limit b.
  3. Evaluate F at the lower limit a.
  4. Subtract: F(b) minus F(a).

Worked example. Evaluate the integral from 1 to 3 of x squared dx.

  1. Step 1, antiderivative. The power rule for integration says that the antiderivative of x to the power n is x to the power (n + 1) divided by (n + 1), when n is not minus 1. For n = 2, F(x) = x cubed/3.
  2. Step 2, check. F'(x) = 3x squared/3 = x squared, which is correct.
  3. Step 3, evaluate. F(3) = 27/3 = 9. F(1) = 1/3.
  4. Step 4, subtract. 9 minus 1/3 = 26/3, which is about 8.67.

TrapDo not forget to subtract the value at the lower limit. Writing only F(b) is a very common mistake. Also remember the constant of integration is not needed for definite integrals, because it cancels.

Quick check: what is the integral from 0 to 2 of 3x squared dx?

The antiderivative is x cubed. Then 8 minus 0 = 8.

Quick check: why does the constant of integration cancel in a definite integral?

If F(x) + C is used instead of F(x), the value is (F(b) + C) minus (F(a) + C), and the C terms cancel.

Integration techniques: substitution and by parts

Not every integral is a simple power. Two methods cover most cases. Partial fractions, which splits a rational function into simpler pieces, is also useful, but we focus on the first two here.

Substitution. If the integrand contains a function and its derivative, replace the inner function with a new variable u. Then du is the derivative times dx.

Worked example. Evaluate the integral from 0 to 1 of 6x (x squared plus 1) squared dx.

  1. Step 1, choose u. Let u = x squared plus 1. Then du = 2x dx.
  2. Step 2, change the limits. When x = 0, u = 1. When x = 1, u = 2.
  3. Step 3, rewrite. 6x dx = 3 times 2x dx = 3 du. The integral becomes the integral from 1 to 2 of 3u squared du.
  4. Step 4, integrate. The antiderivative of 3u squared is u cubed. Evaluate: 8 minus 1 = 7.

Integration by parts. When the integrand is a product, such as x times e to the power x, use the rule:

integral of u dv = u v minus integral of v du

Each symbol means: u is a part of the integrand that becomes simpler when differentiated, and dv is the rest, which must be easy to integrate. Choose u so that the new integral is simpler than the old one. A helpful order is to pick algebraic functions (like x) as u before exponentials and trigonometric functions.

Worked example. Evaluate the integral from 0 to 1 of x e to the power x dx.

  1. Step 1, choose parts. Let u = x, so du = dx. Let dv = e to the power x dx, so v = e to the power x.
  2. Step 2, apply the rule. The integral is x e to the power x minus the integral of e to the power x dx.
  3. Step 3, integrate the rest. The antiderivative is x e to the power x minus e to the power x.
  4. Step 4, evaluate. At x = 1: e minus e = 0. At x = 0: 0 minus 1 = minus 1. Subtract: 0 minus (minus 1) = 1.
import math

def g(x):
    return x * math.exp(x)

n = 1000
h = 1 / n
trap = h * (g(0) / 2 + sum(g(i * h) for i in range(1, n)) + g(1) / 2)
print("trapezoid estimate of x e^x on [0,1]:", f"{trap:.4f}")
trapezoid estimate of x e^x on [0,1]: 1.0000

GATE loves thisWhen a product has a polynomial and an exponential, by parts usually works in one step. If the integral comes back in a harder form, try the other choice of u.

Quick check: in the substitution example, why does 6x dx become 3 du?

Because du = 2x dx, so 6x dx = 3 times (2x dx) = 3 du.

Quick check: for the integral of x times cos(x), which part should be u?

u = x, because differentiating x gives 1, which makes the new integral simpler. dv = cos(x) dx, so v = sin(x).

Improper integrals

A definite integral needs a finite range and a function that stays finite on that range. An improper integral breaks one of these rules. There are two types:

  • Type 1, infinite limit. The range runs to infinity, such as the integral from 1 to infinity.
  • Type 2, infinite discontinuity. The function blows up at an end of the range, such as 1/x at 0.

To handle them, replace the infinite end by a finite variable and take a limit. For example:

integral from 1 to infinity of f(x) dx = lim (t goes to infinity) of integral from 1 to t of f(x) dx

Worked example, type 1. Evaluate the integral from 1 to infinity of 1/x squared dx.

  1. Step 1, finite version. The integral from 1 to t of 1/x squared dx = [minus 1/x] from 1 to t = minus 1/t minus (minus 1) = 1 minus 1/t.
  2. Step 2, take the limit. As t goes to infinity, 1/t goes to 0.
  3. Step 3, answer. The limit is 1. So the integral converges, and its value is 1.
for t in [10, 100, 1000]:
    print("integral from 1 to", t, "of 1/x^2 =", f"{1 - 1 / t:.4f}")
integral from 1 to 10 of 1/x^2 = 0.9000
integral from 1 to 100 of 1/x^2 = 0.9900
integral from 1 to 1000 of 1/x^2 = 0.9990

The values approach 1, which matches the limit. So the improper integral converges to 1.

Worked example, type 2. Evaluate the integral from 0 to 1 of 1/square root of x dx.

  1. Step 1, the problem. The function 1/sqrt(x) = x to the power minus 1/2 blows up at 0, so the integral is improper at the lower limit.
  2. Step 2, finite version. Replace 0 by a small positive number t. The integral from t to 1 of x to the power minus 1/2 dx = [2 sqrt(x)] from t to 1 = 2 minus 2 sqrt(t).
  3. Step 3, take the limit. As t goes to 0 from above, sqrt(t) goes to 0, so the value goes to 2.
  4. Answer. The integral converges to 2.

Key ideaFor an improper integral, always write the finite version with a variable, compute it, then take the limit. Never substitute infinity directly into the antiderivative.

Quick check: what is the integral from 1 to infinity of 1/x dx?

The finite version is ln(t) minus ln(1) = ln(t), which goes to infinity as t goes to infinity. So the integral diverges.

Quick check: is the integral from 0 to 1 of 1/x dx improper?

Yes. The integrand blows up at 0, which is an infinite discontinuity at an end of the range.

The p-integral and the comparison test

Deciding convergence without computing the integral is a key skill. Two tools cover most GATE questions.

The p-integral. Integrals of 1 over x to a power p are the standard benchmarks:

integral from 1 to infinity of 1/x to the power p dx converges if and only if p greater than 1
integral from 0 to 1 of 1/x to the power p dx converges if and only if p less than 1

The first version looks at the tail far to the right, where the function must shrink fast enough. The second looks at the spike near 0, where the function must not grow too fast. Remember that p = 1 is the boundary case, and it always diverges.

For example, the integral from 1 to infinity of 1/square root of x has p = 1/2, which is not greater than 1. So it diverges. You can see it in the calculation: the finite version is 2 sqrt(t) minus 2, which grows without limit. The next program shows this.

import math

for T in [100, 10000]:
    print("integral from 1 to", T, "of 1/sqrt(x) =", f"{2 * math.sqrt(T) - 2:.1f}")
integral from 1 to 100 of 1/sqrt(x) = 18.0
integral from 1 to 10000 of 1/sqrt(x) = 198.0

The comparison test. Often you cannot find the antiderivative, but you can compare your function with a simpler one. Suppose 0 is at most f(x), and f(x) is at most g(x) for all large x. Then:

  • If the integral of g converges, the integral of f converges.
  • If the integral of f diverges, the integral of g diverges.

Worked example. Does the integral from 1 to infinity of (sin squared x)/x squared dx converge?

  1. Step 1, bound the integrand. Since sin squared x is between 0 and 1, we have 0 at most (sin squared x)/x squared at most 1/x squared.
  2. Step 2, benchmark. The integral from 1 to infinity of 1/x squared converges, because p = 2 is greater than 1.
  3. Step 3, conclude. By comparison, the original integral converges too.

TrapThe comparison test needs the bigger function to converge in order to conclude convergence. A smaller function that converges tells you nothing about a bigger one, and a bigger function that diverges tells you nothing about a smaller one.

Quick check: does the integral from 1 to infinity of 1/x cubed converge?

Yes. Here p = 3 is greater than 1, so it converges.

Quick check: does the integral from 0 to 1 of 1/x squared dx converge?

No. Here p = 2 is not less than 1, so the spike at 0 is too strong and the integral diverges.

Evaluating improper integrals with care

Some improper integrals have a finite value, and you need to find it. The method is always the same: write the finite version with a variable, integrate, then take the limit.

Worked example. Evaluate the integral from 0 to 1 of ln(x) dx.

  1. Step 1, antiderivative. Use integration by parts with u = ln(x) and dv = dx. Then du = dx/x and v = x. The antiderivative is x ln(x) minus x.
  2. Step 2, finite version. The integral from t to 1 of ln(x) dx = (1 ln 1 minus 1) minus (t ln t minus t) = minus 1 minus t ln(t) plus t.
  3. Step 3, take the limit. As t goes to 0 from above, t ln(t) goes to 0, and t goes to 0. So the value goes to minus 1.
  4. Answer. The integral converges to minus 1.
import math

for t in [0.1, 0.01, 0.001]:
    val = -1 - (t * math.log(t) - t)
    print("integral from", t, "to 1 of ln x =", f"{val:.4f}")
integral from 0.1 to 1 of ln x = -0.6697
integral from 0.01 to 1 of ln x = -0.9439
integral from 0.001 to 1 of ln x = -0.9921

The numbers approach minus 1, which confirms the limit.

Worked example with an infinite range. Evaluate the integral from 2 to infinity of 1/(x (ln x) squared) dx.

  1. Step 1, substitution. Let u = ln(x). Then du = dx/x, and the limits become ln(2) and infinity.
  2. Step 2, rewrite. The integral becomes the integral from ln(2) to infinity of du/u squared.
  3. Step 3, integrate. The antiderivative is minus 1/u. Evaluate: the limit as t goes to infinity of (minus 1/t) minus (minus 1/ln(2)) = 1/ln(2).
  4. Step 4, numerical value. 1/ln(2) is about 1.4427. So the integral converges.

GATE loves thisA convergent improper integral has a finite answer that you can compute, but a divergent one has no answer. Always check convergence first, because you cannot evaluate a divergent integral to a number.

Quick check: what is the value of the integral from 0 to 1 of x to the power 1/2 dx?

The antiderivative is (2/3) x to the power 3/2. Evaluate from 0 to 1: 2/3 minus 0 = 2/3. The integrand is bounded, so this is a proper integral in the end.

Quick check: why does the substitution u = ln(x) turn the second example into a p-integral?

The integrand becomes 1/u squared, which is 1/u to the power 2 with p = 2. The range from ln(2) to infinity has the right tail, and p = 2 is greater than 1, so the integral converges.

A simple definite integral

Evaluate the integral from 0 to 2 of 3x squared dx. Give your answer as an integer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A definite integral of a polynomial.
  2. Which idea applies. The power rule and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
  3. Work it out. An antiderivative of 3x squared is x cubed. Evaluate: 2 cubed minus 0 cubed = 8 minus 0 = 8.
  4. Check. Differentiate x cubed to get 3x squared, which confirms the antiderivative.

Answer: 8

Integral of 1 over x

Evaluate the integral from 1 to e of 1/x dx. Give your answer as an integer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A definite integral with the natural logarithm as antiderivative.
  2. Which idea applies. The derivative of ln(x) is 1/x.
  3. Work it out. The antiderivative is ln(x). Evaluate: ln(e) minus ln(1) = 1 minus 0 = 1.
  4. Check. The area under 1/x from 1 to e is a little less than the area of a rectangle of height 1 and width e minus 1, which is about 1.7, so 1 is reasonable.

Answer: 1

Select all that apply. Which of the following improper integrals converge? (A) the integral from 1 to infinity of 1/x cubed dx; (B) the integral from 1 to infinity of 1/square root of x dx; (C) the integral from 0 to 1 of 1/square root of x dx; (D) the integral from 0 to 1 of 1/x squared dx.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which integrals have a finite value.
  2. Which idea applies. The p-integral rules.
  3. Work it out. (A) has p = 3 at infinity, which is greater than 1, so it converges. (B) has p = 1/2 at infinity, which is not greater than 1, so it diverges. (C) has p = 1/2 at 0, which is less than 1, so it converges. (D) has p = 2 at 0, which is not less than 1, so it diverges.
  4. Check. The tail at infinity needs p greater than 1, and the spike at 0 needs p less than 1.

Answer: (A) and (C)

Evaluate the integral from 0 to pi/2 of cos(x) dx. Give your answer as an integer.

  1. What is asked. A definite integral of cosine.
  2. Which idea applies. The antiderivative of cos(x) is sin(x).
  3. Work it out. Evaluate: sin(pi/2) minus sin(0) = 1 minus 0 = 1.

Integration by parts

Evaluate the integral from 1 to 2 of ln(x) dx. Give your answer as a decimal correct to four places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A definite integral of a logarithm.
  2. Which idea applies. Integration by parts with u = ln(x) and dv = dx.
  3. Work it out. Then du = dx/x and v = x. The antiderivative is x ln(x) minus x. Evaluate at 2: 2 ln(2) minus 2. Evaluate at 1: 0 minus 1 = minus 1. Subtract: (2 ln(2) minus 2) minus (minus 1) = 2 ln(2) minus 1, which is about 0.3863.
  4. Check. ln(x) is between 0 and ln(2), which is about 0.69, on [1, 2]. The area is therefore between 0 and 0.69, and 0.3863 fits.

Answer: 0.3863

Substitution with a power

Evaluate the integral from 0 to 1 of 6x (x squared plus 1) squared dx. Give your answer as an integer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A definite integral of a product of a function and its derivative.
  2. Which idea applies. Substitution with u = x squared plus 1.
  3. Work it out. du = 2x dx, so 6x dx = 3 du. The limits become u = 1 to u = 2. The integral is the integral from 1 to 2 of 3u squared du = [u cubed] from 1 to 2 = 8 minus 1 = 7.
  4. Check. The integrand is positive on [0, 1], so the answer must be positive, and 7 is positive.

Improper integral with a value

Evaluate the improper integral from 2 to infinity of 1/(x (ln x) squared) dx. Give your answer as a decimal correct to four places.

  2. Which idea applies. Substitution u = ln(x), followed by the limit definition.
  3. Work it out. With u = ln(x), du = dx/x. The limits become ln(2) and infinity. The integral becomes the integral from ln(2) to infinity of 1/u squared du. The antiderivative is minus 1/u, so the value is 0 minus (minus 1/ln(2)) = 1/ln(2), which is about 1.4427.

Convergence with a comparison

Does the integral from 1 to infinity of (1 + sin(x))/(x squared) dx converge? Answer with a single word: yes or no.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A yes or no answer on convergence.
  2. Which idea applies. The comparison test with a p-integral.
  3. Work it out. Since sin(x) is between minus 1 and 1, the numerator 1 + sin(x) is between 0 and 2. So 0 at most (1 + sin(x))/x squared at most 2/x squared. The integral of 2/x squared from 1 to infinity converges, because p = 2 is greater than 1. By comparison, the original integral converges.
  4. Check. The integrand is never negative, which the comparison test needs, and the bound is valid for every x at least 1.

Answer: yes

Exam-level 1: two integrals, one substitution and one identity (NAT, 2 marks)

A signal's energy is found in two stages, and the total is the sum of two definite integrals. The first is I1 = integral from 0 to 1 of x ex2 dx. The second is I2 = integral from 0 to pi/2 of sin2 x dx, with x in radians. Find I1 + I2, correct to 2 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The sum of two definite integrals, rounded to 2 decimal places at the end.
  2. Which idea applies. For I1, use the substitution u = x2, so that du = 2x dx. For I2, use the identity sin2 x = (1 - cos 2x)/2 to turn it into a simple integral.
  3. Work it out. For I1, the limits change to 0 and 1, and the integral becomes (1/2) times the integral from 0 to 1 of eu du = (e - 1)/2, about 0.8591. For I2, the integral of (1 - cos 2x)/2 is x/2 - (sin 2x)/4. Evaluating from 0 to pi/2 gives pi/4 - 0 = pi/4, about 0.7854. The sum is 0.8591 + 0.7854 = 1.6445, which is 1.64 to 2 decimal places.
  4. Check. The Python block applies Simpson's rule to each integral and compares the results with the exact values. The numerical and exact values agree to 4 decimal places, and the sum is 1.64.
import math
def simpson(f, a, b, n=20000):
    h = (b - a) / n
    s = f(a) + f(b)
    for i in range(1, n):
        s += (4 if i % 2 else 2) * f(a + i*h)
    return s * h / 3
I1 = simpson(lambda x: x*math.exp(x*x), 0, 1)
I2 = simpson(lambda x: math.sin(x)**2, 0, math.pi/2)
print("I1 =", round(I1, 4), " exact (e-1)/2 =", round((math.e - 1)/2, 4))
print("I2 =", round(I2, 4), " exact pi/4 =", round(math.pi/4, 4))
print("I1 + I2 to 2 dp =", round(I1 + I2, 2))
I1 = 0.8591  exact (e-1)/2 = 0.8591
I2 = 0.7854  exact pi/4 = 0.7854
I1 + I2 to 2 dp = 1.64

Answer: 1.64

Exam-level 2: convergence of four improper integrals (MSQ, 2 marks)

An improper integral, one with an infinite limit or an infinite integrand, converges when the limit of its proper versions exists and is finite. Select all that apply. (A) The integral from 1 to infinity of x-2 dx converges. (B) The integral from 1 to infinity of x-1/2 dx converges. (C) The integral from 0 to 1 of x-1/2 dx converges. (D) The integral from 0 to 1 of x-1 dx converges.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which of the four improper integrals converge.
  2. Which idea applies. Use the p-test. On [1, infinity), the integral of x-p converges when p is greater than 1. On (0, 1], the integral of x-p converges when p is less than 1. Check each one by computing the proper integral and taking its limit.
  3. Work it out. (A) Here p = 2 is greater than 1 on [1, infinity), so the integral converges, with value 1. This is true. (B) Here p = 1/2 is not greater than 1, so the integral diverges. The proper integral 2(sqrt(R) - 1) grows without limit, so (B) is false. (C) Here p = 1/2 is less than 1 on (0, 1], so the integral converges, with value 2. This is true. (D) Here p = 1 is not less than 1, so the integral diverges. The proper integral from e to 1 is -ln e, which grows without limit as e approaches 0, so (D) is false.
  4. Check. The Python block prints the proper integrals for several cutoffs. The values for (A) and (C) settle near 1 and 2, whereas the values for (B) and (D) keep growing.
import math
for R in [10, 1000]:
    print("int from 1 to %d of x^-2 dx = %.4f" % (R, 1 - 1/R))
for R in [100, 10000]:
    print("int from 1 to %d of x^-1/2 dx = %.4f" % (R, 2*(math.sqrt(R) - 1)))
for e in [0.01, 0.000001]:
    print("int from %g to 1 of x^-1/2 dx = %.4f" % (e, 2 - 2*math.sqrt(e)))
for e in [0.01, 0.000001]:
    print("int from %g to 1 of x^-1 dx = %.4f" % (e, -math.log(e)))
int from 1 to 10 of x^-2 dx = 0.9000
int from 1 to 1000 of x^-2 dx = 0.9990
int from 1 to 100 of x^-1/2 dx = 18.0000
int from 1 to 10000 of x^-1/2 dx = 198.0000
int from 0.01 to 1 of x^-1/2 dx = 1.8000
int from 1e-06 to 1 of x^-1/2 dx = 1.9980
int from 0.01 to 1 of x^-1 dx = 4.6052
int from 1e-06 to 1 of x^-1 dx = 13.8155

Answer: (A) and (C) converge. (B) and (D) diverge.

Exam-level 3: a weighted total that needs two integrations by parts (MCQ, 2 marks)

A queue's waiting time X, in minutes, has an unnormalised weight given by x2 e-2x for x at least 0. The total weight is the improper integral I = integral from 0 to infinity of x2 e-2x dx. What is I? (A) 1/2. (B) 1/4. (C) 1. (D) The integral diverges.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The value of I, or whether it diverges.
  2. Which idea applies. Write I as the limit of integrals from 0 to R as R goes to infinity. Then use integration by parts. Exponential decay beats any polynomial, so the integral is finite.
  3. Work it out. Let J = integral from 0 to infinity of x e-2x dx. Integrate by parts with u = x2 and dv = e-2x dx, so v = -e-2x/2. The boundary term -x2e-2x/2 is zero at 0 and at infinity, which leaves I = J. For J, use u = x and dv = e-2x dx, so v = -e-2x/2. Again the boundary term vanishes, leaving J = (1/2) times the integral from 0 to infinity of e-2x dx = (1/2)(1/2) = 1/4. So I = 1/4, and option (B) is correct.
  4. Check. The Python block approximates the integral with the midpoint rule on [0, 30], since the integrand is negligible beyond that point. It gets 0.25, which matches the standard result 2!/23 = 0.25.

Exam-level 4: normalising a density and finding its mean (NAT, 2 marks)

A waiting time X, in seconds, has the probability density f(x) = k/x3 for x at least 1, and f(x) = 0 otherwise. The constant k is chosen so that the total probability is 1. Find E[X] + P(X greater than 2), where E[X] is the mean of X and P(X greater than 2) is the probability that the wait is more than 2 seconds. Give your answer correct to 2 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The constant k, the mean E[X], and the tail probability P(X greater than 2), then their sum to 2 decimal places.
  2. Which idea applies. A density integrates to 1 over the values it covers. The mean is the integral of x f(x), and a probability is the integral of the density over the event. All three are improper integrals to infinity.
  3. Work it out. The total probability is k times the integral from 1 to infinity of x-3 dx. This integral equals 1/2, so k/2 = 1 and k = 2. The mean is the integral from 1 to infinity of x times 2x-3 dx = 2 times the integral of x-2 dx = 2 times 1 = 2. The tail is P(X greater than 2) = 2 times the integral from 2 to infinity of x-3 dx = 2 times 1/(2 times 22) = 2 times 1/8 = 1/4. The sum is 2 + 0.25 = 2.25.
  4. Check. The Python block finds k, the mean and the tail probability from exact formulas. It also checks that the density integrates to 1 over [1, 104], which gives 1.0 to 6 decimal places. The sum is 2.25.

Summary

Definite integral
Plain meaning: the total between a and b. Precise definition: the limit of Riemann sums, equal to F(b) minus F(a) when F' = f.
Riemann sum
Plain meaning: a sum of thin rectangles. Precise definition: a sum of f times widths over partitions of [a, b].
Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
Plain meaning: differentiate and integrate undo each other. Precise definition: the integral from a to b of f equals F(b) minus F(a) for any antiderivative F.
Substitution
Plain meaning: replace a messy inner function by u. Precise definition: a change of variable that also changes the limits.
Integration by parts
Plain meaning: undo a product rule. Precise definition: the integral of u dv equals u v minus the integral of v du.
Improper integral
Plain meaning: an integral with an infinite limit or a blow-up. Precise definition: a limit of finite integrals.
Convergent
Plain meaning: a finite total. Precise definition: the limit defining the improper integral exists and is finite.
Divergent
Plain meaning: an infinite or undefined total. Precise definition: the limit is infinite or does not exist.
p-integral
Plain meaning: a benchmark integral of 1 over x to a power. Precise definition: converges at infinity if p is greater than 1, and at 0 if p is less than 1.
Comparison test
Plain meaning: a bigger convergent function forces convergence. Precise definition: if 0 is at most f, which is at most g, and g converges, then f converges.

Remember these

  1. A definite integral is a limit of sums, and the Fundamental Theorem gives the value quickly.
  2. Always subtract the value at the lower limit.
  3. For an improper integral, write the finite version and take the limit.
  4. For a tail at infinity, 1/x to the power p converges if p is greater than 1.
  5. For a spike at 0, 1/x to the power p converges if p is less than 1.
  6. The comparison test needs a bigger convergent function to conclude convergence.
