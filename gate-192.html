The idea in plain words

Welcome to a review. You have met several new ideas in Machine Design and CIM. This page adds little new material. Its aim is to make the main ideas stick, so that a question on any of them takes a few clear steps rather than a long search through your notes.

Think of a review as a rehearsal. Run through each step once more, slowly, and notice where you hesitate. A hesitation shows you exactly where to spend extra minutes. For example, if you pause over which theory to pick, or whether to subtract or add two stresses, that is the place to practise.

Why does this matter for GATE? Machine Design and CIM questions are often short. One line may ask for a factor of safety by a named theory, or for the difference between two manufacturing processes. The marks come from knowing the rule and writing the steps cleanly.

Key terms

These terms appear on this page. Each one is defined in full on the earlier topic pages.

Principal stress
A normal stress on a plane with zero shear. Precisely: sigma1 and sigma2 in plane stress, with sigma1 the larger.
Tresca theory
A ductile failure rule based on the largest shear. Precisely: n = Sy/(sigma1 - sigma3).
von Mises theory
A ductile failure rule based on an equivalent stress. Precisely: n = Sy/sigma', the distortion energy rule.
Rankine theory
A brittle failure rule based on the largest principal stress. Precisely: n = Sut/sigma1 for tension.
Modified Goodman line
A fatigue rule that includes a mean stress. Precisely: 1/n = sigmaa/Se + sigmam/Sut.
Additive manufacturing (AM)
Building a part layer by layer from a 3D model.
Subtractive manufacturing
Making a part by removing material from a solid block, as in milling.

Failure theories in one table

Every static failure question asks you to choose a theory, find the principal stresses, and compute a factor of safety. The table puts the rules side by side.

TheoryMaterialFactor of safety n
Maximum principal stress (Rankine)Brittle, in tensionSut/sigma1
Maximum shear stress (Tresca)Ductile, most conservativeSy/(sigma1 - sigma3)
Maximum distortion energy (von Mises)Ductile, most commonSy/sigma'
Maximum strain (Saint-Venant)General rule, uses Poisson's ratioSy/(sigma1 - nu sigma2) in plane stress

Two facts are worth memorising. For ductile metals, the Tresca hexagon lies inside the von Mises ellipse, so Tresca never gives a larger factor than von Mises. For brittle metals, check tension and compression separately.

Key ideaFirst find sigma1 and sigma2 from the normal and shear stresses. Only then choose and apply the theory.

Which theory is the most conservative for a ductile material in plane stress?

Tresca. Its safe region lies inside the von Mises ellipse, so it gives the smaller factor of safety.

Worked check: one stress state, three theories

A ductile steel part has sigmax = 90 MPa, sigmay = 30 MPa and tauxy = 40 MPa. The yield strength is Sy = 200 MPa.

  1. Average. (90 + 30)/2 = 60 MPa.
  2. Radius. square root of (30 squared + 40 squared) = square root of 2500 = 50 MPa.
  3. Principal stresses. sigma1 = 60 + 50 = 110 MPa, sigma2 = 60 - 50 = 10 MPa, and sigma3 = 0.
  4. Tresca. n = 200/(110 - 0) = 1.82.
  5. von Mises. sigma' = square root of (1102 - 110 times 10 + 102) = square root of 11100 = 105.36 MPa, so n = 200/105.36 = 1.90.
  6. Choice. Tresca gives the smaller factor, so it is the conservative answer.

The Python check below reproduces these numbers.

import math
sx, sy, txy = 90, 30, 40
c = (sx + sy) / 2
R = math.sqrt(((sx - sy) / 2) ** 2 + txy ** 2)
s1, s2 = c + R, c - R
print("Principal stresses (MPa):", s1, s2)
print("Tresca n (Sy = 200):", round(200 / (s1 - 0), 2))
print("von Mises n (Sy = 200):", round(200 / math.sqrt(s1 ** 2 - s1 * s2 + s2 ** 2), 2))
Principal stresses (MPa): 110.0 10.0
Tresca n (Sy = 200): 1.82
von Mises n (Sy = 200): 1.9

TrapWhen sigma2 is negative, the term minus sigma1 sigma2 becomes an addition. Always keep the sign.

Brittle materials and two strength checks

A brittle material such as cast iron is much stronger in compression than in tension. So the Rankine check is done twice: against Sut for a positive principal stress, and against Suc for a negative one. The governing factor is the smaller of the two.

Worked example. A cast iron part has Sut = 200 MPa and Suc = 600 MPa. Its principal stresses are sigma1 = 60 MPa (tension) and sigma2 = -150 MPa (compression).

  1. Tension check: 200/60 = 3.33.
  2. Compression check: 600/150 = 4.00.
  3. Governing value: the smaller, 3.33.

GATE loves thisThe compression check is often missed. Write both checks in every answer, then take the minimum.

A quick look at fatigue with the modified Goodman line

Static theories ask whether a steady load causes yield or fracture. Dynamic questions ask about a load that repeats. The modified Goodman line combines the alternating stress sigmaa and the mean stress sigmam into one factor of safety.

1/n = sigmaa/Se + sigmam/Sut

Here Se is the corrected endurance limit and Sut is the ultimate tensile strength, both in MPa.

Worked example. sigmaa = 100 MPa, sigmam = 50 MPa, Se = 200 MPa, Sut = 500 MPa. Then 1/n = 100/200 + 50/500 = 0.5 + 0.1 = 0.6, so n = 1/0.6 = 1.67.

TrapThe Goodman formula gives 1/n, not n. Forgetting the reciprocal is the most common error on dynamic questions.

A solid model defines volume and mass. A parametric model updates when a dimension changes. STEP carries a solid between systems, and STL carries only the triangles a 3D printer needs.

What does a post-processor do?

It converts the generic toolpath into the NC code that one particular machine controller understands.

Additive manufacturing next to subtractive manufacturing

Subtractive manufacturing starts from a solid block and removes material. Additive manufacturing adds material layer by layer, following the 3D model. Subtractive suits accurate metal parts in volume. Additive suits complex shapes, internal channels and quick prototypes.

Build time grows with the number of layers. A part 40 mm tall with 0.1 mm layers has 40/0.1 = 400 layers. If each layer takes 90 s, the build takes 400 times 90 = 36000 s, which is 10 hours. Thinner layers give a smoother surface but a longer build.

Key ideaAdditive is layer by layer from a 3D model. Subtractive removes material from a block.

Mixed GATE style practice

These problems are original, written in the GATE style, and not copied from any real exam paper. Solve each one before reading the answer.

Brittle tension check

A brittle part has tensile strength Sut = 180 MPa and largest principal stress sigma1 = 60 MPa. Using the maximum principal stress theory, what is the factor of safety? (NAT: enter a number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The Rankine factor for tension.
  2. Which idea applies. n = Sut/sigma1.
  3. Work it out. n = 180/60 = 3.0.
  4. Check. The stress is a third of the strength, so n = 3 is consistent.

Answer: 3.0.

Statements about additive manufacturing

Which of the following statements about additive manufacturing are correct? Select all that apply. (MSQ)

  • (A) Additive manufacturing builds a part layer by layer from a 3D model.
  • (B) Thinner layers usually give a smoother surface but a longer build.
  • (C) Subtractive manufacturing adds material to a blank.
  • (D) Stereolithography cures a liquid photopolymer with a light source, layer by layer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements are true.
  2. Which idea applies. The definition of additive manufacturing, the layer trade-off, and the meaning of subtractive.
  3. Work it out. (A) True. (B) True. (C) False: subtractive removes material. (D) True: stereolithography cures resin with a UV source.
  4. Check. Statement (C) reverses the meaning of subtractive, so it is false.

Answer: (A), (B) and (D) are correct.

Tresca and von Mises for one state

A ductile part has sigmax = 60 MPa, sigmay = 0 and tauxy = 40 MPa, in plane stress, with Sy = 250 MPa. Find the Tresca factor of safety. (NAT: enter a number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The Tresca factor n.
  2. Which idea applies. Find the principal stresses, then n = Sy/(sigma1 - sigma3).
  3. Work it out. Average 30, radius sqrt(302 + 402) = 50. So sigma1 = 80 and sigma2 = -20. With sigma3 = -20, the difference is 100. Then n = 250/100 = 2.5.
  4. Check. The largest stress, 80 MPa, is below 250 MPa, so n above 1 is expected.

Answer: 2.5.

von Mises for the same state

Use the same state as the previous problem (sigmax = 60 MPa, sigmay = 0, tauxy = 40 MPa, Sy = 250 MPa). Find the von Mises factor of safety to two decimal places. (NAT: enter a number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The von Mises factor n.
  2. Which idea applies. sigma' = square root of (sigma12 - sigma1 sigma2 + sigma22), then n = Sy/sigma'.
  3. Work it out. sigma1 = 80 and sigma2 = -20. Then 6400 + 1600 + 400 = 8400, and sigma' = 91.65 MPa. So n = 250/91.65 = 2.73.
  4. Check. The von Mises factor 2.73 is larger than the Tresca factor 2.5, as expected.

Answer: 2.73.

Goodman factor for a shaft

A steel shaft has an alternating stress of 100 MPa and a mean stress of 50 MPa. Its corrected endurance limit is 200 MPa and its ultimate tensile strength is 500 MPa. Using the modified Goodman line, find the factor of safety to two decimal places. (NAT: enter a number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The Goodman factor n.
  2. Which idea applies. 1/n = sigmaa/Se + sigmam/Sut.
  3. Work it out. 100/200 + 50/500 = 0.6. So n = 1/0.6 = 1.67.
  4. Check. The factor is above 1, so the shaft is safe on this line.

Answer: 1.67.

Brittle part with two stress checks

A brittle casting has Sut = 200 MPa and Suc = 600 MPa. At a critical point sigma1 = 60 MPa and sigma2 = -150 MPa. Using the maximum principal stress theory with both checks, what is the lowest factor of safety, to two decimal places? (NAT: enter a number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The governing factor of safety.
  2. Which idea applies. Check tension with Sut and compression with Suc, then take the smaller.
  3. Work it out. Tension: 200/60 = 3.33. Compression: 600/150 = 4.00. The lowest is 3.33.
  4. Check. Tension governs here, because the tensile strength is much smaller than the compressive strength.

Answer: 3.33.

Build time of an additive part

A part 40 mm tall is built by additive manufacturing with layers of 0.1 mm. Each layer takes 90 s. How long does the build take, in hours? Give the answer to one decimal place. (NAT: enter a number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The total build time in hours.
  2. Which idea applies. Layers = height/layer thickness, and total time = layers times time per layer.
  3. Work it out. Layers = 40/0.1 = 400. Time = 400 times 90 = 36000 s = 10.0 hours.
  4. Check. Halving the layer thickness would double the layers and so double the time.

Answer: 10.0 hours.

import math
s1, s2 = 80.0, -20.0
print("Tresca n:", 250 / (s1 - s2))
svm = math.sqrt(s1 ** 2 - s1 * s2 + s2 ** 2)
print("von Mises stress (MPa):", round(svm, 2))
print("von Mises n:", round(250 / svm, 2))
print("Goodman n:", round(1 / (100 / 200 + 50 / 500), 2))
print("Brittle tension n:", round(200 / 60, 2), "compression n:", round(600 / 150, 2))
layers = round(40 / 0.1)
print("Layers:", layers, "build time (h):", layers * 90 / 3600)

Exam-level practice

Exam-level 1: Combined review: shaft (NAT, 2 marks)

A shaft of diameter 40 mm has M = 600 N·m and T = 800 N·m. Sy = 350 MPa. Find the factor of safety by the maximum shear stress theory, correct to 2 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. n = (Sy/2)/τmax.
  2. Which idea applies. τmax = 16√(M² + T²)/(πd³).
  3. Work it out. √(M² + T²) = 1000 N·m. τ = 16e6/(π · 64000) = 79.58 MPa. n = 175/79.58 = 2.20.
  4. Check. The code computes it.
import math
tau = 16 * 1000e3 / (math.pi * 40 ** 3)
print("%.2f MPa, n = %.2f" % (tau, 175 / tau))

79.58 MPa, n = 2.20

Answer: about 2.20.

Exam-level 2: Combined review: von Mises version (NAT, 2 marks)

For the same shaft, find the factor of safety by von Mises, using σvm = (32/(πd³))√(M² + 0.75T²), correct to 2 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. n = Sy/σvm.
  2. Which idea applies. Combine bending σ and torsion τ: √(σ² + 3τ²).
  3. Work it out. √(360000 + 480000) = 916.5 N·m. σvm = 32 · 916.5e3/(π · 64000) = 145.87 MPa. n = 2.40.
  4. Check. von Mises gives a higher factor than Tresca.
import math
s = 32 * math.sqrt(600e3 ** 2 + 0.75 * 800e3 ** 2) / (math.pi * 40 ** 3)
print("%.2f MPa, n = %.2f" % (s, 350 / s))

145.87 MPa, n = 2.40

Answer: about 2.40.

Exam-level 3: Review: data exchange (MCQ, 1 mark)

Which file format is most commonly used to send a part to a 3D printer? (A) STL (B) DXF (C) G-code only (D) PDF

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The usual AM input.
  2. Which idea applies. AM slicers read triangulated surfaces.
  3. Work it out. STL.
  4. Check. 3MF is a newer alternative.

Summary

Here are the one-line meanings of the main ideas in this review.

Principal stress
The normal stress on a plane with no shear, found from the average and radius.
Plane stress
A state where one principal stress is zero, as on a free surface.
Rankine theory
Brittle check, n = Sut/sigma1 for tension.
Tresca theory
Ductile check on the largest shear, the most conservative common rule.
von Mises theory
Ductile check using the equivalent stress sigma', the most common rule.
Brittle compression check
Compare the compressive principal stress with Suc and take the smaller factor.
Modified Goodman
1/n = sigmaa/Se + sigmam/Sut, a dynamic check.
Parametric model
A CAD model whose geometry updates when named dimensions change.
Solid model
A CAD model with a defined volume, so mass can be computed.
Post-processor
Converts toolpaths to the NC code of one machine.
STEP and STL
STEP carries solid data between systems. STL carries triangles for printing.
Additive manufacturing
Building a part layer by layer from a 3D model.
Subtractive manufacturing
Removing material from a block, as in milling or turning.
Layer thickness
The height of each AM layer, which sets the finish and the build time.
Circular toolpath
Length 2 pi R for a full circle of radius R.

Remember these

  1. Find the principal stresses before applying any failure theory.
  2. Tresca is the most conservative common theory for ductile metals, and von Mises is the most common.
  3. Brittle material: check tension with Sut and compression with Suc.
  4. Goodman gives 1/n, so take the reciprocal at the end.
  5. A solid model is needed for mass and volume questions.
  6. Additive builds layer by layer, and thinner layers mean a longer build.
