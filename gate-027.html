Vector identities

Vector identities are rules that let you rewrite dot products, cross products and their combinations in simpler forms. They appear in GATE RA questions on rigid-body motion and robot kinematics, where a long expression often collapses to a short one once the right identity is applied.

The idea in plain words

A vector is an arrow. It has a length and a direction. Two arrows can be combined in two very different ways, and each way answers a different question.

The first way is the dot product. It asks: how much do the two arrows point in the same direction? The answer is a single number, a scalar. If the arrows point the same way, the number is large and positive. If they are at right angles, the number is zero. If they point opposite ways, it is negative.

The second way is the cross product. It asks: what is the area spanned by the two arrows, and which way does that area face? The answer is a new vector. Its length is the area of the parallelogram formed by the two arrows, and its direction is perpendicular to both of them.

Once you have dot and cross products, you can combine them in longer expressions. A few identities tell you how to rewrite these expressions. For example, a long product can collapse to a single term, or a messy vector can be replaced by a simple combination of the original arrows. This matters in GATE RA, where torque, angular velocity and velocity of a point on a spinning body all use these rules.

Key ideaDot product gives a number that measures alignment. Cross product gives a vector that measures the perpendicular area. Identities are rules for rewriting combinations of the two.

Key terms

An arrow with a length and a direction. Written as a list of numbers, such as a = (a1, a2, a3), where each number is a component.
Scalar
An ordinary number, with a size but no direction.

Cross product
a × b, a vector perpendicular to both a and b, with length |a||b| sin θ.
Orthogonal (perpendicular)
Two vectors are orthogonal when their dot product is zero.
Parallel
Two vectors are parallel when one is a scalar multiple of the other. Their cross product is then the zero vector.
Scalar triple product
a · (b × c). It is a number. Its size is the volume of the box (parallelepiped) built on a, b and c.
Vector triple product
a × (b × c). It is a vector, and it is always a combination of b and c.
Anticommutative
The cross product changes sign when you swap the order: a × b = -(b × a).
Distributive
Products spread over sums: a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c and a · (b + c) = a · b + a · c.
Coplanar
Lying in the same flat plane through the origin. Three coplanar vectors have zero scalar triple product.

Step 1: Dot and cross products

The dot product

The dot product multiplies matching components and adds them up. Take a = (1, 2, 3) and b = (4, 5, 6):

a · b = 1×4 + 2×5 + 3×6 = 4 + 10 + 18 = 32

Each part of the sum is a product of one component of a with the matching component of b. The sum is one number, so the dot product is a scalar. Two useful special cases follow:

  • a · a = |a|2. The dot product of a vector with itself is its squared length.
  • If a · b = 0, the arrows are perpendicular.

The dot product is commutative: a · b = b · a. It is also distributive: a · (b + c) = a · b + a · c.

The cross product

The cross product has a formula that looks awkward at first. For a = (a1, a2, a3) and b = (b1, b2, b3):

a × b = (a2b3 - a3b2, a3b1 - a1b3, a1b2 - a2b1)

Each component leaves out one index and takes a difference of two products. The first component uses the second and third entries, the second uses the third and first, and the third uses the first and second. Remember the pattern as "cyclic": 2 and 3, then 3 and 1, then 1 and 2.

Worked example: every step

Find a × b for a = (1, 2, 3) and b = (4, 5, 6).

  1. First component. a2b3 - a3b2 = 2×6 - 3×5 = 12 - 15 = -3.
  2. Second component. a3b1 - a1b3 = 3×4 - 1×6 = 12 - 6 = 6.
  3. Third component. a1b2 - a2b1 = 1×5 - 2×4 = 5 - 8 = -3.
  4. Result. a × b = (-3, 6, -3).

Check: the cross product must be perpendicular to a. Compute a · (a × b) = 1×(-3) + 2×6 + 3×(-3) = -3 + 12 - 9 = 0. It is zero, as it should be.

TrapThe cross product is anticommutative: a × b = -(b × a). Swapping the order flips the sign. Also a × a = 0 for any vector a, because a vector is parallel to itself. Many wrong answers come from forgetting the minus sign.

Question: What is (1, 0, 0) × (0, 1, 0)?

Using the formula: first component 0×0 - 0×1 = 0, second component 0×0 - 1×0 = 0, third component 1×1 - 0×0 = 1. The result is (0, 0, 1), the unit vector along the z-axis.

Question: Is a · b always equal to b · a? Is a × b always equal to b × a?

The dot product is commutative, so a · b = b · a always. The cross product is anticommutative, so a × b = -(b × a) always. The two rules differ, and mixing them up is a common error.

Step 2: The scalar triple product and volume

What it measures

Take three arrows a, b and c that start from the same corner. They form the edges of a box that may be tilted (a parallelepiped). The scalar triple product a · (b × c) gives the volume of that box, with a sign that depends on the order of the arrows. The cross product b × c gives the area of the base, and the dot product with a takes the height along the normal direction.

a · (b × c) = det [ a1 a2 a3 ; b1 b2 b3 ; c1 c2 c3 ]

The matrix has the three vectors as rows. Its determinant is the same number as the triple product. This is why the triple product is zero when the three vectors are coplanar: a flat box has no volume.

Cyclic property

Shifting the three vectors around a cycle does not change the value:

a · (b × c) = b · (c × a) = c · (a × b)

Swapping any two vectors flips the sign. So a · (c × b) = -a · (b × c).

Worked example: volume of a tilted box

Let a = (2, 1, 0), b = (0, 3, 1) and c = (1, 0, 2). Find a · (b × c) and the volume.

  1. Cross product b × c. First component: b2c3 - b3c2 = 3×2 - 1×0 = 6. Second: b3c1 - b1c3 = 1×1 - 0×2 = 1. Third: b1c2 - b2c1 = 0×0 - 3×1 = -3. So b × c = (6, 1, -3).
  2. Dot with a. a · (6, 1, -3) = 2×6 + 1×1 + 0×(-3) = 12 + 1 + 0 = 13.
  3. Volume. The box has volume |13| = 13.
  4. Tetrahedron. The solid with the three arrows as edges from one corner (a tetrahedron, a quarter of the box) has volume 13/6, about 2.167.

GATE loves thisA triple product of coplanar vectors is zero. If a question gives three vectors and asks whether they lie in one plane through the origin, compute the triple product first. Zero means yes.

TrapThe volume of the box is the absolute value of the triple product, but the triple product itself can be negative. Also, the tetrahedron has one sixth of the box, not one half.

Question: If a · (b × c) = 5, what is b · (c × a)?

The cyclic property says a · (b × c) = b · (c × a). So the answer is 5.

Question: Are (1, 2, 3), (2, 4, 6) and (0, 0, 1) coplanar through the origin?

The second vector is twice the first, so these two are parallel and the three vectors lie in one plane through the origin. Their triple product is zero. Check: (1, 2, 3) · ((2, 4, 6) × (0, 0, 1)) = (1, 2, 3) · (4, -2, 0) = 4 - 4 + 0 = 0.

Step 3: The vector triple product (BAC-CAB)

Why it is a vector

The triple product a × (b × c) has two cross products. Its result is a vector. The most important fact about it is that it always lies in the plane of b and c. You can write it as a combination of b and c with scalar coefficients. The rule is:

a × (b × c) = b (a · c) - c (a · b)

Students often remember this as "BAC minus CAB", because the letters in the right-hand side read B, A, C and then C, A, B. The vector b is multiplied by the dot product of a with c, and the vector c is multiplied by the dot product of a with b.

Worked example: every step

Let a = (1, 2, 3), b = (0, 1, 4) and c = (5, 6, 0). Compute a × (b × c) directly and then with the rule.

  1. Inner cross product. b × c = (1×0 - 4×6, 4×5 - 0×0, 0×6 - 1×5) = (-24, 20, -5).
  2. Outer cross product. a × (-24, 20, -5). First component: 2×(-5) - 3×20 = -10 - 60 = -70. Second: 3×(-24) - 1×(-5) = -72 + 5 = -67. Third: 1×20 - 2×(-24) = 20 + 48 = 68. So a × (b × c) = (-70, -67, 68).
  3. Rule, step A. a · c = 1×5 + 2×6 + 3×0 = 17.
  4. Rule, step B. a · b = 0 + 2 + 12 = 14.
  5. Assemble. b(a · c) - c(a · b) = 17(0, 1, 4) - 14(5, 6, 0) = (0, 17, 68) - (70, 84, 0) = (-70, -67, 68).

Both methods agree. The rule is usually faster, because it avoids the messy cross product.

Order matters

The triple product (a × b) × c is different. Using the anticommutative property, (a × b) × c = -c × (a × b). Apply the rule to c × (a × b) and you get (a × b) × c = b (a · c) - a (b · c). Compare this with a × (b × c) = b (a · c) - c (a · b). They are not the same expression.

TrapDo not treat (a × b) × c and a × (b × c) as equal. Brackets matter. Always apply the BAC-CAB rule to the vector that sits on the right inside the brackets.

Question: What is i × (i × j), where i = (1,0,0) and j = (0,1,0)?

Apply the rule with a = i, b = i, c = j. The result is i (i · j) - j (i · i) = i × 0 - j × 1 = -j, which is (0, -1, 0). Direct check: i × j = k and i × k = -j.

Question: Why is a × (b × c) always a combination of b and c?

The cross product a × v is always perpendicular to v. Here v = b × c is perpendicular to both b and c. A vector perpendicular to b × c lies in the plane of b and c, and any vector in that plane is a combination of b and c.

Step 4: Product identities and how to simplify

The four-vector identity

The product of two cross products can be written with dot products only. This identity is often needed:

(a × b) · (c × d) = (a · c)(b · d) - (a · d)(b · c)

Read it as: pair the first vector of each cross product with the first vector of the other, and the second with the second, then subtract the cross pairing. Here is the pattern for a numerical check: a = (1, 2, 3), b = (4, 5, 6), c = (0, 1, 2) and d = (3, -1, 1). Then a × b = (-3, 6, -3) and c × d = (3, 6, -3). The left side is (-3)(3) + 6×6 + (-3)(-3) = -9 + 36 + 9 = 36. The right side is (a · c)(b · d) - (a · d)(b · c) = 8×13 - 4×17 = 104 - 68 = 36.

The Lagrange identity

Setting c = a and d = b in the four-vector identity gives:

|a × b|2 = |a|2 |b|2 - (a · b)2

This tells you the length of the cross product without computing it component by component. It is useful when only lengths and dot products are given, as in many GATE questions.

Simplifying with identities

Simplification means turning a complicated expression into a short one. Three quick rules cover most cases:

  1. a · (a × b) = 0 and b · (a × b) = 0. The cross product is perpendicular to both of its inputs.
  2. a × (a × b) = a (a · b) - b (a · a), from the BAC-CAB rule.
  3. (a × b) · c = a · (b × c), from the cyclic property.

Worked example: robotics velocity

A point on a spinning arm has position r and velocity v = ω × r, where ω is the angular velocity. Show that the velocity is perpendicular to the position: v · r = 0.

  1. Write the dot product. v · r = (ω × r) · r.
  2. Use the scalar triple product. (ω × r) · r = ω · (r × r), by the rule that (a × b) · c = a · (b × c) with the vectors reordered.
  3. Simplify. r × r = 0, so ω · (r × r) = ω · 0 = 0.
  4. Conclusion. v · r = 0. The velocity is always at right angles to the position, which is why a spinning point moves around a circle.

Question: Simplify (a × b) · a.

The cross product a × b is perpendicular to a, so (a × b) · a = 0.

Question: If |a| = 2, |b| = 3 and a · b = 0, what is |a × b|?

Use the Lagrange identity: |a × b|2 = 22 × 32 - 02 = 4 × 9 = 36. So |a × b| = 6.

Solved GATE-style problems

These problems are original GATE-style questions written for practice. They are not past exam questions.

Dot and cross product basics

Let a = (2, 0, 1) and b = (1, 3, 0). Find a · b and the z-component of a × b.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. One dot product, and one component of a cross product.
  2. Which idea applies. Multiply matching components and add for the dot product. For the cross product, the third component is a1b2 - a2b1.
  3. Work it out. a · b = 2×1 + 0×3 + 1×0 = 2. The z-component is a1b2 - a2b1 = 2×3 - 0×1 = 6.
  4. Check. The full cross product is (0×0 - 1×3, 1×1 - 2×0, 2×3 - 0×1) = (-3, 1, 6). Its z-component is 6, which matches.

Answer: a · b = 2, and the z-component of a × b is 6.

Which identities hold?

For any vectors a, b and c, which of the following are true? Select all that apply.

(A) a × b = -(b × a). (B) a · (b × c) = (a × b) · c. (C) a × (b × c) = (a × b) × c. (D) a · (a × b) = 1.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Test each identity on its own.
  2. Which idea applies. Anticommutativity, the cyclic property, the BAC-CAB rule, and the perpendicularity of a cross product.
  3. Work it out. (A) is the anticommutative rule, so it is true. (B) is true, because the triple product is the same determinant with the vectors in the same order. (C) is false in general, because brackets matter. (D) is false, since a · (a × b) = 0.
  4. Check. Take a = i, b = j and c = k (the unit vectors). Then b × c = i, so a × (b × c) = i × i = 0. Also a × b = k, so (a × b) × c = k × k = 0. This example happens to agree, so try another: a = i, b = i and c = j. Then b × c = k, so a × (b × c) = i × k = -j, while a × b = 0, so (a × b) × c = 0. They differ, so (C) is false.

Answer: (A) and (B).

Triple product as a determinant

Let a = (1, 2, 3), b = (0, 1, 4) and c = (5, 6, 0). Find the value of (a × b) · c (NAT style).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. One number, the scalar triple product in a different bracket order.
  2. Which idea applies. Compute a × b first, then dot it with c.
  3. Work it out. a × b = (2×4 - 3×1, 3×0 - 1×4, 1×1 - 2×0) = (5, -4, 1). Then (a × b) · c = 5×5 + (-4)×6 + 1×0 = 25 - 24 + 0 = 1.
  4. Check. By the cyclic property, (a × b) · c = a · (b × c). Earlier we found a · (b × c) = 1, so the answer agrees.

Answer: 1.

Length of a cross product

Vectors a and b satisfy |a| = 2, |b| = 3 and a · b = 0. Find |a × b| (NAT style).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The length of a cross product, given only lengths and a dot product.
  2. Which idea applies. The Lagrange identity |a × b|2 = |a|2|b|2 - (a · b)2.
  3. Work it out. |a × b|2 = 4 × 9 - 02 = 36. Taking the square root gives |a × b| = 6.
  4. Check. A zero dot product means the vectors are perpendicular, so the area is |a||b| = 2 × 3 = 6. This agrees.

Answer: 6.

Simplify a nested product

Which expression equals (a × b) × a for any vectors a and b? Select all that apply.

(A) b(a · a) - a(a · b). (B) a(a · b) - b(a · a). (C) 0. (D) a × b.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A simplified form of a triple product.
  2. Which idea applies. Anticommutativity, then the BAC-CAB rule.
  3. Work it out. (a × b) × a = -a × (a × b). Apply the rule to a × (a × b) with the vectors a, a and b: a × (a × b) = a(a · b) - b(a · a). So (a × b) × a = -[a(a · b) - b(a · a)] = b(a · a) - a(a · b). This is option (A). Option (B) has the wrong sign, (C) is wrong in general, and (D) is a different vector.
  4. Check. Take a = (1,0,0) and b = (0,1,0). Then a × b = (0,0,1), and (a × b) × a = k × i = j = (0,1,0). Option (A) gives b(1) - a(0) = (0,1,0). Match.

Answer: (A) only.

Four-vector identity by hand

Let a = (1, 2, 0), b = (0, 1, 1), c = (2, 0, 1) and d = (1, 1, 0). Find (a × b) · (c × d) (NAT style). Use the four-vector identity to check your answer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. One number, the dot product of two cross products.
  2. Which idea applies. Either compute both cross products directly, or use (a · c)(b · d) - (a · d)(b · c).
  3. Work it out. Identity: a · c = 2, b · d = 1, a · d = 3, b · c = 1. So the value is 2×1 - 3×1 = -1. Direct: a × b = (2×1 - 0×1, 0×0 - 1×1, 1×1 - 2×0) = (2, -1, 1). c × d = (0×0 - 1×1, 1×1 - 2×0, 2×1 - 0×1) = (-1, 1, 2). Dot: 2×(-1) + (-1)×1 + 1×2 = -2 - 1 + 2 = -1.
  4. Check. Both methods give -1, so the answer is confirmed.

Answer: -1.

Velocity of a point on a rotating body

A rigid body rotates with angular velocity ω = (0, 0, 2). A point has position r = (3, 4, 5). Find the velocity v = ω × r and the value of v · r (NAT style, give v · r).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The velocity vector and the dot product of velocity with position.
  2. Which idea applies. Compute the cross product, then the dot product. You can also use the rule that v · r = ω · (r × r) = 0.
  3. Work it out. v = (0×5 - 2×4, 2×3 - 0×5, 0×4 - 0×3) = (-8, 6, 0). Then v · r = (-8)(3) + 6×4 + 0×5 = -24 + 24 + 0 = 0.
  4. Check. The zero dot product confirms the general rule that the velocity is perpendicular to the position.

Answer: v = (-8, 6, 0) and v · r = 0.

Volume of a tetrahedron

A tetrahedron has one corner at the origin and the other three corners at the ends of a = (2, 1, 0), b = (0, 3, 1) and c = (1, 0, 2). Find its volume as a decimal to three places (NAT style).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The volume of a tetrahedron, not of the box.
  2. Which idea applies. The box volume is |a · (b × c)|, and a tetrahedron is one sixth of that box.
  3. Work it out. Earlier we found b × c = (6, 1, -3) and a · (b × c) = 13. The box volume is 13, so the tetrahedron has volume 13/6 = 2.1666..., which rounds to 2.167.
  4. Check. A tetrahedron is a pyramid with a triangular base. Its volume is one third of base area times height, and the box has the base area times height, so the ratio is 1/3 × 1/2 = 1/6.

Answer: 2.167.

Exam-level 1: vector triple product (NAT, 2 marks)

Three vectors are a = (1, 2, 3), b = (0, 1, 4) and c = (5, 0, -1). Use the vector triple product identity a x (b x c) = b(a . c) - c(a . b) to find a x (b x c). Then give the sum of the three components of this vector as a number. As a check, compute the cross products directly as well.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The vector a x (b x c), and the sum of its components.
  2. Which idea applies. The BAC-CAB identity, which needs the two dot products a . c and a . b.
  3. Work it out. a . c = 5 + 0 - 3 = 2 and a . b = 0 + 2 + 12 = 14. So a x (b x c) = 2b - 14c = (0, 2, 8) - (70, 0, -14) = (-70, 2, 22). The components add to -70 + 2 + 22 = -46.
  4. Check. The Python check computes b x c first, then a x (b x c), and compares it with the identity.
def cross(u, v):
    return (u[1]*v[2] - u[2]*v[1], u[2]*v[0] - u[0]*v[2], u[0]*v[1] - u[1]*v[0])

def dot(u, v):
    return sum(p*q for p, q in zip(u, v))

a = (1, 2, 3)
b = (0, 1, 4)
c = (5, 0, -1)
lhs = cross(a, cross(b, c))
rhs = tuple(dot(a, c)*bi - dot(a, b)*ci for bi, ci in zip(b, c))
print("a x (b x c) =", lhs)
print("BAC-CAB right side =", rhs)
print("sum of components =", sum(lhs))
a x (b x c) = (-70, 2, 22)
BAC-CAB right side = (-70, 2, 22)
sum of components = -46

Answer: -46 (the vector is (-70, 2, 22)).

Exam-level 2: volume of a robot's joint parallelepiped (NAT, 2 marks)

Three joint axes of a robot arm, measured in a common frame, are d = (2, -1, 1), e = (0, 3, 1) and f = (1, 1, -2). The parallelepiped spanned by these three vectors has volume equal to the absolute value of the scalar triple product d . (e x f). Find this volume. Then decide whether the three axes lie in one plane, which is the case exactly when the volume is zero. Give the volume as a number.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The volume, and whether the three axes are coplanar.
  2. Which idea applies. The scalar triple product: first the cross product e x f, then a dot product with d.
  3. Work it out. e x f = (3 x (-2) - 1 x 1, 1 x 1 - 0 x (-2), 0 x 1 - 3 x 1) = (-7, 1, -3). Then d . (e x f) = 2(-7) + (-1)(1) + 1(-3) = -14 - 1 - 3 = -18. The volume is |-18| = 18. The volume is not zero, so the axes are not coplanar.
  4. Check. The Python check computes the same triple product with the cross product function and then takes its absolute value.
def cross(u, v):
    return (u[1]*v[2] - u[2]*v[1], u[2]*v[0] - u[0]*v[2], u[0]*v[1] - u[1]*v[0])

def dot(u, v):

print("triple product =", triple)
print("volume =", abs(triple))

Exam-level 3: testing a dot and cross product identity (NAT, 2 marks)

For vectors a = (1, 0, 2), b = (0, 1, 1), c = (2, 1, 0) and d = (1, 2, 1), the identity (a x b) . (c x d) = (a . c)(b . d) - (a . d)(b . c) holds for any vectors. Use this identity to find the value of (a x b) . (c x d) without first computing either cross product. Then check your value by computing the two cross products directly. Give the value as a number.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The number (a x b) . (c x d).
  2. Which idea applies. The identity that turns a dot product of two cross products into four ordinary dot products.
  3. Work it out. a . c = 2 + 0 + 0 = 2. b . d = 0 + 2 + 1 = 3. a . d = 1 + 0 + 2 = 3. b . c = 0 + 1 + 0 = 1. So the value is 2 x 3 - 3 x 1 = 6 - 3 = 3.
  4. Check. The Python check computes both sides. a x b = (-2, -1, 1) and c x d = (1, -2, 3), whose dot product is -2 + 2 + 3 = 3.
def cross(u, v):
    return (u[1]*v[2] - u[2]*v[1], u[2]*v[0] - u[0]*v[2], u[0]*v[1] - u[1]*v[0])

def dot(u, v):
    return sum(p*q for p, q in zip(u, v))

a = (1, 0, 2)
b = (0, 1, 1)
c = (2, 1, 0)
d = (1, 2, 1)
left = dot(cross(a, b), cross(c, d))
right = dot(a, c) * dot(b, d) - dot(a, d) * dot(b, c)
print("left side:", left)
print("right side:", right)
left side: 3
right side: 3

Answer: 3.

Exam-level 4: which vector identities hold always (MSQ, 2 marks)

For vectors a, b and c in three-dimensional space, which of the following statements are true for every choice of vectors? Select all that apply.

(A) a x b = -(b x a). (B) a x (b x c) = (a x b) x c. (C) a . (b x c) = (a x b) . c. (D) a x a = 0.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which identities hold for all vectors, not just for some.
  2. Which idea applies. Swapping the order of a cross product changes its sign. Cross products are not associative. The scalar triple product can be cyclically rearranged.
  3. Work it out. (A) is true, because swapping the two vectors reverses the cross product. (C) is true, because both sides equal the same determinant of the three vectors. (D) is true, because two parallel vectors have zero cross product. (B) is false: take a = (1, 0, 0) and b = c = (0, 1, 0). Then b x c = 0, so the left side is 0. But a x b = (0, 0, 1), and (0, 0, 1) x (0, 1, 0) = (-1, 0, 0), which is not zero.
  4. Check. The Python check evaluates the counterexample for (B).
def cross(u, v):
    return (u[1]*v[2] - u[2]*v[1], u[2]*v[0] - u[0]*v[2], u[0]*v[1] - u[1]*v[0])

a = (1, 0, 0)
b = (0, 1, 0)
c = (0, 1, 0)
print("a x (b x c) =", cross(a, cross(b, c)))
print("(a x b) x c =", cross(cross(a, b), c))
a x (b x c) = (0, 0, 0)
(a x b) x c = (-1, 0, 0)

Answer: (A), (C) and (D). (B) is wrong, because the counterexample above gives different vectors on the two sides.

Summary

Vector
An arrow with length and direction, written as components.
Scalar
A plain number with no direction.
Dot product
Sum of matching component products. A scalar that measures alignment.
Cross product
A vector perpendicular to both inputs, with length equal to the parallelogram area.
Anticommutative
a × b = -(b × a). Swapping the order flips the sign.
Perpendicular test
Two vectors are perpendicular when their dot product is zero.
Scalar triple product
a · (b × c), equal to a determinant with the vectors as rows.
Cyclic property
a · (b × c) = b · (c × a) = c · (a × b).
Coplanar vectors
Vectors lying in one plane. Their triple product is zero.
Vector triple product
a × (b × c), a vector in the plane of b and c.
BAC-CAB rule
a × (b × c) = b(a · c) - c(a · b).
Four-vector identity
(a × b) · (c × d) = (a · c)(b · d) - (a · d)(b · c).
Lagrange identity
|a × b|2 = |a|2|b|2 - (a · b)2.
Tetrahedron volume
One sixth of the absolute value of the triple product.
Velocity of a rotating point
v = ω × r, which is always perpendicular to r.

Remember these

  • Dot product gives a number. Cross product gives a vector.
  • Swapping the two inputs of a cross product flips its sign.
  • A cross product is perpendicular to both of its inputs, so a · (a × b) = 0.
  • Brackets matter: a × (b × c) is not (a × b) × c.
  • Use BAC-CAB for a × (b × c) = b(a · c) - c(a · b).
  • The scalar triple product is zero for coplanar vectors and gives the box volume otherwise.
