Gears and gear trains

Gears are toothed wheels that pass rotation from one shaft to another while changing speed and torque. This page explains the parts of a gear, the three kinds of gear train, and how to find output speed and torque, including the tabular method for planetary gears.

The idea in plain words

Imagine two people with their fingers interlocked. If one hand turns, the other hand must turn too. A pair of gears works the same way. Each gear is a toothed wheel, and the teeth of one wheel push the teeth of the other. Because the teeth cannot slip past each other, the speed ratio is exact every time. A plain friction wheel can slip, but a gear cannot.

Gears trade speed for torque (turning force). A small gear driving a large gear slows the output down and gives it more torque. A bicycle does this when you change to a low gear: the back wheel turns more slowly, but with more force, so a hill is easier to climb. In a robot arm, a motor often reaches a joint through a gear reducer, so the joint turns slowly with a large torque. That is why gear trains matter most in the Robotics and Automation (RA) paper.

Key terms

Read this list once now, and come back to it when a word feels strange.

Gear
A toothed wheel that transmits motion by meshing with another toothed wheel.
Pitch circle
An imaginary circle through a gear. Two meshing gears roll on each other's pitch circles without slipping.
Pitch circle diameter (d)
The diameter of the pitch circle. d = m × T.
Module (m)
How big the teeth are: m = d/T, usually in millimetres. Two gears mesh only if their modules are equal.
Circular pitch (p)
Distance from one tooth to the next, measured on the pitch circle. p = πm.
Pressure angle (φ)
The slant of the push between teeth, measured between the line of action and the common tangent. Standard value: 20 degrees.
Addendum
Height of a tooth above the pitch circle. For a standard gear, addendum = m.
Dedendum
Depth of the tooth space below the pitch circle. For a standard gear, dedendum = 1.25m.
Whole depth
Addendum plus dedendum, which is 2.25m for a standard gear.
Velocity ratio (VR)
Input speed divided by output speed: VR = Nin/Nout.
Gear train
Two or more gears connected so that power flows from the first gear to the last.
Idler gear
A gear between the driver and the driven gear. It changes direction but not the speed ratio.
Simple gear train
A train in which each shaft carries exactly one gear.
Compound gear train
A train in which two or more gears are fixed to the same shaft.
Epicyclic gear train
A train in which at least one gear moves around the centre of another gear. Also called planetary.
Sun gear (S)
The central gear of an epicyclic train.
Planet gear (P)
A gear that orbits the sun gear and meshes with it.
Arm or carrier (A)
The rotating frame that holds the planet gears.
Ring gear or annulus (R)
A gear with teeth on its inside edge, around the planets.
Torque
The turning force on a shaft, in newton metres (N m).
Tabular method
Willis's method: fix the arm, find relative motions in a table, then add one common rotation to every element.

TrapOn this page a bare T means the number of teeth. Torque is always written in words or as τ.

Gear geometry

Why the pitch circle matters

Two gears that mesh behave like two rollers pressed together. If the rollers do not slip, their surfaces move at the same speed. The pitch circles are the imaginary rollers. All speed calculations use the pitch circles.

Module, pitch and the number of teeth

m = d / T, so d = m × T

Here d is the pitch circle diameter in millimetres, T is the number of teeth and m is the module. The circular pitch is

p = πd / T = π m

Notice that the circular pitch depends only on the module, not on the number of teeth.

Addendum, dedendum and the outside diameter

For a standard full-depth gear, the addendum is m and the dedendum is 1.25m. So the two other diameters are:

outside diameter = d + 2m = m(T + 2)
root diameter = d - 2(1.25m) = d - 2.5m

The 2 appears because the addendum sits on both sides of the gear. The outside diameter is the largest size of the gear. The root diameter is the size at the bottom of the tooth spaces.

Key ideaThe pressure angle is the slant of the push between teeth. Standard gears use 20 degrees. Two meshing gears must share both the module and the pressure angle.

Worked example: 30 teeth, module 4 mm

Step 1. Pitch circle diameter: d = m × T = 4 × 30 = 120 mm.

Step 2. Outside diameter: d + 2m = 120 + 8 = 128 mm.

Step 3. Root diameter: d - 2.5m = 120 - 10 = 110 mm.

Step 4. Circular pitch: πm = 3.1416 × 4, about 12.57 mm.

import math

m = 4          # module in mm
T = 30         # number of teeth

d = m * T                  # pitch circle diameter
od = d + 2 * m             # outside diameter (addendum = m)
rd = d - 2 * 1.25 * m      # root diameter (dedendum = 1.25 m)
p = math.pi * m            # circular pitch

print(f"pitch circle diameter = {d:.1f} mm")
print(f"outside diameter = {od:.1f} mm")
print(f"root diameter = {rd:.1f} mm")
print(f"circular pitch = {p:.2f} mm")
pitch circle diameter = 120.0 mm
outside diameter = 128.0 mm
root diameter = 110.0 mm
circular pitch = 12.57 mm
Self-check: if the module doubles and T stays the same, what happens to d?

It doubles, because d = m × T.

Self-check: why can a module 2 gear not mesh with a module 3 gear?

Their teeth have different sizes and spacing, so the teeth would not fit into each other's tooth spaces.

Velocity ratio of one pair of gears

Suppose a driver with 20 teeth meshes with a driven gear with 50 teeth. Each tooth of the driver pushes one tooth of the driven gear. Count the teeth passing the meshing point: both gears pass the same number of teeth per second. So the product of teeth and speed is equal for both gears.

   driver (T1 = 20)                    driven (T2 = 50)
       ___                                  ___
      /   \    teeth push teeth            /   \
     |  o  |  -------------------------->  |  o  |
      \___/                                 \___/
   input shaft, N1 rpm                  output shaft, N2 rpm
T1 × N1 = T2 × N2, so VR = Nin/Nout = Tout/Tin

In the formula, T is the number of teeth and N is the speed in rpm. A velocity ratio above 1 means the output is slower.

Worked example

Step 1. Write the rule: 20 × N1 = 50 × N2.

Step 2. With N1 = 1200 rpm: 20 × 1200 = 24000.

Step 3. Divide by 50: N2 = 24000 / 50 = 480 rpm.

Step 4. Check: the driven gear has more teeth, so it must be slower. 480 is less than 1200. The velocity ratio is 1200/480 = 2.5, which equals 50/20.

Two gears on parallel shafts that mesh externally turn in opposite directions. Each external mesh reverses the direction.

TrapStudents often flip the ratio. The faster gear always has fewer teeth, so use that to check your fraction.

Self-check: a 10-tooth gear meshes with a 40-tooth gear turning at 300 rpm. What is the speed of the 10-tooth gear?

Use 10 × N10 = 40 × 300, so N10 = 12000/10 = 1200 rpm. The small gear spins four times faster.

Self-check: does an idler between two gears change the speed ratio?

No. It changes the direction, not the ratio. The next section shows why.

Simple and compound gear trains

Simple gear train

In a simple gear train each shaft carries one gear. Here gear 2 is an idler.

  gear 1 (driver)     gear 2 (idler)      gear 3 (driven)
    T1 = 20             T2 = 40             T3 = 60
    N1 rpm  --------->  N2 rpm  --------->  N3 rpm
    shaft A             shaft B             shaft C
N2 = N1 × T1/T2 and N3 = N2 × T2/T3

Put the first into the second. The T2 terms cancel:

N3 = N1 × T1/T3

The idler disappears from the speed ratio. Each external mesh reverses direction, so one mesh reverses the output and two meshes (with one idler) keep the same direction as the input.

Key ideaIn a simple train, only the first and last gears set the speed ratio. Idlers change only the direction.

Compound gear train

In a compound gear train, two gears are fixed to one shaft and turn together. This gives a large ratio in a small space.

  shaft A:  gear 1 (T1 = 20) meshes with gear 2 (T2 = 60)
  shaft B:  gears 2 and 3 are fixed together      (N3 = N2)
  shaft C:  gear 3 (T3 = 20) meshes with gear 4 (T4 = 80)
N4/N1 = (T1 × T3) / (T2 × T4)

The rule: the overall ratio is the product of driver teeth divided by the product of driven teeth. Each stage contributes one factor.

Worked example

Gear 1 turns at 1200 rpm. Step 1. Stage 1: N2 = 1200 × 20/60 = 400 rpm. Step 2. Gear 3 shares the shaft, so N3 = 400 rpm. Step 3. Stage 2: N4 = 400 × 20/80 = 100 rpm. Step 4. Check: (20 × 20)/(60 × 80) = 1/12, and 1200/12 = 100 rpm.

from fractions import Fraction as F

T1, T2, T3, T4 = 20, 60, 20, 80
N1 = 1200                 # rpm of the input gear

N2 = N1 * F(T1, T2)       # gear 1 drives gear 2
N3 = N2                   # gears 2 and 3 share a shaft
N4 = N3 * F(T3, T4)       # gear 3 drives gear 4

print("N2 =", float(N2), "rpm")
print("N4 =", float(N4), "rpm")
print("overall ratio N1/N4 =", N1 / N4)
print("overall ratio check =", F(T1 * T3, T2 * T4) == F(1, 12))
N2 = 400.0 rpm
N4 = 100.0 rpm
overall ratio N1/N4 = 12
overall ratio check = True

TrapDo not multiply the speeds of the stages. Multiply the tooth ratios. Gears on one shaft share one speed.

Self-check: in the simple train above, what is N3 when N1 = 600 rpm?

N3 = 600 × 20/60 = 200 rpm. Here T3 is 60, and the idler (40 teeth) does not appear.

Self-check: why use a compound train?

One pair with a large ratio would need a very small driver, which is weak, or a very large driven gear. Two stages give the same ratio with sensible gear sizes.

Torque and power in a gear train

Power is torque times angular speed. A gear train does not create energy, so with no friction losses the power in equals the power out. A slow output shaft therefore has a large torque, and a fast one has a small torque. The torque is multiplied by the same number by which the speed is divided:

Tout/Tin = Nin/Nout = VR

Worked example

The compound train above has overall ratio 12 and an input torque of 5 N m. Step 1. Output torque = 5 × 12 = 60 N m. Step 2. Check by power: input 5 × 1200 = 6000, output 60 × 100 = 6000. They match.

GATE loves thisIf an efficiency η is given, use Tout Nout = η × Tin Nin. If no efficiency is given, assume no losses.

Self-check: speed falls from 900 rpm to 300 rpm and input torque is 4 N m. What is the output torque with no losses?

The ratio is 3, so the output torque is 12 N m.

Self-check: when speed goes down, does torque go up or down?

Torque goes up, because power stays the same.

Epicyclic (planetary) gear trains

In an epicyclic train, a planet gear's centre moves around the sun gear. Car automatic gearboxes use this layout because it is compact and gives large ratios. The planets are carried by an arm, and the ring gear surrounds them.

        ring gear R (teeth point inwards)
   ____________________________________
  |                                    |
  |        planet P       planet P      |
  |          (o)            (o)         |
  |             \        /              |
  |              \  sun S /  <-- arm A   |
  |____________________________________|

  sun S meshes with planet P (outside teeth)
  planet P meshes with ring R (inside teeth)

Three parts can move: the sun, the ring and the arm. The tabular (Willis) method handles this. Step 1: fix the arm and give the sun +1 turn. Find the planet and ring turns. Each external mesh gives a minus sign, and an internal mesh keeps the direction. With the arm fixed, the ring to sun relation is

(ωR - ωA) / (ωS - ωA) = -TS/TR

Step 2: add one common amount x to every element so that the given condition holds (for example, the ring is fixed, so its total must be 0). Step 3: read off the arm and sun totals.

Worked example: ring fixed

Sun: 30 teeth, input at 300 rpm. Planet: 15 teeth. Ring: 60 teeth, fixed. Check: 30 + 2 × 15 = 60, so the numbers fit.

RowOperationArmSunPlanetRing
1Arm fixed, sun +10+1-2-1/2
2Add x to every elementx1 + x-2 + x-1/2 + x
3Ring fixed, so x = 1/21/23/2-3/20

Step 4. Planet check: the sun meshes externally with the planet, so the planet's fixed-arm turn is -TS/TP = -30/15 = -2 per sun turn. Ring check: -2 × 15/60 = -1/2, as the table shows. Step 5. Arm per sun turn = (1/2)/(3/2) = 1/3. At 300 rpm the arm turns at 100 rpm, in the same direction as the sun. The shortcut gives the same answer: 30 × 300/(30 + 60) = 100 rpm.

from fractions import Fraction as F

Ts, Tp, Tr = 30, 15, 60
print("ring teeth = sun + 2 x planet:", Tr == Ts + 2 * Tp)

arm, sun, ring = F(0), F(1), -F(Ts, Tr)   # step 1: arm fixed, sun +1 turn
x = -ring                                  # step 2: add x to every element
arm, sun, ring = arm + x, sun + x, ring + x

print("ring turns after step 2:", ring)
print("arm turns per sun turn:", arm / sun)
print("arm rpm for sun at 300 rpm:", 300 * arm / sun)
ring teeth = sun + 2 x planet: True
ring turns after step 2: 0
arm turns per sun turn: 1/3
arm rpm for sun at 300 rpm: 100

TrapBefore you start, write down which element is fixed, which is the input and which is the output. Most epicyclic errors come from fixing the wrong element.

Key ideaWith the sun as input and the ring fixed, the arm turns the same way as the sun, at TS/(TS + TR) of its speed.

Self-check: why is the arm slower than the sun in the example?

The ring is fixed, so the planets must walk around it. The arm gets only 30/90 = 1/3 of the sun's speed.

Self-check: the ring is the input at 900 rpm, the sun is fixed, and TS = 30, TR = 70. What is the arm speed?

ωA = TRωR/(TS + TR) = 70 × 900/100 = 630 rpm, in the same direction as the ring.

Solved GATE-style problems

These are original GATE-style problems for practice, not real past paper questions. Try each one before reading the answer.

Gear size from module

A spur gear has 40 teeth and module 2.5 mm. Find its pitch circle diameter and its outside diameter.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Two diameters of one gear.
  2. Which idea applies. d = m × T and outside diameter = m(T + 2).
  3. Work it out. d = 2.5 × 40 = 100 mm. Outside diameter = 2.5 × 42 = 105 mm.
  4. Check. The outside diameter must be 2m = 5 mm bigger than d. 105 - 100 = 5, correct.

Answer: 100 mm and 105 mm.

Output of a single mesh

A driver gear with 24 teeth turns at 600 rpm and meshes with a driven gear of 36 teeth. Find the speed of the driven gear and its direction compared with the driver.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Speed and direction of the driven gear.
  2. Which idea applies. T1N1 = T2N2, and one external mesh reverses direction.
  3. Work it out. N2 = 24 × 600 / 36 = 400 rpm.
  4. Check. More teeth means slower: 400 is less than 600. One external mesh gives reversal.

Answer: 400 rpm, opposite direction.

Two-stage compound train

Gear A (20 teeth) is the input at 1000 rpm and meshes with gear B (50 teeth). Gear C (16 teeth) is on the same shaft as gear B and meshes with gear D (64 teeth). Find the speed of gear D.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The speed of gear D.
  2. Which idea applies. B and C share a speed. Each stage multiplies by driver over driven teeth.
  3. Work it out. NB = 1000 × 20/50 = 400 rpm. NC = 400 rpm. ND = 400 × 16/64 = 100 rpm.
  4. Check. Overall ratio (20 × 16)/(50 × 64) = 1/10, and 1000/10 = 100.

Answer: 100 rpm.

Which statements about a three-gear train are true?

A simple gear train has three gears in a line: driver (gear 1), idler (gear 2) and driven (gear 3). Consider these statements.

(A) The idler changes the speed ratio between gear 1 and gear 3.
(B) Gear 3 turns in the same direction as gear 1.
(C) The output speed equals the input speed times T1/T3.
(D) The idler must have a different module from gears 1 and 3.

Select all that apply.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements are true (multiple selection).
  2. Which idea applies. The idler cancels from the ratio. Two external meshes keep the direction. Meshing gears share one module.
  3. Work it out. (A) false, the idler cancels. (B) true, two reversals. (C) true, N3 = N1T1/T3. (D) false, all meshing gears share a module.
  4. Check. With T1 = 20, T3 = 60, N1 = 600, N3 = 200 whatever the idler size, which supports (C).

Answer: (B) and (C).

Speed after two stages

A compound train has stage 1 with a driver of 18 teeth and a driven gear of 54 teeth, and stage 2 with a driver of 15 teeth and a driven gear of 45 teeth. The input speed is 1440 rpm. Find the output speed in rpm (numerical answer).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The output speed as a number.
  2. Which idea applies. Multiply the stage ratios.
  3. Work it out. Stage 1 = 18/54 = 1/3. Stage 2 = 15/45 = 1/3. Overall = 1/9. Output = 1440/9 = 160 rpm.
  4. Check. Each stage divides speed by 3, so two stages divide by 9.
from fractions import Fraction as F

ratio = F(18, 54) * F(15, 45)
print("overall ratio =", ratio)
print("output rpm =", float(1440 * ratio))
overall ratio = 1/9
output rpm = 160.0

Answer: 160 rpm.

Planetary train with a fixed ring

In a simple epicyclic train, the sun gear (40 teeth) is the input at 600 rpm. The planets have 20 teeth. The ring gear (80 teeth) is fixed. Find the arm speed and say whether it turns the same way as the sun.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Arm speed and direction.
  2. Which idea applies. Check ring = sun + 2 × planet, then use ωA = TSωS/(TS + TR) with ωR = 0.
  3. Work it out. 40 + 2 × 20 = 80, correct. ωA = 40 × 600 / 120 = 200 rpm.
  4. Check. The arm must be slower than the sun, and 200 is less than 600. The sign is positive, so it turns the same way.
from fractions import Fraction as F

Ts, Tp, Tr = 40, 20, 80
ws = 600
print("ring check:", Tr == Ts + 2 * Tp)
arm = F(Ts * ws, Ts + Tr)
print("arm rpm =", float(arm))
ring check: True
arm rpm = 200.0

Answer: 200 rpm, same direction as the sun.

Planetary train with a fixed sun

A simple epicyclic train has a fixed sun gear with 30 teeth. The planets have 20 teeth. The ring gear (70 teeth) is the input at 900 rpm. Find the arm speed, which is the output.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Arm speed when the ring drives and the sun is fixed.
  2. Which idea applies. Check ring = sun + 2 × planet. Then ωA = TRωR/(TS + TR).
  3. Work it out. 30 + 40 = 70, correct. ωA = 70 × 900 / 100 = 630 rpm.
  4. Check. Using the fixed-arm relation: (900 - 630)/(0 - 630) = -270/630 = -3/7, and -TS/TR = -30/70 = -3/7. Correct.

Answer: 630 rpm, same direction as the ring.

Exam-level practice

Exam-level 1: Gear ratio (NAT, 2 marks)

A compound train has gears A (20) driving B (60), B is keyed to C (25) driving D (75). A turns at 1800 rpm. Find the speed of D.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The output speed.
  2. Which idea applies. Train value = product of driver teeth / product of driven teeth.
  3. Work it out. (20/60)(25/75) = 1/9. D = 200 rpm.
  4. Check. Same direction as A, since two meshes reverse twice.

Answer: 200 rpm.

Exam-level 2: Epicyclic train (NAT, 2 marks)

A simple epicyclic train has sun 30 teeth and ring 90 teeth. The ring is fixed and the sun turns at 400 rpm. Find the carrier speed.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Carrier speed.
  2. Which idea applies. Willis: (Ns − Nc)/(Nr − Nc) = −Tr/Ts.
  3. Work it out. (400 − Nc)/(0 − Nc) = −3. 400 − Nc = 3Nc, Nc = 100 rpm.
  4. Check. Ratio 1 + Tr/Ts = 4.

Answer: 100 rpm.

Exam-level 3: Minimum teeth (NAT, 2 marks)

For a 20° full depth pinion meshing with a rack (addendum 1 module), find the minimum number of teeth to avoid interference.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Tmin = 2/sin²φ.
  2. Which idea applies. Rack interference condition.
  3. Work it out. 2/0.117 = 17.1, so 18 teeth.
  4. Check. The code computes it.
import math
print(2 / math.sin(math.radians(20)) ** 2)

17.09726434082606

Answer: 18 (from 17.1).

A toothed wheel that transmits motion by meshing.

The imaginary circle on which meshing gears roll without slipping.
Module
Pitch circle diameter divided by number of teeth, m = d/T.
Circular pitch
Distance along the pitch circle from one tooth to the next, equal to πm.
Pressure angle
The slant of the tooth push, usually 20 degrees.

Height of a tooth above the pitch circle, equal to m.
Dedendum
Depth of a tooth space below the pitch circle, equal to 1.25m.
Outside diameter
Largest diameter of a gear, equal to m(T + 2).
Velocity ratio
Input speed over output speed, equal to output teeth over input teeth for one pair.

One gear per shaft. Only the first and last gears set the ratio.
Idler gear
A middle gear that changes direction but not the ratio.
Compound gear train
Two gears on some shafts. The overall ratio is a product of stage ratios.
Torque ratio
Output torque over input torque, equal to the velocity ratio with no losses.
Epicyclic gear train
A train with planet gears carried by an arm around a sun gear.
Sun, planet, ring, arm
The four parts of a simple epicyclic train.
Tabular method
Fix the arm, find relative motions, then add one common rotation.
Fixed-arm relation
With the arm fixed, the ring to sun ratio is -TS/TR.
Arm speed shortcut
With one planet, ωA = (TSωS + TRωR)/(TS + TR).

Remember these

  • Two gears mesh only when they share a module and a pressure angle.
  • Velocity ratio is input speed over output speed.
  • Idlers change direction, not the speed ratio.
  • Each external mesh reverses the direction of rotation.
  • In a compound train, gears on one shaft share one speed.
  • With no losses, slow output means high torque.
  • In an epicyclic train, fix the arm first, then add one common rotation.
  • Check that ring teeth equal sun teeth plus twice the planet teeth.
