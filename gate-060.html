Fourier Series

A Fourier series writes a repeating signal as a sum of simple sine and cosine waves. Engineers use it to see which frequencies a signal contains, and to study filters and vibrations. This page builds the idea from a musical chord, gives the coefficient formulas with their meaning, shows the shortcuts for even and odd functions, and works through the GATE-style calculations.

The idea in plain words

Play a single note on a piano and you hear one pitch. Play a chord and you hear several pitches together. The sound wave of the chord is the sum of the individual waves. Even though the combined wave looks complicated on a screen, it is built from simple sine waves.

A Fourier series makes this idea exact. It says that a repeating signal can be written as a sum of sine and cosine waves, each with a whole-number multiple of a basic frequency. The formula looks big at first, but each piece has a clear job. The first term gives the average level. The later terms add the wiggles, from slow to fast.

Think of a recipe. A cake is made from flour, sugar, eggs and butter. The recipe tells you how much of each ingredient to add. A Fourier series is a recipe for a signal: it tells you how much of each sine and cosine wave to add. The numbers that give the amounts are the coefficients. Finding them is the main job in every Fourier problem.

Why do engineers care? A filter can remove high frequencies from a noisy signal, and a motor controller can predict how a repeating force will shake a machine. Both start by writing the signal as a Fourier series.

Why this matters for GATE. In the Robotics and Automation paper, Fourier series appear in signal analysis, vibration of rotating parts and control. Questions often ask for a coefficient, the value of the series at a point, or whether a function is even or odd. The calculations are short if you use the shortcuts in this topic.

Key terms

Read this list once now. You will use these words in every example.

Periodic function
A function that repeats itself. If f(x + T) = f(x) for all x, then T is a period. Example: sin x has period 2π.
Period
The length of one full repeat. The standard period in this topic is 2π, the length of one full turn of a circle.
Frequency
How many cycles happen per unit length. The wave cos(nx) has n cycles in each 2π.
Harmonic
A wave whose frequency is a whole-number multiple of the basic frequency. The n-th harmonic is cos(nx) or sin(nx).
Coefficient
A number that multiplies a wave in the series. an multiplies cos(nx), and bn multiplies sin(nx).
a0 term
The constant term. Its half, a0/2, is the average value of the function over one period.
Integral
A way of adding up many tiny pieces. ∫ab g(x) dx is the total of g over the interval from a to b.
Orthogonality
The fact that sine and cosine waves with different frequencies average to zero when multiplied together over a period. This is why the coefficients can be found one at a time.
Even function
A function with f(-x) = f(x), symmetric about the vertical axis. Example: x2 or cos x.
Odd function
A function with f(-x) = -f(x), symmetric about the origin. Example: x or sin x.
Half-range series
A Fourier series for a function given only on half a period, extended to the full period in a chosen way.
Dirichlet conditions
Mild rules that guarantee the Fourier series converges. Roughly: the function is bounded, has a finite number of jumps, and has finitely many maxima and minima in a period.
Jump discontinuity
A point where a function suddenly changes value, with a left limit and a right limit that differ.
Gibbs phenomenon
A small overshoot near a jump in a partial sum. It does not vanish as more terms are added, but it becomes narrower.
Partial sum
The sum of the series up to a chosen number of terms, N. Example: the sum of the first 5 terms.

Step 1: The series and its coefficient formulas

The standard form

Take a function f(x) with period 2π. Its Fourier series is:

f(x) = a0/2 + Σn=1∞ (an cos nx + bn sin nx)

Here Σ means "add up", the sum runs over n = 1, 2, 3, and so on forever, and ∞ means "without end". The coefficients an and bn are numbers that we must find for each n. The constant term is written a0/2 so that its formula looks the same as the others.

The formulas for the coefficients

a0 = (1/π) ∫-ππ f(x) dx
an = (1/π) ∫-ππ f(x) cos nx dx
bn = (1/π) ∫-ππ f(x) sin nx dx

Read these carefully. Each formula multiplies f(x) by a wave and adds up the result over one full period, from -π to π. The factor 1/π is a normalising constant. It makes the answer come out right.

Why the formulas work: orthogonality

Here is the key fact. Over one period, the integral of a sine or cosine multiplied by a different sine or cosine is zero. For example, ∫-ππ cos 2x sin 3x dx = 0, and ∫-ππ cos 2x cos 3x dx = 0. But when the frequencies match, the integral is not zero. For example, ∫-ππ cos2(3x) dx = π. This matching makes each coefficient "pick out" its own wave, like a sieve that catches one size of stone.

Worked example 1: the sawtooth x on (-π, π)

Find the Fourier series of f(x) = x on the period from -π to π. Then repeat the pattern outside this interval.

  1. The constant term. a0 = (1/π) ∫-ππ x dx. The integral of x from -π to π is [x2/2] evaluated from -π to π, which is (π2/2) - (π2/2) = 0. So a0 = 0.
  2. The cosine coefficients. an = (1/π) ∫ x cos nx dx. The function x is odd and cos nx is even, so their product is odd. The integral of an odd function over a symmetric interval is zero. So an = 0 for all n.
  3. The sine coefficients. bn = (1/π) ∫-ππ x sin nx dx. Here x sin nx is even, so the integral equals twice the integral from 0 to π: bn = (2/π) ∫0π x sin nx dx.
  4. Integrate by parts. Use ∫ u dv = uv - ∫ v du with u = x and dv = sin nx dx. Then v = -cos nx/n. So ∫0π x sin nx dx = [-x cos nx/n] from 0 to π, plus ∫0π cos nx/n dx. The second integral is [sin nx/n2] from 0 to π, which is 0 because sin nπ = 0.
  5. Evaluate the first part. At x = π, -π cos nπ/n = -π(-1)n/n. At x = 0 the term is 0. So ∫0π x sin nx dx = -π(-1)n/n.
  6. Combine. bn = (2/π) · (-π(-1)n/n) = -2(-1)n/n = 2(-1)n+1/n.
  7. Series. x = 2 sin x - sin(2x) + (2/3) sin(3x) - .... The first few terms are 2, -1, 2/3, -1/2, and so on.

TrapWhen you integrate by parts, keep the sign. The minus sign in v = -cos nx/n is easy to drop. Check each step by differentiating your answer back.

The program below adds the first 1000 terms at x = π/2 and compares the result with π/2. At x = π/2 the true value is π/2.

import math

def partial_sum(x, N):
    total = 0.0
    for n in range(1, N + 1):
        bn = 2 * (-1) ** (n + 1) / n
        total += bn * math.sin(n * x)
    return total

print("partial sum at x = pi/2 with 1000 terms:", round(partial_sum(math.pi / 2, 1000), 2))
print("pi/2 =", round(math.pi / 2, 2))
partial sum at x = pi/2 with 1000 terms: 1.57
pi/2 = 1.57
Question: What is a0/2 for any function, in words?

It is the average value of the function over one period. For the sawtooth x on (-π, π), the average is 0, which matches a0 = 0.

Question: Why does the sine coefficient bn of x on (-π, π) alternate in sign?

Because of the factor (-1)n+1 that comes from cos nπ = (-1)n. The sign flips each time n increases by one.

Step 2: Even and odd functions, and half-range series

Why symmetry saves work

Many functions are symmetric, and symmetry tells you which coefficients must be zero. Before you integrate, ask whether the function is even or odd. This can cut the work in half, or more.

  • Even function, f(-x) = f(x). Its graph is a mirror image across the vertical axis. Cosine is even, because cos(-x) = cos x. Then all bn = 0. The series is a cosine series.
  • Odd function, f(-x) = -f(x). Its graph is unchanged by a half turn about the origin. Sine is odd, because sin(-x) = -sin x. Then all an = 0, including a0. The series is a sine series.

For an even function, the coefficient formulas become an = (2/π) ∫0π f(x) cos nx dx. For an odd function, bn = (2/π) ∫0π f(x) sin nx dx. In both cases you integrate only from 0 to π, which halves the work.

Key ideaOdd times even is odd, and even times even is even. An integral of an odd function over a symmetric interval is zero. This is the reason behind every zero coefficient in a symmetric problem.

Worked example 2: the even function x2

Find the Fourier series of f(x) = x2 on (-π, π).

  1. Symmetry. x2 is even, so every bn = 0.
  2. Constant term. a0 = (2/π) ∫0π x2 dx = (2/π)(π3/3) = 2π2/3.
  3. Cosine coefficients. an = (2/π) ∫0π x2 cos nx dx. Integrate by parts twice. The first step gives [x2 sin nx/n] minus ∫ 2x sin nx/n dx. The boundary term is zero, since sin nπ = 0.
  4. Second integration by parts. ∫0π 2x sin nx dx = 2π(-1)n+1/n, using the result from Worked example 1 for ∫ x sin nx dx. Then an = -(1/n) × 2π(-1)n+1/n × (2/π) = 4(-1)n/n2.
  5. Series. x2 = a0/2 + Σ an cos nx = π2/3 + Σ 4(-1)n cos(nx)/n2.

The program checks the series at x = π. The true value is π2, and the partial sum with 2000 terms should be close to it.

import math

def partial_sum_x2(x, N):
    total = math.pi ** 2 / 3
    for n in range(1, N + 1):
        total += 4 * (-1) ** n / n ** 2 * math.cos(n * x)
    return total

print("x^2 series at x = pi:", round(partial_sum_x2(math.pi, 2000), 2))
print("pi^2 =", round(math.pi ** 2, 2))
x^2 series at x = pi: 9.87
pi^2 = 9.87

Half-range series

Sometimes a function is given only on half a period, such as f(x) on (0, L). We can extend it to a full period in two ways, and each choice gives a different series:

  • Sine series (odd extension). Extend f to be odd. The series has only sine terms: f(x) = Σ bn sin(nπx/L), with bn = (2/L) ∫0L f(x) sin(nπx/L) dx.
  • Cosine series (even extension). Extend f to be even. The series has only cosine terms: f(x) = a0/2 + Σ an cos(nπx/L), with an = (2/L) ∫0L f(x) cos(nπx/L) dx.

Read the question carefully. It will say "half-range sine" or "half-range cosine", and the formula you use depends on that word.

Worked example 3: the function f(x) = 1 on (0, 1), with the sine series. Here L = 1, so bn = 2 ∫01 sin(nπx) dx = 2(1 - cos nπ)/(nπ). For odd n this is 4/(nπ), and for even n it is 0. The first coefficient is 4/π, which is about 1.2732. The program below checks this with a numerical integral.

import math

def b(n, N=100000):
    # half-range sine coefficient for f(x) = 1 on (0, 1): b_n = 2 * integral of sin(n pi x)
    h = 1.0 / N
    total = 0.0
    for k in range(N):
        x = (k + 0.5) * h
        total += math.sin(n * math.pi * x)
    return 2 * total * h

print("b1 =", round(b(1), 4), "and 4/pi =", round(4 / math.pi, 4))
print("b2 is about zero:", abs(b(2)) < 1e-6)
b1 = 1.2732 and 4/pi = 1.2732
b2 is about zero: True

TrapFor a half-range series, the integral runs over (0, L), not over a full period. Also, the factor is 2/L, not 1/π, because the period of the extension is 2L.

Question: A function is odd. Which coefficients are zero?

All an, including a0. Only sine terms remain.

Question: For a half-range sine series on (0, 2), what is the factor in front of the integral for bn?

The factor is 2/L with L = 2, so it is 1. The formula is bn = ∫02 f(x) sin(nπx/2) dx.

Step 3: General period, convergence and the jump

Changing the period

Most engineering signals do not have period 2π. A signal might repeat every T seconds. Then the basic frequency is ω0 = 2π/T, and the harmonics are nω0. The series becomes:

f(t) = a0/2 + Σ (an cos nω0t + bn sin nω0t), with ω0 = 2π/T

The coefficients use the same idea, but the integral runs over one full period T, and the factor is 2/T instead of 1/π:

an = (2/T) ∫ f(t) cos nω0t dt, bn = (2/T) ∫ f(t) sin nω0t dt

The integrals run over any interval of length T. Choose the interval that makes the function simplest, usually from -T/2 to T/2 or from 0 to T.

Where the series converges

When the function is smooth, the Fourier series converges to the function itself at every point. When there is a jump, the series converges to the average of the two limits. Write f(t-) for the value just before the jump, and f(t+) for the value just after. Then the series gives:

series value at a jump = (f(t-) + f(t+))/2

This is a standard result for functions that satisfy the Dirichlet conditions. It is one of the most common GATE traps, so learn it well.

Worked example 4: the square wave

Let f(x) = 1 for 0 < x < π and f(x) = -1 for -π < x < 0, repeated with period 2π.

  1. Symmetry. f is odd, so every an = 0 and a0 = 0.
  2. Sine coefficients. bn = (2/π) ∫0π sin nx dx = (2/π)(1 - cos nπ)/n.
  3. Simplify. For odd n, cos nπ = -1, so bn = 4/(nπ). For even n, cos nπ = 1, so bn = 0.
  4. Series. f(x) = (4/π)(sin x + (1/3) sin 3x + (1/5) sin 5x + ...).
  5. Value at the jump. At x = 0, the left limit is -1 and the right limit is 1. The average is 0. The series gives sin 0 = 0 for every term, so it converges to 0, as the rule predicts.

The Gibbs overshoot

Near a jump, the partial sums overshoot. For the square wave, the overshoot peak is about 9 percent of the jump size, and it does not disappear as more terms are added. The peak moves closer to the jump, but its height stays. The program below shows the peak for 51 terms.

import math

def square_partial(x, N):
    total = 0.0
    for n in range(1, N + 1, 2):
        total += 4 / (n * math.pi) * math.sin(n * x)
    return total

N = 51
xs = [k * 0.001 for k in range(1, 2000)]
peak = max(square_partial(x, N) for x in xs)
print("peak of partial sum with", N, "terms:", round(peak, 2))
print("value at x = pi/2:", round(square_partial(math.pi / 2, N), 2))
peak of partial sum with 51 terms: 1.18
value at x = pi/2: 0.99

GATE loves thisGibbs overshoot does not go away with more terms. A question that asks whether the maximum error near a jump tends to zero as N grows has the answer no. Only the width of the overshoot shrinks.

Question: A periodic signal jumps from 2 to 6 at t = 3. What does its Fourier series converge to at t = 3?

The average of the two limits: (2 + 6)/2 = 4.

Question: If the period of a signal is T = 4 s, what is the fundamental angular frequency ω0?

ω0 = 2π/T = 2π/4 = π/2 rad/s.

Step 4: Typical GATE calculations

A four-step routine

Most Fourier questions in GATE can be solved with this routine:

  1. Read the period. If it is not 2π, use ω0 = 2π/T and the factor 2/T.
  2. Check symmetry. If the function is even or odd, half of the coefficients are zero. Use the half-range integral.
  3. Compute only what is asked. Often the question wants one coefficient or one value of the series. Do not compute extra terms.
  4. Evaluate at the point. If the point is a jump, use the average of the two limits.

Worked example 5: a period-2 sawtooth

Let f(t) = t on 0 ≤ t < 2, repeated with period 2. Find the average value, the coefficient b1, and the value the series converges to at t = 2.

  1. Period. T = 2, so ω0 = π and the factor is 2/T = 1.
  2. Average. a0 = (2/T) ∫02 t dt = ∫02 t dt = [t2/2] from 0 to 2 = 2. So the average value is a0/2 = 1.
  3. First sine coefficient. b1 = ∫02 t sin(πt) dt. Integrate by parts: [-t cos(πt)/π] from 0 to 2 gives -2 cos(2π)/π = -2/π. The remaining term is [sin(πt)/π2] from 0 to 2, which is 0. So b1 = -2/π, which is about -0.6366.
  4. Value at the jump. At t = 2 the function jumps from 2 (the left limit) back to 0 (the right limit, since the next period starts at 0). The series converges to (2 + 0)/2 = 1.

The program checks b1 with a numerical integral.

import math

def b1(N=200000):
    # b_1 = (2/T) * integral from 0 to 2 of t sin(pi t) dt, with T = 2 so 2/T = 1
    h = 2.0 / N
    total = 0.0
    for k in range(N):
        t = (k + 0.5) * h
        total += t * math.sin(math.pi * t)
    return total * h

print("b1 =", round(b1(), 4), "and -2/pi =", round(-2 / math.pi, 4))
b1 = -0.6366 and -2/pi = -0.6366

TipWrite the answer to a jump question as "average of the limits" before you compute anything. This tells you the answer to check against at the end.

Question: For the period-2 sawtooth above, what is the value of the series at t = 1?

At t = 1 the function is continuous, with f(1) = 1. So the series converges to 1, the function value.

Question: A signal has period T = 1 s. Which factor replaces 1/π in the coefficient formulas?

The factor 2/T = 2. For a general period, the coefficients use (2/T) times the integral over one period.

Solved GATE-style problems

These problems are original, written in the style of GATE questions. They are not past exam questions. Try each one on paper before you read the answer.

A coefficient of a sum of waves

Let f(x) = 3 + 2 cos x on (-π, π), with period 2π. Find a1. (Answer type: NAT, a whole number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The cosine coefficient for n = 1.
  2. Which idea applies. Use an = (1/π) ∫ f cos nx dx with n = 1, and orthogonality.
  3. Work it out. a1 = (1/π) ∫-ππ (3 + 2 cos x) cos x dx = (1/π) [3 ∫ cos x dx + 2 ∫ cos2 x dx]. The first integral is 0. The second is 2π, because ∫-ππ cos2 x dx = π. So a1 = (1/π)(2π) = 2.
  4. Check. The function is already a cosine wave of amplitude 2 plus a constant, so the coefficient of cos x must be exactly 2.

Answer: a1 = 2.

Properties of x squared

Let f(x) = x2 on (-π, π), extended with period 2π. Select all that apply.

(A) f is an even function.
(B) bn = 0 for every n.
(C) a0 = π2/3.
(D) The Fourier series converges to π2 at x = π.

Answer type: MSQ, select all that apply.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which of the four statements are true.
  2. Which idea applies. Symmetry for (A) and (B), the coefficient formula for (C), and the convergence rule for (D).
  3. Work it out. (A) (-x)2 = x2, so f is even. True. (B) An even function has zero sine coefficients. True. (C) a0 = (2/π) ∫0π x2 dx = 2π2/3, not π2/3. False. (D) f is continuous at π, because f(π) = π2 and the periodic copy also gives π2 at -π. So the series converges to π2. True.
  4. Check. The average of x2 over (-π, π) is (π2/3), and a0/2 must equal this average. So a0 = 2π2/3, which confirms (C) is false.

Answer: (A), (B) and (D).

A higher sine coefficient

Let f(x) = x on (-π, π), extended with period 2π. Find b3, rounded to four decimal places. (Answer type: NAT, a decimal.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The sine coefficient for n = 3.
  2. Which idea applies. The function is odd, so use bn = (2/π) ∫0π x sin nx dx. Worked example 1 gave bn = 2(-1)n+1/n.
  3. Work it out. With n = 3, (-1)4 = 1, so b3 = 2/3, which is about 0.6667.
  4. Check. The program integrates the definition numerically over (-π, π).
import math

N = 200000
h = 2 * math.pi / N
total = 0.0
for k in range(N):
    x = -math.pi + (k + 0.5) * h
    total += x * math.sin(3 * x)
print(round(total * h / math.pi, 4))
0.6667

Answer: b3 = 2/3, which is 0.6667 to four decimal places.

Half-range sine coefficient

Find the first coefficient b1 of the half-range sine series of f(x) = x on (0, 1), rounded to four decimal places. (Answer type: NAT, a decimal.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. b1 for the sine series on (0, 1).
  2. Which idea applies. Half-range sine formula with L = 1: bn = 2 ∫01 x sin nπx dx.
  3. Work it out. Integrate by parts as in the sawtooth example: ∫01 x sin nπx dx = -cos(nπ)/(nπ) + 0 = -(-1)n/(nπ). So bn = -2(-1)n/(nπ). For n = 1, b1 = 2/π, which is about 0.6366.
  4. Check. The program evaluates 2/π.
import math

print(round(2 / math.pi, 4))
0.6366

Answer: b1 = 2/π, which is 0.6366 to four decimal places.

Square wave facts

Let f(x) = 1 for 0 < x < π and f(x) = -1 for -π < x < 0, with period 2π. Select all that apply.

(A) f is an odd function, so only sine terms appear.
(B) b2 is not zero.
(C) The series converges to 0 at x = 0.
(D) The series converges to 1 at x = π/2.

Answer type: MSQ, select all that apply.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which of the four statements are true.
  2. Which idea applies. Symmetry, the coefficient formula bn = 4/(nπ) for odd n and 0 for even n, and the jump rule.
  3. Work it out. (A) f(-x) = -f(x), so f is odd. True. (B) n = 2 is even, so b2 = 0. False. (C) At x = 0 the average of the limits -1 and 1 is 0. True. (D) f is continuous at π/2 with value 1. The series converges to 1 there. True.
  4. Check. The series at π/2 is (4/π)(1 - 1/3 + 1/5 - ...). The bracket is π/4, so the sum is (4/π)(π/4) = 1, which confirms (D).

Answer: (A), (C) and (D).

A famous sum from a Fourier series

Use the Fourier series of f(x) = x2 on (-π, π) to find the value of Σn=1∞ (-1)n+1/n2. Give the answer rounded to four decimal places. (Answer type: NAT, a decimal.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The value of an infinite sum of fractions, using a Fourier series.
  2. Which idea applies. Substitute a point where the series is easy to evaluate. At x = 0, cos(0) = 1 for every term.
  3. Work it out. The series for x2 is x2 = π2/3 + Σ 4(-1)n cos(nx)/n2. At x = 0 the left side is 0, and cos(0) = 1, so 0 = π2/3 + Σ 4(-1)n/n2. Therefore Σ (-1)n/n2 = -π2/12, and Σ (-1)n+1/n2 = π2/12 ≈ 0.8225.
  4. Check. The program adds 100000 terms of the sum directly.
print(round(sum((-1) ** (n + 1) / n ** 2 for n in range(1, 100001)), 4))
0.8225

Answer: the sum is π2/12, which is 0.8225 to four decimal places.

What a series converges to at a jump

Let f(t) = t on 0 ≤ t < 2, repeated with period 2, so f jumps from 2 back to 0 at each even time. What value does the Fourier series of f converge to at t = 2? (Answer type: NAT, a whole number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The value of the series at the jump point t = 2.
  2. Which idea applies. At a jump, the series gives the average of the left and right limits.
  3. Work it out. The left limit at t = 2 is f(2-) = 2, from the end of the first period. The right limit is f(2+) = f(0) = 0, because the next period starts at 0. The average is (2 + 0)/2 = 1.
  4. Check. Worked example 5 gave b1 = -2/π, and the average value a0/2 = 1. The value at the jump is 1, which is also the average of the function, a sensible result.

Answer: 1.

Exam-level 1: Fourier coefficient (NAT, 2 marks)

f(x) = x on −π < x < π is extended with period 2π. Find the coefficient b3 of sin 3x, correct to 3 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The sine coefficient for n = 3.
  2. Which idea applies. For an odd function, bn = (2/π)∫0π x sin nx dx = 2(−1)n+1/n.
  3. Work it out. b3 = 2/3 ≈ 0.667.

import math
N = 200000; h = 2 * math.pi / N

Exam-level 3: Series at a jump (MCQ, 1 mark)

The Fourier series of f(x) = 0 for −π < x < 0 and f(x) = 1 for 0 < x < π converges at x = 0 to (A) 0 (B) 1/2 (C) 1 (D) it diverges

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The value of the series at a jump.
  2. Which idea applies. Dirichlet's theorem: at a jump the series converges to the average of the left and right limits.
  3. Work it out. (0 + 1)/2 = 1/2.
  4. Check. Every sine term vanishes at 0, leaving only a0/2 = 1/2.

Answer: (B).

Summary

Fourier series
A way to write a periodic function as a sum of sine and cosine waves.
Period
The length of one full repeat of a function.
Harmonic
A wave whose frequency is a whole-number multiple of the basic frequency.
a0, an, bn
The coefficients of the constant term, cosine terms and sine terms.
Orthogonality
Sines and cosines with different frequencies integrate to zero over a period, which isolates each coefficient.
Even function
A function with f(-x) = f(x). All sine coefficients are zero.
Odd function
A function with f(-x) = -f(x). All cosine coefficients, including a0, are zero.
Half-range series
A series for a function on (0, L), using an odd extension (sine) or an even extension (cosine).
Fundamental angular frequency
ω0 = 2π/T for a signal of period T.
Dirichlet conditions
Mild conditions that guarantee convergence of the series.
Jump rule
At a jump, the series converges to the average of the left and right limits.
Gibbs phenomenon
An overshoot near a jump that does not vanish as more terms are added.
Partial sum
The sum of the first N terms of a series.
Average value
a0/2 is the average of f over one period.

Remember these

  1. Check for even or odd symmetry before integrating. It tells you which coefficients vanish.
  2. The factor is 1/π for period 2π and 2/T for period T. The half-range factor is 2/L.
  3. At a jump, the series gives the average of the two limits, not either limit.
  4. The sawtooth x on (-π, π) has bn = 2(-1)n+1/n and no cosine terms.
  5. The square wave has bn = 4/(nπ) for odd n and 0 for even n.
  6. Gibbs overshoot does not disappear as more terms are added.
