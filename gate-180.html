Gyroscope

A gyroscope is a fast spinning rotor. It resists any change in the direction of its spin axis, and when that axis is turned it pushes back with a gyroscopic couple. This page explains where the couple comes from, which way it points, and how to calculate it for ships, aircraft and rotating machinery.

The idea in plain words

Hold a bicycle wheel by the ends of its axle and ask a friend to spin it fast. Now try to tilt the axle sideways. You feel a strong twisting push that turns your hands the other way. The spinning wheel is resisting the tilt. This is the gyroscopic effect.

A spinning top shows the same thing. It does not fall over at once. Instead its axis slowly traces a circle, a motion called precession. The spinning rotor keeps its direction, and it fights every attempt to change that direction.

Engineers use this effect in ships, aircraft and turbines. A rotor that spins fast inside a moving vehicle creates forces on its bearings and frame whenever the vehicle turns, rolls or pitches. Knowing how big that push is, and which way it acts, is an important design task. In GATE, the topic sits in the Robotics and Automation (RA) paper, where rotating parts in robots and vehicles are analysed with the same ideas.

Key terms

Each term has a plain meaning first, then a precise definition.

Gyroscope
A rotating body whose spin axis can change direction. Plain meaning: a fast spinning wheel or disc.
Spin
The rotation of the rotor about its own axis. Plain meaning: how the rotor turns on its own.
Spin axis
The line through the centre of the rotor along which the spin vector points.
Spin speed (ωs)
The angular speed of the rotor about its spin axis, in rad/s.
Precession
The slow turning of the spin axis about a second axis, perpendicular to the spin axis.
Precession speed (Ω)
The angular speed at which the spin axis turns about the precession axis, in rad/s.
Moment of inertia (I)
How hard it is to change the rotation of a body, here about the spin axis, in kg m². Plain meaning: the rotational mass.
Angular momentum (H)
The rotational quantity of a spinning body. For a rotor spinning about its axis, H = I ωs.
Couple
A turning effect made by two equal and opposite forces, measured in N m. Plain meaning: a twisting push.
Gyroscopic couple (C)
The couple needed to make the spin axis precess. C = I ωs Ω.
Vector
A quantity with size and direction, such as a force or an angular velocity.
Cross product (×)
A way to multiply two vectors to get a third vector at right angles to both. Its size is the product of sizes times the sine of the angle between them.
Right-hand rule
Curl the fingers of the right hand in the direction of rotation; the thumb points along the rotation vector.
Nutation
A small wobble of the spin axis that can happen during precession. Plain meaning: a small nod.

TrapThe gyroscopic couple is not the force that makes the rotor precess. It is the couple that the rotor needs in order to precess. The reaction of that couple acts on the supports, so the bearings and frame feel it.

Spin, precession and angular momentum

Why a spinning rotor resists change

Newton's law for turning says that a couple changes the angular momentum of a body. A stationary object has no spin, so a small couple changes it easily. A rotor with a large spin speed has a large angular momentum, and it takes a much larger couple to change its direction. This is why a fast rotor feels so stiff.

H = I ωs

Here H is the angular momentum in kg m²/s, I is the moment of inertia about the spin axis, and ωs is the spin speed in rad/s. The angular momentum is a vector. It points along the spin axis, in the direction given by the right-hand rule.

Why precession needs a couple

Now let the spin axis turn at precession speed Ω. The angular momentum vector H keeps its size, but its direction turns. A vector that changes direction is changing, so there must be a rate of change. Think of a stone on a string: the string keeps pulling the stone round the circle, so the stone's velocity direction keeps changing. Here the couple keeps the spin axis turning.

Over a short time Δt, the spin axis turns through the small angle Ω Δt. The tip of the H vector moves by about H × Ω Δt, because the vector length stays equal to H. So the rate of change of H has size

|dH/dt| = H Ω = I ωs Ω

This rate of change of angular momentum is exactly the couple that must be applied. We write it as a vector equation: C = dH/dt = Ω × H, where Ω points along the precession axis, and the cross product gives the right size and direction.

Key ideaPrecession of a spinning rotor requires a couple equal to the rate of change of its angular momentum. Both the spin and the precession must be present for this couple to exist.

Worked example: the size of the change

A rotor has I = 0.5 kg m² and spins at 3000 rpm. Its axis precesses at Ω = 0.1 rad/s. Find the angular momentum and the rate at which it changes direction.

Step 1. Spin speed: ωs = 2π × 3000/60 = 314.16 rad/s.

Step 2. Angular momentum: H = 0.5 × 314.16 = 157.08 kg m²/s.

Step 3. Rate of change: H Ω = 157.08 × 0.1 = 15.71 N m. This is the size of the couple.

Step 4. Check the units: H is in kg m²/s and Ω is in 1/s, so H Ω is in kg m²/s², which is N m, the unit of a couple. Good.

Self-check: if the rotor is not spinning (ωs = 0), what is the gyroscopic couple?

Zero. The gyroscopic couple needs both spin and precession. Without spin, there is no angular momentum to turn.

Self-check: if the spin speed is doubled and the precession speed is kept the same, how does the couple change?

It doubles, because C = I ωs Ω is proportional to the spin speed.

Direction of the gyroscopic couple

Using the right-hand rule

Size is only half of the answer. In exam questions, the direction of the couple decides whether a ship bows up or down, or whether a bearing is pushed left or right. The direction follows from the cross product of the spin vector and the precession vector:

C = I (ωs × Ω)

To find the direction, point the fingers of your right hand along the spin vector, then curl them towards the precession vector. Your thumb points along the couple vector. The couple vector is at right angles to both the spin axis and the precession axis.

        precession axis (Omega), vertical, upward

              ^  Omega
              |
              |
              |_________>  spin axis (omega_s), horizontal
             /
            /
           v

   couple C = I (omega_s x Omega) points along the thumb.
   Here omega_s points forward, Omega points up, so C points
   to the side (the thumb of the right hand, fingers forward
   then curling up).

The couple on the rotor and on the frame

The gyroscopic couple C is the couple that the supports must apply to the rotor to make its axis precess. By Newton's third law, the rotor pushes back on the frame with an equal and opposite couple, -C. This reaction is what the vehicle, ship or bearing feels. When a question asks for "the gyroscopic couple acting on the ship", it usually means the reaction on the frame.

TrapAlways say which body you are talking about: the couple the supports apply to the rotor, or the reaction the rotor applies to the frame. They are equal and opposite. Many wrong answers come from mixing up the two signs.

Worked example with vectors

Let the spin vector be along the x axis with size 50 rad/s, and the precession vector along the z axis with size 2 rad/s. The rotor has I = 0.5 kg m². Find the couple vector.

Step 1. Write the vectors: ωs = (50, 0, 0) and Ω = (0, 0, 2).

Step 2. Cross product: (aybz - azby, azbx - axbz, axby - aybx) = (0 × 2 - 0 × 0, 0 × 0 - 50 × 2, 50 × 0 - 0 × 0) = (0, -100, 0).

Step 3. Multiply by I: C = 0.5 × (0, -100, 0) = (0, -50, 0) N m. The couple points along the negative y axis.

def cross(a, b):
    return (a[1] * b[2] - a[2] * b[1],
            a[2] * b[0] - a[0] * b[2],
            a[0] * b[1] - a[1] * b[0])

I = 0.5
ws = (50.0, 0.0, 0.0)
Om = (0.0, 0.0, 2.0)
C = tuple(I * c for c in cross(ws, Om))
print("couple C =", C, "N m")
couple C = (0.0, -50.0, 0.0) N m
Self-check: if the spin and the precession vectors point in the same direction, what is the couple?

Zero. The cross product of two parallel vectors is zero, so there is no gyroscopic couple.

Self-check: is the gyroscopic couple ever along the spin axis?

No. The cross product is always at right angles to both input vectors, so it cannot lie along the spin axis when the precession is perpendicular to it.

The gyroscopic couple C = I ω Ω

The standard form

In most GATE questions, the spin axis and the precession axis are perpendicular. Then the cross product has size equal to the product of the two sizes, and the formula is

C = I ωs Ω

Read each symbol: C is the couple in N m, I is the moment of inertia about the spin axis in kg m², ωs is the spin speed in rad/s, and Ω is the precession speed in rad/s. The direction is found by the right-hand rule above.

Converting rpm to rad/s

Gyroscope questions often give the spin in rpm but the formula needs rad/s. Use

ωs = 2π N / 60 rad/s, where N is in rpm

TipThe precession speed is often given in rad/s, or as a time for one full turn. If the axis turns through one full circle in T seconds, then Ω = 2π/T.

Worked example: a rotor on a ship

A turbine rotor on a ship has I = 1200 kg m² and spins at 1500 rpm. The ship turns in a circle so that the rotor's axis precesses at Ω = 0.05 rad/s. Find the gyroscopic couple.

Step 1. Spin speed: ωs = 2π × 1500/60 = 157.08 rad/s.

Step 2. Couple: C = I ωs Ω = 1200 × 157.08 × 0.05.

Step 3. Multiply: 1200 × 157.08 = 188496; then × 0.05 = 9424.8 N m, about 9.4 kN m.

Step 4. Check: a large rotor at high speed gives a large couple, and the couple is in kN m, which matches the size expected for a ship turbine.

import math

I = 1200.0
N = 1500.0
Omega = 0.05
ws = 2 * math.pi * N / 60
C = I * ws * Omega
print(f"spin = {ws:.2f} rad/s")
print(f"couple = {C:.1f} N m")
spin = 157.08 rad/s
couple = 9424.8 N m
Self-check: the precession rate doubles. What happens to the couple?

It doubles, because C is proportional to Ω.

Self-check: which two quantities must be in rad/s before you multiply them?

The spin speed ωs and the precession speed Ω must both be in rad/s, so that the product has units of N m when I is in kg m².

Effects on ships, aircraft and rotating machinery

Ships

A ship's turbine rotor, aligned along the ship, has its spin axis fixed to the hull. When the ship turns, the hull makes the spin axis precess about the vertical axis. The gyroscopic couple then acts on the bearings and on the hull, and it tends to tilt the bow up or down. Rolling and pitching of the ship can also make the axis precess about other axes, and each case has its own couple. In exam questions, you are usually asked for the size of the couple, which you find from C = I ωs Ω.

Aircraft

An aircraft engine's rotating compressor and turbine parts are spinning fast. When the aircraft pitches or yaws, the axis of these rotors precesses. The couple then loads the engine mounts. The same formula applies, with the axis of the engine as the spin axis and the aircraft's turning rate as the precession.

Rotating machinery and stability

A disc fixed at the end of a shaft, such as a grinding wheel or a turbine rotor, is a gyroscope. When the shaft whirls around its bearings, the disc's axis precesses. The gyroscopic couple then adds to the bending of the shaft, which changes the bearing reactions and can affect the speed at which the shaft vibrates strongly. This link to vibration is developed on the page about the critical speeds of shafts.

Key ideaIn every application, the same formula C = I ωs Ω gives the size of the couple. What changes is which axis precesses and which supports take the reaction.

Worked example: a disc on a shaft

A disc with I = 0.5 kg m² spins at 3000 rpm inside a machine. The shaft bends so the disc's axis precesses at Ω = 0.1 rad/s. Find the couple on the bearings.

Step 1. Spin in rad/s: 314.16 rad/s.

Step 2. Couple: 0.5 × 314.16 × 0.1 = 15.71 N m.

Step 3. This matches the value of 15.71 N m found earlier. The bearings take the reaction of this couple, and it adds to the bending of the shaft.

Self-check: a disc on an overhung shaft has a larger precession rate than a disc at midspan. Which gives a larger couple?

The larger precession rate, since C is proportional to Ω. The position of the disc changes the precession rate, which is why overhung discs need careful design.

Self-check: which way does a fast rotor resist a sudden tilt?

It resists by producing a couple in the direction that keeps the spin axis in its original direction. The couple opposes the change, so it looks like a stiff spring for tilts.

Working through gyroscope problems

Most GATE numerical questions follow the same four steps. Use them every time.

  1. Identify the spin. Find the spin speed, convert it to rad/s, and note the moment of inertia I about the spin axis.
  2. Identify the precession. Find the rate at which the spin axis turns, in rad/s. If the question gives a time for one full turn, use Ω = 2π/T.
  3. Check that the axes are perpendicular. If they are, use C = I ωs Ω. If they are parallel, the couple is zero.
  4. Decide on the direction and the body. Use the right-hand rule. State whether the couple acts on the rotor or on the frame.

Worked example: the time for one turn

A rotor has I = 0.04 kg m² and spins at 1000 rad/s. Its spin axis makes one full turn in 2 seconds. Find the precession speed and the couple.

Step 1. Precession speed: Ω = 2π/2 = 3.1416 rad/s.

Step 2. Couple: C = 0.04 × 1000 × 3.1416 = 125.66 N m.

Step 3. Check: the spin is very fast compared with the precession, so the couple is large, which is the reason fast rotors are hard to tilt.

import math

I, ws, T = 0.04, 1000.0, 2.0
Omega = 2 * math.pi / T
C = I * ws * Omega
print(f"Omega = {Omega:.4f} rad/s")
print(f"couple = {C:.2f} N m")
Omega = 3.1416 rad/s
couple = 125.66 N m

GATE loves thisA common question gives the maximum couple a bearing can take and asks for the largest precession rate. Rearrange the formula: Ω = C/(I ωs).

Self-check: with the same couple limit, why does a faster spin allow a slower maximum precession?

Because Ω = C/(I ωs). A faster spin makes the denominator bigger, so the allowed precession is smaller.

Self-check: is the gyroscopic couple the same as the centrifugal force?

No. Centrifugal force acts outward on each part of a rotating body, while the gyroscopic couple is a turning effect that appears when the spin axis is turned.

Solved GATE-style problems

These are original GATE-style problems for practice, not real past paper questions. Work each one on paper before reading the answer.

Couple from a small rotor

A rotor has moment of inertia 0.2 kg m² about its spin axis. It spins at 1200 rpm, and its axis precesses at 0.05 rad/s, with the two axes perpendicular. Find the gyroscopic couple in N m.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The couple C in N m.
  2. Which idea applies. C = I ωs Ω, with ωs in rad/s.
  3. Work it out. ωs = 2π × 1200/60 = 125.66 rad/s. C = 0.2 × 125.66 × 0.05 = 1.257 N m.
  4. Check. A small rotor and a slow precession give a small couple, about 1.3 N m. This is reasonable.
import math

I, N, Omega = 0.2, 1200.0, 0.05
ws = 2 * math.pi * N / 60
print(f"couple = {I * ws * Omega:.2f} N m")
couple = 1.26 N m

Answer: about 1.26 N m.

Rotor with a given spin speed

A rotor spins at 300 rad/s with moment of inertia 0.01 kg m². Its spin axis precesses at 2 rad/s, perpendicular to the spin axis. Find the gyroscopic couple in N m.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. C in N m.
  2. Which idea applies. C = I ωs Ω. The spin is already in rad/s, so no conversion is needed.
  3. Work it out. C = 0.01 × 300 × 2 = 6 N m.
  4. Check. The units are kg m² × rad/s × rad/s = N m, which is a couple.

Answer: 6 N m.

Which statements are true?

Consider these statements about gyroscopes.

(A) The gyroscopic couple is proportional to the spin speed and to the precession speed.
(B) If the spin and precession axes are parallel, the gyroscopic couple is at its maximum.
(C) For the same precession speed, a faster spin gives a larger couple.
(D) The gyroscopic couple acts along the spin axis.

Select all that apply.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements are true (multiple selection).
  2. Which idea applies. C = I ωs Ω for perpendicular axes, and the cross product is zero for parallel vectors.
  3. Work it out. (A) is true. (B) is false, because parallel axes give zero couple. (C) is true, because C is proportional to ωs. (D) is false, because the couple is perpendicular to both axes.
  4. Check. Take ωs = 100, Ω = 1, I = 1: C = 100. If ωs doubles, C doubles, which confirms (C).

Answer: (A) and (C).

Ship turbine couple

A turbine rotor of a ship has I = 800 kg m² and spins at 1500 rpm. The ship turns so that the rotor's axis precesses at 0.04 rad/s. Find the gyroscopic couple in N m (numerical answer).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. C in N m.
  2. Which idea applies. Convert the spin to rad/s, then C = I ωs Ω.
  3. Work it out. ωs = 157.08 rad/s. C = 800 × 157.08 × 0.04 = 5026.5 N m.
  4. Check. The precession is smaller than in the worked example, and the moment of inertia is smaller, so the couple is smaller than 9425 N m. 5027 is smaller, which is consistent.
import math

I, N, Omega = 800.0, 1500.0, 0.04
ws = 2 * math.pi * N / 60
print(f"couple = {I * ws * Omega:.1f} N m")
couple = 5026.5 N m

Answer: 5026.5 N m (about 5027 N m).

Direction from the right-hand rule

A rotor has I = 0.5 kg m². Its spin vector is 50 rad/s along the positive x axis. Its precession vector is 2 rad/s along the positive z axis. Find the direction of the gyroscopic couple C = I (ωs × Ω), and say which axis it is along (numerical answer for the size in N m is 50).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The axis and sign of the couple, and its size.
  2. Which idea applies. The right-hand rule and the cross product.
  3. Work it out. x cross z = -y, so the couple points along the negative y axis. The size is 0.5 × 50 × 2 = 50 N m.
  4. Check. Point the right-hand fingers along x, curl them towards z. The thumb points down the negative y axis.

Answer: 50 N m along the negative y axis.

Precession through a quarter turn

A rotor has I = 0.5 kg m² and spins at 3000 rpm. Its spin axis turns through 90 degrees (a quarter of a full circle) at a constant rate in 2 seconds. Find the gyroscopic couple in N m (numerical answer).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. C in N m, given a precession angle and time.
  2. Which idea applies. Precession speed Ω = angle/time, then C = I ωs Ω.
  3. Work it out. Ω = (π/2)/2 = 0.7854 rad/s. C = 0.5 × 314.16 × 0.7854 = 123.4 N m.
  4. Check. A full turn in 2 seconds would give Ω = π rad/s, which is four times the quarter-turn value of 0.7854 rad/s. So a full turn would need a couple of about 4 × 123.4 = 494 N m, and the quarter turn needs one quarter of that.
import math

I, N = 0.5, 3000.0
ws = 2 * math.pi * N / 60
Omega = (math.pi / 2) / 2.0
print(f"Omega = {Omega:.4f} rad/s")
print(f"couple = {I * ws * Omega:.1f} N m")
Omega = 0.7854 rad/s
couple = 123.4 N m

Answer: about 123.4 N m.

Largest precession under a bearing limit

A rotor has I = 0.04 kg m² and spins at 1000 rad/s. The bearings can take a gyroscopic couple of at most 4 N m. Find the largest precession speed the axis can have, in rad/s and in rpm (numerical answer, rad/s).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The maximum Ω in rad/s and rpm.
  2. Which idea applies. Rearrange C = I ωs Ω to get Ω = C/(I ωs).
  3. Work it out. Ω = 4/(0.04 × 1000) = 4/40 = 0.1 rad/s. In rpm: 0.1 × 60/(2π) = 0.955 rpm.
  4. Check. Multiply back: 0.04 × 1000 × 0.1 = 4 N m, which equals the bearing limit.
import math

I, ws, C_max = 0.04, 1000.0, 4.0
Omega = C_max / (I * ws)
print(f"Omega max = {Omega:.2f} rad/s")
print(f"Omega max = {Omega * 60 / (2 * math.pi):.3f} rpm")
Omega max = 0.10 rad/s
Omega max = 0.955 rpm

Answer: 0.1 rad/s, which is about 0.955 rpm.

Exam-level practice

Exam-level 1: Gyroscopic couple (NAT, 2 marks)

A rotor of mass 20 kg and radius of gyration 0.25 m spins at 3000 rpm. It precesses at 1 rad/s. Find the gyroscopic couple in N·m, correct to 1 decimal place.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. C = Iωωp.
  2. Which idea applies. I = mk².
  3. Work it out. I = 1.25. ω = 314.16. C = 392.7 N·m.
  4. Check. The code computes it.
import math
print("%.1f" % (20 * 0.25 ** 2 * 3000 * 2 * math.pi / 60 * 1))

392.7

Answer: about 392.7 N·m.

Exam-level 2: Ship turning (MCQ, 2 marks)

A ship's rotor spins clockwise when viewed from the stern. The ship turns left (to port). The gyroscopic effect makes the (A) bow rise (B) bow dip (C) ship roll to starboard (D) no effect.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The reaction to turning.
  2. Which idea applies. Spin vector points forward (right hand rule, clockwise from the stern). Precession vector for a left turn points up. The active couple is precession × spin, and the reaction is opposite.
  3. Work it out. Up × forward points to port, so the active couple's axis points to port. The reaction couple's axis points to starboard, which lifts the bow.
  4. Check. This matches the standard result: clockwise rotor viewed from aft, left turn, bow rises.

Answer: (A).

Exam-level 3: Precession speed (NAT, 2 marks)

A disc (I = 0.02 kg·m²) spins at 200 rad/s on an axle supported at one end 0.1 m from its centre. The disc mass is 1 kg. Take g = 9.81. Find the precession rate in rad/s, correct to 3 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. ωp = mgL/(Iω).
  2. Which idea applies. Gravity gives the couple that drives precession.
  3. Work it out. 9.81 · 0.1/(0.02 · 200) = 0.245 rad/s.
  4. Check. The code computes it.
print("%.3f" % (1 * 9.81 * 0.1 / (0.02 * 200)))

0.245

Answer: about 0.245 rad/s.

Summary

Gyroscope
A fast spinning rotor whose axis resists changes in direction.
Spin
Rotation of the rotor about its own axis, at speed ωs.
Precession
Slow turning of the spin axis about a perpendicular axis, at rate Ω.
Moment of inertia I
Rotational mass about the spin axis, in kg m².
Angular momentum
H = I ωs, a vector along the spin axis.
Gyroscopic couple
C = I ωs Ω, the couple needed to precess the axis.
Cross product
A vector product at right angles to both inputs, giving the couple direction.
Right-hand rule
Fingers along spin, curl towards precession, thumb along the couple.
Reaction couple
The equal and opposite couple that the rotor applies to the frame, -C.
Zero couple
When the spin and precession vectors are parallel, C is zero.
Spin in rad/s
ωs = 2πN/60, with N in rpm.
Precession from time
Ω = 2π/T, when the axis makes one full turn in time T.
Bearing limit
The largest precession is Ω = C/(I ωs).
Ships and aircraft
Turning and rolling of a vehicle makes the spinning rotor precess and load its mounts.
Nutation
A small wobble that can ride on top of steady precession.

Remember these

  • A gyroscopic couple needs both spin and precession.
  • Use C = I ωs Ω when the axes are perpendicular, with both speeds in rad/s.
  • If the spin and precession are parallel, the couple is zero.
  • The couple direction is given by the right-hand rule.
  • The rotor pushes back on its frame with an equal and opposite couple.
  • A faster spin allows only a slower precession under the same bearing limit.
