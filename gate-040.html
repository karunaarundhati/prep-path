Conditional PDF

A conditional PDF describes how one continuous variable behaves once you know the value of another. You get it by dividing a joint density by a marginal density, and it lets you compute conditional probabilities, expectations and variances.

The idea in plain words

Suppose you measure two things about the same object: its height X and its weight Y. The pair (X, Y) has a joint distribution. It tells you how likely each combination is. Now suppose someone tells you the weight is 70 kilograms. Does that change your belief about the height? Usually it does, because heavier people tend to be taller. The new belief about the height, given the weight, is a conditional distribution.

Everyday picture: a weather forecaster knows the chance of rain tomorrow and the chance of rain given that the morning is cloudy. The second number is a conditional probability. For continuous variables, the same idea holds, but the probabilities come from densities and areas instead of simple fractions.

The formula for the conditional density has a simple shape. It is the joint density divided by the density of the variable you are conditioning on. You will learn this in three steps: find the joint density, find the marginal density by integrating out the other variable, and then divide.

Why does this matter for GATE? The Data Science and AI (DA) paper uses conditional densities in probability, in Bayesian reasoning and in models where one variable depends on another. The Robotics and Automation (RA) paper uses them for sensor fusion and for estimating a hidden state from a noisy measurement. GATE questions usually give a joint density on a region, then ask for a marginal, a conditional or an independence check.

Key terms

Each term is given first in plain words, then in precise form.

Joint PDF f(x, y)
Plain meaning: a surface whose volume gives the probability of a pair of values. Precise definition: a function f(x, y) ≥ 0 whose total volume is ∫∫ f(x, y) dx dy = 1, with P((X, Y) in a region) equal to the volume over that region.
Marginal PDF fX(x)
Plain meaning: the density of X alone, ignoring Y. Precise definition: fX(x) = ∫ f(x, y) dy, integrated over all y.
Marginal PDF fY(y)
Plain meaning: the density of Y alone. Precise definition: fY(y) = ∫ f(x, y) dx, integrated over all x.
Conditional PDF f(x | y)
Plain meaning: the density of X once Y = y is known. Precise definition: f(x | y) = f(x, y) / fY(y), for values y where fY(y) > 0.
Integrating out
Plain meaning: adding up over one variable to leave the other. Precise definition: forming a marginal by integrating the joint density over the other variable.
Conditional expectation E[X | Y = y]
Plain meaning: the average of X, once Y is known to equal y. Precise definition: ∫ x f(x | y) dx.
Conditional variance Var(X | Y = y)
Plain meaning: the spread of X, once Y = y. Precise definition: E[X² | Y = y] - (E[X | Y = y])².
Independence
Plain meaning: knowing one variable tells you nothing about the other. Precise definition: X and Y are independent when f(x, y) = fX(x) fY(y) for all x and y.

Concept 1: Joint and marginal PDFs

The joint density

A joint PDF describes two continuous variables together. It is a surface over a region in the plane. The probability that the pair lies in some region is the volume under the surface above that region. Just as in one dimension, a joint density must be non-negative and must have total volume 1:

f(x, y) ≥ 0, and ∫ ∫ f(x, y) dx dy = 1 over the whole region

Each symbol means: f(x, y) is the density at the point (x, y), and the double integral adds up thin columns over the whole region where f is not zero.

Integrating out to get a marginal

To find the density of X alone, we ignore Y by integrating it out. For each fixed x, we add up f(x, y) over all possible y:

fX(x) = ∫ f(x, y) dy

The result is a function of x only. Think of slicing the surface at a fixed x and measuring the area of the slice. The marginal is the total weight of that slice. The same logic gives fY(y) by integrating over x.

Worked example: the density x + y

Let f(x, y) = x + y on the unit square 0 ≤ x ≤ 1 and 0 ≤ y ≤ 1, and zero elsewhere.

  1. Check the total volume. The integral of x + y over the square is ∫ from 0 to 1 of (1/2 + y) dy, which equals 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. So it is a valid joint PDF.
  2. Find the marginal of X. fX(x) = ∫ from 0 to 1 of (x + y) dy = [xy + y²/2] from 0 to 1 = x + 1/2.
  3. By the same method, fY(y) = y + 1/2.
  4. Check. The marginal fX must also integrate to 1: ∫ from 0 to 1 of (x + 1/2) dx = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. Good.

Key ideaA marginal is found by integrating the joint density over the other variable. Always check that the marginal integrates to 1, which catches most errors.

TrapDo not confuse the limits. When you integrate out y, the limits are the y-range for the given x. For a triangular region, the limits depend on x, and you must write them carefully.

Self-check: for f(x, y) = 4xy on the unit square, what is fX(x)?

fX(x) = ∫ from 0 to 1 of 4xy dy = 4x × [y²/2] from 0 to 1 = 4x × 1/2 = 2x.

Self-check: why must a marginal PDF integrate to 1?

Because it is a density for one variable, and that variable must take some value. The total probability of the marginal is therefore 1.

Concept 2: The conditional PDF f(x | y)

The definition

f(x | y) = f(x, y) / fY(y)

Each symbol means:

  • f(x, y) is the joint density, the weight of the pair (x, y).
  • fY(y) is the marginal density of Y at the value we are given. It is the total weight of that slice.
  • f(x | y) is the joint weight divided by the slice total, so it is the shape of X inside that slice.

Dividing by the slice total rescales the slice so that its area becomes 1. This is exactly the conditional probability idea from discrete probability, with the slice playing the role of "B has happened".

Why it integrates to 1

For fixed y, the integral of f(x | y) over x is ∫ f(x, y) dx / fY(y) = fY(y) / fY(y) = 1. So the conditional density is a valid density in x for every y where fY(y) > 0. Always check this, because it confirms your work.

Worked example: the density x + y

Using f(x, y) = x + y on the unit square, find the conditional density of X given Y = 1/2.

  1. Joint density at y = 1/2. f(x, 1/2) = x + 1/2.
  2. Marginal at y = 1/2. fY(1/2) = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1.
  3. Divide. f(x | 1/2) = (x + 1/2) / 1 = x + 1/2 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1.
  4. Check. ∫ from 0 to 1 of (x + 1/2) dx = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. The conditional density is valid.

Notice the conditional density does not depend on y in the same way as the joint. For a different y, say y = 0, we get f(x | 0) = x/(1/2) = 2x. The shape of X changes as Y changes, which is the mark of dependence.

TipWhen a question asks for the conditional density, first write the joint density with y fixed, then compute the marginal of Y at that same y, then divide. Three short lines usually finish the problem.

TrapThe conditional density is only defined where the marginal is positive. Outside the support of Y, the conditioning event has zero probability, and f(x | y) is not meaningful there.

Self-check: f(x, y) = 2 on 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1, and fY(y) = 2(1 - y). What is f(x | y)?

f(x | y) = 2 / (2(1 - y)) = 1/(1 - y) for y ≤ x ≤ 1. This is uniform on [y, 1].

Self-check: does f(x | y) integrate to 1 over x?

Yes. For fixed y, the integral of f(x, y)/fY(y) over x equals fY(y)/fY(y) = 1.

Concept 3: Conditional probabilities and expectations

Probabilities from the conditional density

Once you have f(x | y), a conditional probability is an ordinary integral of that density:

P(a ≤ X ≤ b | Y = y) = ∫ from a to b of f(x | y) dx

Read this as: the conditional density is now the density of X, so probabilities come from its area.

Worked example: the triangle

Let f(x, y) = 2 on the region 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1. Find P(X ≤ 1/2 | Y = 1/4).

  1. Marginal of Y. For fixed y, x runs from y to 1, so fY(y) = ∫ from y to 1 of 2 dx = 2(1 - y).
  2. Conditional density. f(x | y) = 2 / (2(1 - y)) = 1/(1 - y), for y ≤ x ≤ 1. At y = 1/4 this is 1/(3/4) = 4/3 on the interval [1/4, 1].
  3. Probability. P(X ≤ 1/2 | Y = 1/4) = ∫ from 1/4 to 1/2 of (4/3) dx = (4/3)(1/2 - 1/4) = (4/3)(1/4) = 1/3.
  4. Check. The interval [1/4, 1/2] has length 1/4, and the whole interval [1/4, 1] has length 3/4. The ratio (1/4)/(3/4) = 1/3, which agrees with the integral.

Conditional expectation

The conditional expectation is the mean of X inside the slice:

E[X | Y = y] = ∫ x f(x | y) dx

Each symbol means: x is the value, f(x | y) is the conditional weight, and the integral averages x over the slice.

Worked example: conditional mean on the triangle

Using the same triangle, the conditional density at y = 1/2 is uniform on [1/2, 1] with density 2. Find E[X | Y = 1/2].

  1. Write the conditional density. f(x | 1/2) = 1/(1 - 1/2) = 2 on [1/2, 1].
  2. Integrate x times the density. ∫ from 1/2 to 1 of 2x dx = [x²] from 1/2 to 1 = 1 - 1/4 = 3/4.
  3. Check. For a uniform distribution on [a, b], the mean is (a + b)/2 = (1/2 + 1)/2 = 3/4. This matches.
n = 100000
h = 1 / n
mids = [(i + 0.5) * h for i in range(n)]

# joint density 2 on 0 <= y <= x <= 1; given Y = 0.5, X is uniform on [0.5, 1] with density 2
cond = [x for x in mids if x >= 0.5]
area = sum(2 * h for x in cond)
mean = sum(x * 2 * h for x in cond)

print(f"area under conditional density = {area:.4f}")
print(f"E[X | Y = 0.5] = {mean:.4f}")
area under conditional density = 1.0000
E[X | Y = 0.5] = 0.7500

Conditional variance

The conditional variance uses the same shortcut as before, applied inside the slice:

Var(X | Y = y) = E[X² | Y = y] - (E[X | Y = y])²

For the triangle at y = 1/2, the conditional distribution is uniform on [1/2, 1], so Var = (1 - 1/2)²/12 = (1/4)/12 = 1/48, which is about 0.0208.

GATE loves thisMany questions give a joint density on a triangle and ask for a conditional mean. If the conditional turns out uniform, use the uniform formulas (a + b)/2 and (b - a)²/12 instead of integrating. This saves time and reduces mistakes.

TrapE[X | Y = y] is a function of y, not a number, when y is unknown. Only at a fixed value of y does it become a number. Read the question carefully to see whether the value is given.

Self-check: for the triangle with y = 0, what is the conditional density of X?

f(x | 0) = 1/(1 - 0) = 1 on [0, 1], which is uniform on [0, 1].

Self-check: a conditional density is uniform on [2, 6]. What is its mean?

(2 + 6)/2 = 4.

Concept 4: Independence of continuous variables

The test

Two variables are independent when knowing one does not change the distribution of the other. For continuous variables, the test is a factorisation:

X and Y are independent if and only if f(x, y) = fX(x) × fY(y) for all x and y

If the joint density can be written as a product of a function of x and a function of y, the variables are independent, and the factors are the marginals. This is the continuous version of the discrete rule P(A and B) = P(A) P(B).

Equivalent check through conditionals

If X and Y are independent, then f(x | y) = fX(x) for every y. The conditional density does not depend on the conditioning value. So another way to test independence is to see whether the conditional shape changes with y.

Worked example: two candidates

  1. Candidate 1: f(x, y) = 4xy on the unit square. The marginals are fX(x) = 2x and fY(y) = 2y. Their product is 4xy, which equals the joint density. So X and Y are independent.
  2. Candidate 2: f(x, y) = x + y on the unit square. The marginals are x + 1/2 and y + 1/2. Their product is (x + 1/2)(y + 1/2), which is not equal to x + y. For example, at x = y = 0 the joint is 0, while the product is 1/4. So X and Y are not independent.

The Python block below checks the factorisation at a few points, using exact fractions.

from fractions import Fraction as F

def joint_a(x, y):          # f(x, y) = 4xy
    return 4 * x * y

def joint_b(x, y):          # f(x, y) = x + y
    return x + y

def fx_a(x):  return 2 * x          # marginal of X for joint_a
def fy_a(y):  return 2 * y          # marginal of Y for joint_a
def fx_b(x):  return x + F(1, 2)    # marginal of X for joint_b
def fy_b(y):  return y + F(1, 2)    # marginal of Y for joint_b

pts = [(F(1, 4), F(1, 2)), (F(0), F(0)), (F(1), F(1, 3))]
print("candidate a independent:", all(joint_a(x, y) == fx_a(x) * fy_a(y) for x, y in pts))
print("candidate b independent:", all(joint_b(x, y) == fx_b(x) * fy_b(y) for x, y in pts))
candidate a independent: True
candidate b independent: False

The Python result matches the hand calculation. Candidate a factorises, and candidate b does not.

Key ideaIndependence means the joint density splits into a product of two one-variable functions. If it does not split, the variables are dependent, however close the shapes look.

TrapA non-zero joint density on a rectangle does not prove independence, and a triangular region always breaks it. If the support is a triangle, the range of X depends on y, so the variables cannot be independent.

Self-check: f(x, y) = 2 on the triangle 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1. Are X and Y independent?

No. For a given y, x can only range from y to 1, so the range of X depends on y. Independent variables must have a range that does not depend on the other variable.

Self-check: if X and Y are independent, what is f(x | y)?

It equals fX(x) for every y, because the conditioning on Y does not change the distribution of X.

Solved GATE-style problems

These problems are original GATE-style questions written for practice. They are not past paper questions. Work each one on paper first, then compare with the solution.

Independence of a product density

A joint density is f(x, y) = 4xy for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1 and 0 ≤ y ≤ 1, and zero otherwise. Are X and Y independent? Answer yes or no.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Whether f factorises into a function of x times a function of y.
  2. Which idea applies. Compute the marginals and compare their product with the joint density.
  3. Work it out. fX(x) = ∫ from 0 to 1 of 4xy dy = 2x. fY(y) = ∫ from 0 to 1 of 4xy dx = 2y. Their product is 4xy, which equals the joint density.
  4. Check. The support is a rectangle, so the range of X does not depend on y. Both conditions for independence hold.

Answer: Yes

Select all true statements about x + y

A joint density is f(x, y) = x + y on the unit square, zero elsewhere. Select all that apply.

(A) f is a valid joint PDF.
(B) The marginal of X is fX(x) = x + 1/2.
(C) X and Y are independent.
(D) The conditional density is f(x | y) = (x + y)/(y + 1/2).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Tick each true statement.
  2. Which idea applies. Joint validity, integrating out, the conditional formula and the independence test.
  3. Work it out. Option A: the total volume is 1/2 + 1/2 = 1, so A is true. Option B: integrating out y gives x + 1/2, so B is true. Option C: f(0, 0) = 0, but fX(0) fY(0) = 1/4, so C is false. Option D: f(x | y) = (x + y)/(y + 1/2), which matches the definition, so D is true.
  4. Check. Option C is false because the joint density does not factorise. This matches the trap discussed in the concept.

Answer: (A), (B) and (D) are true. Option (C) is false.

Conditional probability on a triangle

A joint density is f(x, y) = 2 on the region 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1, and zero elsewhere. Given Y = 0.5, find the probability that X ≥ 0.75. Give the answer as a decimal.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. P(X ≥ 0.75 | Y = 0.5).
  2. Which idea applies. Find fY(0.5), form f(x | 0.5), and integrate from 0.75 to 1.
  3. Work it out. fY(y) = 2(1 - y), so fY(0.5) = 1. Then f(x | 0.5) = 2 / 1 = 2 on [0.5, 1]. P(X ≥ 0.75 | Y = 0.5) = ∫ from 0.75 to 1 of 2 dx = 2 × 0.25 = 0.5.
  4. Check. The conditional range is [0.5, 1], of length 0.5, and the part [0.75, 1] has length 0.25. The ratio 0.25/0.5 = 0.5 agrees.

Answer: 0.5

Conditional mean for a power density

NAT question. A joint density is f(x, y) = x + y on the unit square, zero elsewhere. Find E[X | Y = 0] as a decimal, correct to three decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The mean of X in the slice Y = 0.
  2. Which idea applies. Find fY(0), form f(x | 0), then integrate x f(x | 0).
  3. Work it out. fY(0) = 0 + 1/2 = 1/2. So f(x | 0) = (x + 0)/(1/2) = 2x on [0, 1]. Then E[X | Y = 0] = ∫ from 0 to 1 of x × 2x dx = ∫ from 0 to 1 of 2x² dx = [2x³/3] from 0 to 1 = 2/3, which is 0.667 to three decimals.
  4. Check. The conditional density 2x increases towards 1, so the mean should be above 1/2. The value 0.667 is above 0.5, which agrees.

Answer: 0.667

A constant and a conditional probability

A joint density is f(x, y) = c on the region 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1, and zero elsewhere. Find c. Then find P(Y ≤ 1/2 | X = 1). Give the answers as a number and a decimal.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The constant c, then a conditional probability given X = 1.
  2. Which idea applies. The joint density must have volume 1. Then compute fX(x) and the conditional density of Y.
  3. Work it out. Volume: ∫ from 0 to 1 of (∫ from 0 to x of c dy) dx = ∫ from 0 to 1 of cx dx = c/2. Setting this to 1 gives c = 2. Then fX(x) = 2x, so fX(1) = 2 and f(y | 1) = 2/2 = 1 on [0, 1]. So P(Y ≤ 1/2 | X = 1) = ∫ from 0 to 1/2 of 1 dy = 0.5.
  4. Check. Given X = 1, the variable Y is uniform on [0, 1], so the chance of being at most 1/2 is one half. This matches.

Answer: c = 2 and P(Y ≤ 1/2 | X = 1) = 0.5

Joint probability from independent marginals

A joint density is f(x, y) = 6xy² on the unit square, zero elsewhere. Find P(X ≤ 1/2, Y ≤ 1/2). Give the answer as a fraction.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The joint probability that both variables are at most 1/2.
  2. Which idea applies. First test independence, then use the product of the two CDFs.
  3. Work it out. fX(x) = ∫ from 0 to 1 of 6xy² dy = 2x and fY(y) = ∫ from 0 to 1 of 6xy² dx = 3y². Their product is 6xy², which equals f, so X and Y are independent. Then P(X ≤ 1/2) = ∫ from 0 to 1/2 of 2x dx = 1/4, and P(Y ≤ 1/2) = ∫ from 0 to 1/2 of 3y² dy = 1/8. Product: 1/4 × 1/8 = 1/32.
  4. Check. 1/32 is about 0.031, which is small, as expected when both variables must be in the lower half. The product rule is valid because of the factorisation.

Answer: 1/32

Conditional variance on a triangle

NAT question. A joint density is f(x, y) = 2 on the region 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1, and zero elsewhere. Find Var(X | Y = 0.5). Give the answer as a decimal correct to four decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The conditional variance of X at Y = 0.5.
  2. Which idea applies. Find f(x | 0.5), then compute the conditional mean and second moment, or use the uniform formula.
  3. Work it out. f(x | 0.5) = 2 on [0.5, 1], which is uniform on [0.5, 1]. The uniform variance is (b - a)²/12 = (0.5)²/12 = 0.25/12 = 1/48, which is 0.0208 to four decimals. Check by integration: E[X | 0.5] = 0.75 and E[X² | 0.5] = ∫ from 0.5 to 1 of 2x² dx = 2(1 - 0.125)/3 = 0.5833. Then 0.5833 - 0.5625 = 0.0208.
  4. Check. The interval has length 0.5, so the variance must be at most (0.5)²/4 = 0.0625. The value 0.0208 is well within that bound.

Answer: 0.0208

Building the joint density from a marginal and a conditional

NAT question. The marginal density of Y is fY(y) = 2y for 0 ≤ y ≤ 1. Given Y = y, X is uniform on [0, y]. Let f(x, y) be the joint density. Find P(X ≤ 0.2). Give the answer as a decimal.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A marginal probability of X, found from a joint density we must build first.
  2. Which idea applies. The joint density is the conditional times the marginal, f(x, y) = f(x | y) fY(y). Then integrate out y.
  3. Work it out. f(x | y) = 1/y on 0 ≤ x ≤ y, so f(x, y) = (1/y)(2y) = 2 on the region 0 ≤ x ≤ y ≤ 1. Marginal of X: fX(x) = ∫ from x to 1 of 2 dy = 2(1 - x). Then P(X ≤ 0.2) = ∫ from 0 to 0.2 of 2(1 - x) dx = [2x - x²] from 0 to 0.2 = 0.4 - 0.04 = 0.36.
  4. Check. The joint density integrates to 1 over the triangle, since 2 × 1/2 = 1. The answer 0.36 is below 0.5, which fits because X is more likely to be large given the marginal 2(1 - x) falls with x.

Answer: 0.36

Exam-level 1: A conditional probability from a joint density (NAT, 2 marks)

NAT question. Two continuous random variables X and Y have the joint density f(x, y) = x + y for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1 and 0 ≤ y ≤ 1, and f(x, y) = 0 otherwise. The marginal density of Y is f_Y(y) = y + 1/2 on [0, 1]. Find the conditional probability P(X ≤ 0.5 | Y = 0.5) using the conditional density f(x | y) = f(x, y) / f_Y(y). Give the answer as a decimal with three places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. P(X ≤ 0.5 | Y = 0.5), as a decimal with three places.
  2. Which idea applies. Divide the joint density by the marginal density of Y to get the conditional density, then integrate it over the required range of x.
  3. Work it out. f_Y(0.5) = 0.5 + 0.5 = 1. So f(x | 0.5) = (x + 0.5) / 1 = x + 0.5. Then P(X ≤ 0.5 | Y = 0.5) = ∫ from 0 to 0.5 of (x + 0.5) dx = [x²/2 + 0.5x] from 0 to 0.5 = 0.125 + 0.25 = 0.375.
  4. Check. The conditional density f(x | 0.5) = x + 0.5 integrates to 1 over [0, 1], since 0.5 + 0.5 = 1, so it is a valid density. The answer 0.375 is below one half, which fits because the density is larger at large x.

Answer: 0.375

from fractions import Fraction as F
y = F(1, 2)
def fY(t):
    return F(1, 2) + t
def A(t):
    return t * t / 2 + y * t
print("f_Y(1/2) =", fY(y))
print("area over [0,1] =", (A(F(1)) - A(F(0))) / fY(y))
p = (A(F(1, 2)) - A(F(0))) / fY(y)
print("P(X at most 1/2 | Y = 1/2) =", p, "=", float(p))
f_Y(1/2) = 1
area over [0,1] = 1
P(X at most 1/2 | Y = 1/2) = 3/8 = 0.375

Exam-level 2: A conditional mean on a triangle (MCQ, 2 marks)

Continuous random variables X and Y have the joint density f(x, y) = 8xy for 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1, and f(x, y) = 0 otherwise. Given that Y = 1/2, which of the following is the conditional expected value E[X | Y = 1/2]? (A) 0.5 (B) 7/9 (C) 3/4 (D) 2/3. Select one option.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. E[X | Y = 1/2].
  2. Which idea applies. The marginal density f_Y(y) comes from integrating over the allowed x, which here runs from y to 1. The conditional density is f(x | y) = f(x, y) / f_Y(y), and E[X | Y = y] is the integral of x f(x | y) over the same range.
  3. Work it out. f_Y(y) = ∫ from y to 1 of 8xy dx = 4y(1 - y²). At y = 1/2 this is 4(1/2)(3/4) = 3/2. So f(x | 1/2) = 8x(1/2) / (3/2) = (8/3)x for 1/2 ≤ x ≤ 1. Check that it integrates to 1: (8/3)(1 - 1/4)/2 = 1. Then E[X | Y = 1/2] = ∫ from 1/2 to 1 of x (8/3)x dx = (8/3)(1 - 1/8)/3 = (8/3)(7/24) = 7/9, which is about 0.7778. The answer is option B.
  4. Check. The range is [1/2, 1], so the mean must lie there, and 0.778 does. The density (8/3)x rises with x, so the mean lies above the midpoint 0.75, which matches. Exam-level 3: Testing independence from a joint density (MSQ, 2 marks)

Two random variables X and Y, both positive, have the joint density f(x, y) = e^(-(x + y)) for x > 0 and y > 0, and f(x, y) = 0 otherwise. Select all the statements that are true.

  • (A) X and Y are independent.
  • (B) The conditional density of Y given X = x is e^(-y) for y > 0.
  • (C) E[X | Y = y] = 1 for every y > 0.
  • (D) P(X > Y) = 1/4.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which of the four statements are true. Select all that apply.
  2. Which idea applies. Find the marginals by integrating out the other variable. X and Y are independent when the joint density equals the product of the marginals.
  3. Work it out. f_X(x) = ∫ from 0 to ∞ of e^(-x-y) dy = e^(-x), and similarly f_Y(y) = e^(-y). Their product is e^(-x) e^(-y) = f(x, y), so (A) is true. Dividing the joint density by f_X(x) gives f(y | x) = e^(-y), so (B) is true. Since X and Y are independent, E[X | Y = y] = E[X] = ∫ from 0 to ∞ of x e^(-x) dx = 1, so (C) is true. For (D), P(X > Y) = ∫ from 0 to ∞ of e^(-y) P(X > y) dy = ∫ from 0 to ∞ of e^(-2y) dy = 1/2, so (D) is false.
  4. Check. The statements X > Y and Y > X are equally likely by symmetry, and ties have probability 0, so each must be 1/2. That confirms (D) is false.

Answer: A, B and C

from math import exp
def f(x, y):
    return exp(-(x + y))

    return exp(-x)
def fY(y):
    return exp(-y)

print("independent at sample points:", all(abs(f(x, y) - fX(x) * fY(y)) < 1e-12 for x, y in pts))

Exam-level 4: Conditional variance from a product density (NAT, 2 marks)

NAT question. Two random variables X and Y have the joint density f(x, y) = c x y for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1 and 0 ≤ y ≤ 1, and f(x, y) = 0 otherwise, where c is a positive constant. Given that Y = 1/2, find the conditional variance Var(X | Y = 1/2). Give the answer correct to four decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Var(X | Y = 1/2), to four decimal places.
  2. Which idea applies. Find c from the total area, then build the conditional density f(x | y) = f(x, y) / f_Y(y), then use Var = E[X²] - (E[X])² under that conditional density.
  3. Work it out. ∫∫ c x y dx dy = c × (1/2) × (1/2) = c/4 = 1, so c = 4. Then f_Y(y) = ∫ from 0 to 1 of 4xy dx = 2y, and f_Y(1/2) = 1. So f(x | 1/2) = 4x(1/2) / 1 = 2x on [0, 1]. Then E[X | Y = 1/2] = ∫ from 0 to 1 of x (2x) dx = 2/3, and E[X² | Y = 1/2] = ∫ from 0 to 1 of x² (2x) dx = 1/2. So Var(X | Y = 1/2) = 1/2 - (2/3)² = 1/2 - 4/9 = 1/18 ≈ 0.0556.
  4. Check. The density 2x on [0, 1] has mean 2/3, which is above 1/2, as expected since it puts more weight near 1. Its variance 0.0556 is below the variance 1/12 ≈ 0.0833 of the uniform density on [0, 1], which is also reasonable because this density is more concentrated.

Answer: 0.0556

from fractions import Fraction as F
c = F(1, F(1, 4))
y = F(1, 2)
fY = c * y / 2
coef = c * y / fY
def moment(k):
    return coef * F(1, k + 2)
EX = moment(1)
EX2 = moment(2)
VX = EX2 - EX ** 2
print("c =", c)
print("f_Y(1/2) =", fY)
print("E[X | Y = 1/2] =", EX)
print("E[X^2 | Y = 1/2] =", EX2)
print("Var(X | Y = 1/2) =", VX, "=", round(float(VX), 4))

Summary

Joint PDF
A non-negative function f(x, y) whose total volume is 1.
Marginal of X
fX(x) = ∫ f(x, y) dy, found by integrating out y.
Marginal of Y
fY(y) = ∫ f(x, y) dx, found by integrating out x.
Conditional PDF
f(x | y) = f(x, y) / fY(y), a density in x for fixed y.
Conditional probability
P(a ≤ X ≤ b | Y = y) = ∫ from a to b of f(x | y) dx.
Conditional expectation
E[X | Y = y] = ∫ x f(x | y) dx.
Conditional variance
Var(X | Y = y) = E[X² | Y = y] - (E[X | Y = y])².
Independence
f(x, y) = fX(x) fY(y) for all x and y.
Joint from conditional
f(x, y) = f(x | y) fY(y).
Triangular support
A region such as 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1, which makes the variables dependent.
Conditional uniform
When f(x | y) is flat, its mean and variance follow the uniform formulas.
Normalising
Dividing by a marginal so that a conditional density has total area 1.

Remember these

  1. To get a marginal, integrate the joint density over the other variable.
  2. The conditional density is the joint density divided by the marginal of the given variable.
  3. Always check that a conditional density integrates to 1 over the variable it describes.
  4. For a triangular support, the range of one variable depends on the other, so they are dependent.
  5. Independence needs the joint density to factorise into a product of one-variable functions.
  6. When the conditional density is flat, use the uniform mean (a + b)/2 and variance (b - a)²/12.
  7. Joint probabilities for independent variables are products of marginal probabilities.
