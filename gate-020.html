Maxima and minima

A maximum is the highest point of a function and a minimum is the lowest. Derivatives find these points: a flat slope is the candidate, and a second test or a sign check tells you whether it is a peak, a valley or neither. This is the core skill behind optimisation in GATE DA.

The idea in plain words

Imagine walking a hilly path. At the top of a hill, the ground is flat for a moment before it falls away again. At the bottom of a valley, the ground is also flat for a moment. These flat moments are the places where a maximum or a minimum can hide. The slope of the path is the derivative, so the flat moments are the points where the derivative is zero.

Flat is not enough, though. A flat point can also be a place where the path levels out and keeps climbing, like a step on a staircase. So we need a second check to decide whether we are on a peak, in a valley, or neither. This topic gives you the checks.

Why does this matter for GATE? Almost every optimisation question starts by finding the maximum or minimum of a function. In machine learning, a loss function is a valley you want to reach. Finding the bottom of the valley is the same as finding a minimum, so the ideas here are the foundation for the next topics.

Key terms

Each term gets a plain meaning first, then a precise definition.

Local maximum
Plain meaning: a peak, higher than everything nearby. Precise definition: f(c) is at least f(x) for all x close to c.
Local minimum
Plain meaning: a valley, lower than everything nearby. Precise definition: f(c) is at most f(x) for all x close to c.
Global maximum
Plain meaning: the highest value on the whole domain or interval. Precise definition: f(c) is at least f(x) for every x in the domain.
Global minimum
Plain meaning: the lowest value on the whole domain or interval. Precise definition: f(c) is at most f(x) for every x in the domain.
Critical point
Plain meaning: a place to check for a peak or valley. Precise definition: a point c in the domain where f'(c) = 0 or f'(c) does not exist.
Closed interval
Plain meaning: an interval that includes its two ends. Precise definition: [a, b] is the set of all x with a at most x at most b.
Concave up
Plain meaning: the curve bends like a cup, holding water. Precise definition: f''(x) is greater than 0 on the interval.
Concave down
Plain meaning: the curve bends like a dome, shedding water. Precise definition: f''(x) is less than 0 on the interval.
Inflection point
Plain meaning: a point where the bend changes direction. Precise definition: a point where the concavity changes, so f'' changes sign there.

TipA local extremum is a local idea: it only compares with nearby values. A global extremum compares with every value on the domain. Read each question carefully to see which one it asks for.

Critical points: where to look first

Every local maximum or minimum of a smooth function is a critical point. So the first job is to list the critical points. There are two kinds of candidates:

  1. Stationary points, where f'(x) = 0. The tangent is flat.
  2. Singular points, where f'(x) does not exist. These include corners and cusps.

An important warning: not every critical point is a maximum or a minimum. Some are inflection points, where the function keeps rising or keeps falling through a flat moment.

Worked example. Find the critical points of f(x) = x cubed minus 3x.

  1. Step 1. Differentiate: f'(x) = 3x squared minus 3.
  2. Step 2. Set f'(x) = 0: 3x squared minus 3 = 0, so x squared = 1.
  3. Step 3. Solve: x = 1 or x = minus 1.
  4. Step 4. f' exists everywhere, so there are no singular points.
  5. Answer. The critical points are x = minus 1 and x = 1.
Quick check: what are the critical points of f(x) = |x|?

The derivative is undefined at x = 0, which is a corner. f'(x) is never zero elsewhere. So x = 0 is the only critical point, and it is a singular point.

Quick check: is every critical point a local maximum or minimum?

No. For example f(x) = x cubed has f'(0) = 0, but 0 is neither a maximum nor a minimum. The graph keeps rising through 0.

The first derivative test

The first derivative test looks at the sign of f' just before and just after a critical point. The sign of f' tells you whether the function is going up or down. Think of a car on a road: positive slope means moving uphill, negative slope means moving downhill.

Sign of f' just left of cSign of f' just right of cConclusion
Positive (going up)Negative (going down)Local maximum at c
Negative (going down)Positive (going up)Local minimum at c
Same sign on both sidesSame sign on both sidesNeither a maximum nor a minimum

Here is the check for f(x) = x cubed minus 3x. Its critical points are at minus 1 and 1. We test the sign of f' at points on either side.

def f_prime(x):
    return 3 * x * x - 3

for x in [-2, -0.5, 0.5, 2]:
    print("x =", x, "f'(x) =", f_prime(x))
x = -2 f'(x) = 9
x = -0.5 f'(x) = -2.25
x = 0.5 f'(x) = -2.25
x = 2 f'(x) = 9

Read the signs. At x = minus 2 the slope is positive, and at x = minus 0.5 it is negative. So the function goes up and then down as x passes minus 1, which makes minus 1 a local maximum. Similarly, the slope is negative just left of 1 and positive just right of 1, so 1 is a local minimum.

Key ideaThe first derivative test asks "did the slope change sign?" If yes, there is a peak or a valley. If no, the point is not an extremum.

Quick check: f'(x) = x squared on both sides of 0 for f(x) = x cubed/3 is positive, except at 0 itself. Is 0 an extremum?

No. The sign of f' does not change at 0. It is positive on both sides, so the function keeps rising through 0.

Quick check: f' goes from negative to positive at c. What kind of point is c?

A local minimum, because the function falls and then rises.

The second derivative test

The second derivative test is a faster shortcut. Instead of checking signs on both sides, you evaluate f'' at the critical point. The second derivative measures how the slope itself changes, so it tells you the bend of the curve at that point.

  • If f'(c) = 0 and f''(c) is greater than 0, the curve is cup-shaped at c, so c is a local minimum.
  • If f'(c) = 0 and f''(c) is less than 0, the curve is dome-shaped at c, so c is a local maximum.
  • If f'(c) = 0 and f''(c) = 0, the test is inconclusive. Use the first derivative test instead.

Worked example. For f(x) = x cubed minus 3x, the second derivative is f''(x) = 6x. The critical points are minus 1 and 1.

def f(x):
    return x ** 3 - 3 * x

def f2(x):
    return 6 * x

for c in [-1, 1]:
    kind = "local max" if f2(c) < 0 else "local min"
    print("x =", c, "f(x) =", f(c), "f''(x) =", f2(c), "->", kind)
x = -1 f(x) = 2 f''(x) = -6 -> local max
x = 1 f(x) = -2 f''(x) = 6 -> local min

So the local maximum is 2 at x = minus 1, and the local minimum is minus 2 at x = 1. These agree with the sign test from the previous section.

TrapWhen f''(c) = 0, the second derivative test says nothing. Do not conclude that c is a point of inflection or a maximum. Switch to the first derivative test.

Quick check: for f(x) = minus x squared plus 6x, where is the maximum and what is its value?

f'(x) = minus 2x plus 6, which is 0 at x = 3. f''(x) = minus 2, which is less than 0, so x = 3 is a local maximum. The value is f(3) = minus 9 plus 18 = 9.

Quick check: why is the second derivative test inconclusive for x to the power 4 at 0?

The second derivative is 12x squared, which equals 0 at x = 0. So the test gives no answer, even though 0 is a minimum. The first derivative test resolves it, because f' = 4x cubed changes sign from negative to positive.

Concavity and inflection points

Concavity describes how a curve bends. Picture a cup. If you pour water in, it stays in the cup. The curve is concave up. Picture an upside-down cup, which sheds water. The curve is concave down.

f''(x) > 0 means concave up; f''(x) < 0 means concave down

An inflection point is where the bend changes, from cup to dome or from dome to cup. At such a point, the second derivative changes sign. A candidate for an inflection point is any x with f''(x) = 0, but you must check that the sign really changes.

Worked example. For f(x) = x cubed minus 3x, we have f''(x) = 6x. Its sign is negative for x less than 0 and positive for x greater than 0. The sign changes at 0, so the curve changes from dome to cup there. The point (0, 0) is an inflection point.

GATE loves thisA point where f'(c) = 0 but f''(c) = 0 is not automatically an inflection point. Check the sign of f'' on both sides. In exam questions, a double root of f'' can give a point with no sign change.

Quick check: is x = 0 an inflection point of f(x) = x to the power 4?

No. The second derivative 12x squared is 0 at 0, but it does not change sign. It is positive on both sides, so the curve is cup-shaped on both sides.

Quick check: what is the concavity of f(x) = x squared on the whole real line?

Concave up everywhere, because f''(x) = 2, which is positive for every x.

Global extrema on a closed interval

A local maximum only beats its neighbours. The highest point on a closed interval is a global maximum. The extreme value theorem says that a function continuous on a closed interval [a, b] always has a global maximum and a global minimum somewhere on that interval.

To find them, compare a short list of candidates:

  1. The endpoints a and b.
  2. Every critical point c that lies inside the interval (strictly between a and b).

Evaluate f at each candidate. The largest value is the global maximum, and the smallest value is the global minimum. Do not forget the endpoints, because the extremum is often there.

Worked example. Find the global maximum and minimum of f(x) = x cubed minus 3x on [0, 3].

  1. Step 1, candidates. The endpoints are 0 and 3. The critical points from earlier are minus 1 and 1. Only 1 lies inside [0, 3].
  2. Step 2, values. f(0) = 0, f(1) = minus 2, f(3) = 27 minus 9 = 18.
  3. Step 3, compare. The smallest value is minus 2 at x = 1, and the largest is 18 at x = 3.
def f(x):
    return x ** 3 - 3 * x

points = [0, 1, 3]
for x in points:
    print("x =", x, "f(x) =", f(x))
print("global min =", min(f(x) for x in points), "global max =", max(f(x) for x in points))
x = 0 f(x) = 0
x = 1 f(x) = -2
x = 3 f(x) = 18
global min = -2 global max = 18

TrapThe point x = minus 1 is a local maximum of f, but it lies outside [0, 3]. Using a local maximum as a global maximum on an interval is a common error.

Quick check: for f(x) = x squared on [-1, 2], where is the global minimum?

At x = 0, which is a critical point inside the interval, with value 0. The endpoint values are 1 and 4, so 0 is the smallest.

Quick check: must the global maximum be a critical point?

No. It may be an endpoint of the interval. Always compare the endpoints with the critical points.

Tricky cases where the tests disagree

Sometimes the second derivative test gives no answer, and sometimes a critical point is not an extremum. Here are the cases you are most likely to meet.

Case 1: f''(c) = 0. The function f(x) = x to the power 4 has f'(0) = 0 and f''(0) = 0. The second derivative test fails. The first derivative test shows that f' = 4x cubed is negative on the left and positive on the right, so 0 is a local minimum.

Case 2: a critical point with no sign change. The function f(x) = x cubed has f'(0) = 0, but f' = 3x squared is positive on both sides. So 0 is not an extremum, but it is an inflection point, because f'' = 6x changes sign.

Case 3: a corner. The function f(x) = |x| has no derivative at 0. The corner is a critical point, and it is a local minimum, because the function falls then rises.

Key ideaWhen in doubt, return to the first derivative test. It works in every case where f' exists on both sides of the point.

Quick check: is x = 0 a local extremum of f(x) = x to the power 3 minus x?

f'(x) = 3x squared minus 1, which is minus 1 at 0. It is not zero there, so 0 is not a critical point and cannot be an extremum.

Minimum value of a parabola

Find the minimum value of f(x) = x squared minus 4x plus 1. Give your answer as an integer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The lowest value the function takes.
  2. Which idea applies. Find f'(x), set it to 0, then use f''(x) to check.
  3. Work it out. f'(x) = 2x minus 4 = 0 gives x = 2. f''(x) = 2, which is positive, so x = 2 is a minimum. f(2) = 4 minus 8 plus 1 = minus 3.
  4. Check. Try x = 1: f(1) = 1 minus 4 plus 1 = minus 2, which is larger than minus 3. Try x = 3: f(3) = 9 minus 12 plus 1 = minus 2, also larger.

Answer: -3

Type of extremum

For f(x) = minus x squared plus 6x, the point x = 3 is a critical point. Is it a local maximum, a local minimum, or neither?

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The type of the critical point x = 3.
  2. Which idea applies. The second derivative test.
  3. Work it out. f''(x) = minus 2, which is negative for every x. So f''(3) is negative, and the graph is dome-shaped at 3.
  4. Check. The graph is a downward parabola, so its only critical point is its peak.

Answer: a local maximum

Cubic function, select all that apply

Let f(x) = x cubed minus 3x plus 2. Select all that apply: (A) x = minus 1 is a local maximum; (B) x = 1 is a local minimum; (C) x = 0 is an inflection point; (D) f is concave up for every negative x.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which of the four statements are true.
  2. Which idea applies. Critical points, the second derivative test, and the sign of f''.
  3. Work it out. f'(x) = 3x squared minus 3, so the critical points are minus 1 and 1. f''(x) = 6x. At minus 1, f'' = minus 6, so it is a local maximum, and (A) is true. At 1, f'' = 6, so it is a local minimum, and (B) is true. f''(0) = 0 and f'' changes sign at 0, so (C) is true. For negative x, f'' = 6x is negative, so f is concave down, and (D) is false.
  4. Check. The values are f(minus 1) = 4 and f(1) = 0, so the peak is higher than the valley, as expected.

Answer: (A), (B) and (C)

Minimum of a sum

Let f(x) = x squared plus 16/x for x greater than 0. Find the minimum value of f. Give your answer as an integer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The smallest value of f for positive x.
  2. Which idea applies. Find f'(x), solve f'(x) = 0, then check the second derivative.
  3. Work it out. f'(x) = 2x minus 16/x squared. Setting this to 0 gives 2x cubed = 16, so x cubed = 8 and x = 2. Also f''(x) = 2 + 32/x cubed, which is positive for x greater than 0, so x = 2 is a minimum. f(2) = 4 + 8 = 12.
  4. Check. f(1) = 1 + 16 = 17 and f(3) = 9 + 16/3 = 14.33, both larger than 12.

Answer: 12

Global maximum on an interval

Let f(x) = 12x minus x cubed on the interval [minus 1, 3]. Find the global maximum value of f on this interval. Give your answer as an integer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The largest value on the closed interval.
  2. Which idea applies. Compare endpoints and critical points inside the interval.
  3. Work it out. f'(x) = 12 minus 3x squared = 0 gives x = 2 or x = minus 2. Only x = 2 lies inside [minus 1, 3]. Values: f(minus 1) = minus 12 plus 1 = minus 11. f(2) = 24 minus 8 = 16. f(3) = 36 minus 27 = 9.
  4. Check. 16 is the largest of minus 11, 16 and 9, so the global maximum is 16.

Answer: 16

Flat point that is not a valley

Let f(x) = x to the power 4. Which one of the following is true about x = 0? (A) it is a local maximum; (B) it is a local minimum; (C) it is an inflection point; (D) it is not a critical point.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The nature of the point x = 0.
  2. Which idea applies. The first derivative test, since the second derivative test fails.
  3. Work it out. f'(x) = 4x cubed is 0 at 0, so 0 is a critical point, and (D) is false. f' is negative for x less than 0 and positive for x greater than 0, so the function falls then rises. This makes 0 a local minimum, (B). f''(x) = 12x squared does not change sign, so 0 is not an inflection point, and (C) is false.
  4. Check. f(0) = 0 and f is positive everywhere else, so 0 is indeed the lowest point.

Answer: (B)

Counting inflection points

Let f(x) = x to the power 4 minus 4x cubed. Find the number of inflection points of f. Give your answer as an integer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. How many points change the bend of the curve.
  2. Which idea applies. Find f'', then look for sign changes.
  3. Work it out. f'(x) = 4x cubed minus 12x squared = 4x squared (x minus 3). f''(x) = 12x squared minus 24x = 12x (x minus 2). The candidates are x = 0 and x = 2. For x less than 0, f'' is positive. For 0 less than x less than 2, f'' is negative, so the sign changes at 0. For x greater than 2, f'' is positive, so the sign changes again at 2. Both are inflection points.
  4. Check. The sign pattern is plus, minus, plus, which gives two changes.

Answer: 2

Absolute maximum on a closed interval

Let f(x) = x cubed minus 6x squared plus 9x plus 1 on [0, 4]. Find the absolute maximum value of f on this interval. Give your answer as an integer.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The highest value on [0, 4].
  2. Which idea applies. Compare endpoints with critical points inside the interval.
  3. Work it out. f'(x) = 3x squared minus 12x plus 9 = 3(x minus 1)(x minus 3). Critical points: 1 and 3, both inside [0, 4]. Values are computed below.
  4. Check. The values show the maximum is 5, attained at both x = 1 and x = 4, and the minimum is 1, attained at x = 0 and x = 3.
def f(x):
    return x ** 3 - 6 * x ** 2 + 9 * x + 1

for x in [0, 1, 3, 4]:
    print("x =", x, "f(x) =", f(x))
x = 0 f(x) = 1
x = 1 f(x) = 5
x = 3 f(x) = 1
x = 4 f(x) = 5

Answer: 5

Exam-level 1: spread of a profit index on a closed interval (NAT, 2 marks)

A small firm's weekly profit index is modelled as P(t) = t3 - 12t + 5 for t in the closed interval [-3, 3], where t is a scaled time with t = 0 at the midpoint of the period. The index is reviewed at its highest and lowest points within this interval. Find the largest value of P minus the smallest value of P on [-3, 3]. Give the answer as a whole number.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The absolute maximum and absolute minimum of P on [-3, 3], and then their difference.
  2. Which idea applies. On a closed interval, the extreme values occur either at critical points inside the interval, where P'(t) = 0, or at the endpoints. Compare every candidate value.
  3. Work it out. P'(t) = 3t2 - 12 = 0 gives t = -2 and t = 2, and both lie in [-3, 3]. The values are P(-3) = -27 + 36 + 5 = 14, P(-2) = -8 + 24 + 5 = 21, P(2) = 8 - 24 + 5 = -11, and P(3) = 27 - 36 + 5 = -4. The largest is 21 at t = -2, and the smallest is -11 at t = 2. The difference is 21 - (-11) = 32.
  4. Check. The Python block lists the four candidate values, and the maximum and minimum are read from them. Since P''(t) = 6t is negative at t = -2 and positive at t = 2, the first point is a local maximum and the second a local minimum, which agrees with the values.
def f(x):
    return x**3 - 12*x + 5
pts = [-3, -2, 2, 3]
for x in pts:
    print("x =", x, "f =", f(x))
hi = max(f(x) for x in pts)
lo = min(f(x) for x in pts)
print("max =", hi, " min =", lo, " max - min =", hi - lo)
x = -3 f = 14
x = -2 f = 21
x = 2 f = -11
x = 3 f = -4
max = 21  min = -11  max - min = 32

Answer: 32

Exam-level 2: stationary points and concavity (MSQ, 2 marks)

Let f(x) = x4 - 4x3 for all real x. This is a cost curve, and the sign of its slope shows whether the cost is rising or falling. Select all that apply. (A) x = 3 is a local minimum of f. (B) x = 0 is a local maximum of f. (C) x = 0 is a local minimum of f. (D) x = 0 is an inflection point of f, meaning that the concavity changes there.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements about the stationary points of f are true.
  2. Which idea applies. Find the critical points from f'(x) = 0. Then use the sign of f' on each side of each point to decide whether it is a local maximum, a local minimum, or neither. Use f''(x) to find where the concavity changes.
  3. Work it out. f'(x) = 4x3 - 12x2 = 4x2(x - 3), so the critical points are x = 0 and x = 3. Near 0 the sign of f' is negative on both sides, so f does not turn at 0, and (B) and (C) are false. At x = 3, f' changes from negative to positive, so 3 is a local minimum and (A) is true. For (D), f''(x) = 12x2 - 24x = 12x(x - 2). Its sign changes at x = 0, so the concavity changes there and (D) is true. Note that f''(0) = 0 alone does not settle it; the sign change does.
  4. Check. The Python block prints f' at points on each side of 0 and 3, which show the sign pattern, and f'' at points near 0 and 2. The slope is negative on both sides of 0, and f'' changes sign at 0.
def f1(x):
    return 4*x**3 - 12*x**2
def f2(x):
    return 12*x**2 - 24*x
print("f(3) =", 3**4 - 4*3**3, "  f(0) =", 0)
for x in [-0.1, 0.1, 2.9, 3.1]:
    print("f'(%s) = %.4f" % (x, f1(x)))
for x in [-0.1, 0.1, 1.9, 2.1]:
    print("f''(%s) = %.4f" % (x, f2(x)))
f(3) = -27   f(0) = 0
f'(-0.1) = -0.1240
f'(0.1) = -0.1160
f'(2.9) = -3.3640
f'(3.1) = 3.8440
f''(-0.1) = 2.5200
f''(0.1) = -2.2800
f''(1.9) = -2.2800

Answer: (A) and (D) are true. (B) and (C) are false.

Exam-level 3: peak of a decaying concentration (MCQ, 2 marks)

A drug's concentration over time is modelled by C(x) = x e-x for x at least 0, where x is the time in hours after a dose. Which option gives the peak concentration, meaning the global maximum of C on x at least 0, together with the time at which it occurs? (A) 0 at x = 0. (B) 1/e, about 0.3679, at x = 1. (C) 1 at x = 1. (D) 2/e2, about 0.2707, at x = 2.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The global maximum value of C and the time at which it occurs.
  2. Which idea applies. Use the product rule to find C'(x), set it to zero, and test the critical point with the second derivative. Then check the endpoint and the behaviour as x grows.
  3. Work it out. C'(x) = e-x - x e-x = e-x(1 - x). Since e-x is never zero, C'(x) = 0 only at x = 1. Next, C''(x) = e-x(x - 2), so C''(1) = -e-1 is negative, and x = 1 is a local maximum. At x = 0, C = 0, and C tends to 0 as x grows because the exponential decays faster than x grows. So the global maximum is C(1) = e-1, about 0.3679, which is option (B). Option (D) gives C(2), which is a value at a point that is not a maximum.
  4. Check. The Python block samples C on a fine grid from 0 to 20 and finds the largest value and where it occurs. The grid maximum is 0.3679 at x = 1.0, and the second derivative at 1 is -0.3679, which is negative.
import math
best_x, best_v = None, -1.0
for i in range(0, 20001):
    x = i / 1000
    v = x * math.exp(-x)
    if v > best_v:
        best_v, best_x = v, x
print("max value =", round(best_v, 4), "at x =", round(best_x, 3))
print("1/e =", round(1 / math.e, 4))
print("f''(1) =", round((1 - 2) * math.exp(-1), 4))
max value = 0.3679 at x = 1.0
1/e = 0.3679
f''(1) = -0.3679

Answer: (B)

Exam-level 4: best output for a factory (NAT, 2 marks)

A factory's monthly profit, in thousands of pounds, depends on its output q, measured in hundreds of units, by P(q) = -q3 + 9q2 + 15q - 20. Output can only lie between 0 and 12 hundred units. Find the maximum monthly profit, in thousands of pounds, correct to 2 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The maximum of P over the allowed output range [0, 12], to 2 decimal places.
  2. Which idea applies. Find the critical points inside the range from P'(q) = 0. Then compare P at those points with P at the two endpoints 0 and 12.
  3. Work it out. P'(q) = -3q2 + 18q + 15. Setting this to zero and dividing by -3 gives q2 - 6q - 5 = 0, so q = 3 plus or minus sqrt(14). The root 3 - sqrt(14) is negative, so it is outside the range. The root q = 3 + sqrt(14), about 6.7417, is inside. Now compare: P(0) = -20, P(12) = -272, and P(6.7417) is about 183.77. The maximum profit is about 183.77.
  4. Check. The Python block evaluates P at the critical point and at both endpoints. The interior point gives by far the largest value, so it is the maximum.
import math
def P(q):
    return -q**3 + 9*q*q + 15*q - 20
qs = 3 + math.sqrt(14)
print("critical point q* =", round(qs, 4))

Summary

Local maximum
Plain meaning: a peak. Precise definition: f(c) is at least f(x) for x near c.
Local minimum
Plain meaning: a valley. Precise definition: f(c) is at most f(x) for x near c.
Global maximum
Plain meaning: the highest value overall. Precise definition: f(c) is at least f(x) for every x in the domain.
Global minimum
Plain meaning: the lowest value overall. Precise definition: f(c) is at most f(x) for every x in the domain.
Critical point
Plain meaning: a candidate peak or valley. Precise definition: f'(c) = 0 or f'(c) does not exist.
First derivative test
Plain meaning: did the slope change sign? Precise definition: a sign change of f' from + to - gives a maximum, and from - to + gives a minimum.
Second derivative test
Plain meaning: is the curve a cup or a dome? Precise definition: f'(c) = 0 with f''(c) positive gives a minimum, and negative gives a maximum.
Concave up
Plain meaning: shaped like a cup. Precise definition: f''(x) is greater than 0.
Concave down
Plain meaning: shaped like a dome. Precise definition: f''(x) is less than 0.
Inflection point
Plain meaning: where the bend changes. Precise definition: a point where f'' changes sign.
Closed interval
Plain meaning: an interval with both ends included. Precise definition: [a, b], all x with a at most x at most b.
Extreme value theorem
Plain meaning: a continuous function on a closed interval always has a highest and lowest point. Precise definition: a continuous f on [a, b] attains a global maximum and a global minimum.

Remember these

  1. Every local extremum of a smooth function is a critical point, but not every critical point is an extremum.
  2. Use the second derivative for a quick check, and the first derivative when f'' is zero.
  3. On a closed interval, compare the endpoints with the critical points inside.
  4. A local maximum outside the interval is not the answer on that interval.
  5. An inflection point needs a sign change in f'', not just f'' equal to zero.
