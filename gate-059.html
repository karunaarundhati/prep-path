Solutions of heat, wave & Laplace's equations

Some physical quantities depend on both position and time, such as the temperature along a rod or the shape of a vibrating string. Their rules are partial differential equations. This page teaches the three standard equations (heat, wave and Laplace), the separation of variables method that solves them, and how boundary and initial conditions pick one answer.

The idea in plain words

Imagine a long metal rod. One end is in a flame and the other end is in ice. Over time, heat travels along the rod, and the temperature at each point changes. The temperature depends on two things: where you are along the rod, and how much time has passed. A function that depends on more than one variable is written u(x, t).

Physics gives a rule for how such a temperature changes. This rule is a partial differential equation, usually shortened to PDE. The word "partial" means that the equation has derivatives with respect to one variable at a time, while the other variables are held still. A PDE is like an ordinary differential equation with more than one independent variable.

Three standard PDEs appear again and again in engineering:

  • Heat equation. It describes how heat spreads out, like ink spreading in still water.
  • Wave equation. It describes how a disturbance travels, like a plucked guitar string.
  • Laplace's equation. It describes a steady state, where nothing is changing with time anymore, like the final temperature of a plate that has settled down.

To solve these equations, the most common method is separation of variables. The idea is to guess that the answer splits into a product of a function of x and a function of t. This turns one hard PDE into two easier ordinary differential equations, which you already know how to solve from the previous topics.

Why this matters for GATE. The Robotics and Automation paper expects you to recognise these three equations, know their standard forms, understand what each one means physically, and apply simple boundary and initial conditions. Questions are usually conceptual or use a short standard solution. You do not need advanced theory, but you do need to read each equation correctly.

Key terms

Read this list once now. Return to it when a symbol or word feels unfamiliar.

Partial derivative
A derivative of a function with several variables, taken with respect to one variable while the others are held constant. Written as ∂u/∂x or ux.
Subscript notation
A short way to write derivatives. ux means ∂u/∂x, uxx means ∂2u/∂x2, and ut means ∂u/∂t.
Partial differential equation (PDE)
An equation that contains partial derivatives of an unknown function of two or more variables.
Heat (diffusion) equation
ut = c2uxx. It describes how temperature spreads over time.
Wave equation
utt = c2uxx. It describes how a disturbance travels with speed c.
Laplace's equation
uxx + uyy = 0. It describes a steady state with no change in time.
Harmonic function
A function that satisfies Laplace's equation.
Separation of variables
A method that assumes u(x, t) = X(x)T(t), a product of a function of x and a function of t.
Eigenvalue and eigenfunction
In this topic, an eigenvalue λ is a special constant for which a boundary problem has a non-zero solution. The matching solution is the eigenfunction. Example: sin(nπx/L) for λ = (nπ/L)2.
Mode
One of the simple patterns a system can vibrate or decay in. Mode n has n half-waves along the length.
Boundary condition
A rule for u at the edge of the region, such as u(0, t) = 0. It says what happens at the ends.
Initial condition
A rule for u at the starting time, such as u(x, 0) = f(x). It says what the system looks like when you start.
Steady state
A situation where the solution does not change with time. Then ut = 0.
Standing wave
A wave pattern that stays in the same place and only goes up and down in height. Its nodes are the points that never move.
Node
A point on a vibrating string that stays at rest all the time.
Diffusivity c2
The constant in the heat equation. A larger value means heat spreads faster.
Wave speed c
The constant in the wave equation. It is the speed at which disturbances travel.

Step 1: The three standard equations

Reading the derivatives

Let us start with the notation. Suppose u(x, t) gives the temperature at position x and time t. There are four derivatives we need:

  • ut is the rate of change over time at a fixed position. It tells you whether a point is getting hotter or colder.
  • ux is the slope along the rod at a fixed time. It tells you which way heat is flowing.
  • uxx is the curvature along the rod. It tells you whether a point is hotter or colder than its neighbours on average.
  • utt is the rate at which the rate of change itself is changing. It is used for vibration.

The heat equation

ut = c2 uxx

In words: the rate of change of temperature over time is proportional to the curvature of temperature in space. Heat flows from hot to cold, and the rate is set by c2, the diffusivity. The equation has one time derivative and two space derivatives. This is why it has a special shape. It is called parabolic, but you do not need that name for GATE.

The wave equation

utt = c2 uxx

In words: the acceleration of a point is proportional to the curvature of the string. A string that bends sharply is pulled back quickly, and this makes it vibrate. Here c is the wave speed. The equation has two time derivatives, which is why it describes motion that keeps going rather than dying out.

Laplace's equation

uxx + uyy = 0

In words: the curvature in x plus the curvature in y is zero. There is no time variable at all. This is the steady state. Once the heat has settled, the temperature at each point is the average tendency of its neighbours, so the curvatures balance.

Key ideaHeat has one time derivative, so it forgets its past and dies out. The wave equation has two time derivatives, so vibration keeps going. Laplace's equation has no time, so it describes a settled state.

Question: Which of the three equations has no time variable?

Laplace's equation, uxx + uyy = 0. It describes a steady state where nothing changes with time.

Question: The equation utt = 25uxx is a wave equation. What is its wave speed c?

Compare with utt = c2uxx. Here c2 = 25, so c = 5.

Step 2: Heat equation and separation of variables

The problem

Consider a rod of length L whose ends are kept at zero temperature. At the start, the temperature along the rod is given by a function f(x). We want to know how the temperature evolves. Written in full:

ut = c2 uxx, for 0 < x < L and t > 0
boundary conditions: u(0, t) = 0 and u(L, t) = 0
initial condition: u(x, 0) = f(x)

The boundary conditions say the ends stay cold. The initial condition says what the rod looks like at the beginning.

The method, one step at a time

  1. Assume a product. Let u(x, t) = X(x)T(t). This means the shape in x and the decay in t are separate.
  2. Substitute. ut = XT' and uxx = X''T. So XT' = c2X''T.
  3. Separate. Divide both sides by c2XT to get T'/(c2T) = X''/X. The left side depends only on t, and the right side only on x. They can only be equal if both equal a constant. Call this constant -λ.
  4. Solve the x-part. X'' + λX = 0 with X(0) = 0 and X(L) = 0. The solutions that are not all zero exist only for special values λn = (nπ/L)2, with n = 1, 2, 3, .... The matching shapes are Xn(x) = sin(nπx/L).
  5. Solve the t-part. T' = -c2λnT. This gives Tn(t) = e-c2λnt.
  6. Add the modes. Each mode works on its own, and the sum of solutions is also a solution. So u(x, t) = Σ bn sin(nπx/L) e-c2(nπ/L)2t.
  7. Use the initial condition. At t = 0, u(x, 0) = Σ bn sin(nπx/L) = f(x). This is a Fourier sine series, and the coefficients are bn = (2/L) ∫0L f(x) sin(nπx/L) dx.

TipIn step 4, the equation X'' + λX = 0 is the same kind you solved in the previous topic. Its boundary conditions force the special values of λ. The number n is the mode number.

Worked example 1: the initial shape is one mode

Solve the heat problem with L = 1, c = 1, and f(x) = sin(πx).

  1. Modes. With L = 1, the modes are sin(nπx) with exponents -(nπ)2t.
  2. Coefficients. The initial shape sin(πx) is exactly mode n = 1. So b1 = 1, and all other bn = 0.
  3. Solution. u(x, t) = sin(πx) e-π2t.
  4. Check. At t = 0, u = sin(πx), which matches. At x = 0 and x = 1, sin is zero, so the ends stay cold. The shape keeps its form and shrinks in height over time.

Worked example 2: a shape that needs many modes

Take L = 1, c = 1, and f(x) = x(1 - x). This is a parabola that is zero at both ends. Its sine coefficients are found by integration. Using the known result for this f, the coefficients are bn = 8/(n3π3) for odd n, and bn = 0 for even n. A numerical check follows.

import math

def b(n, N=100000):
    # b_n = 2 * integral from 0 to 1 of x(1 - x) sin(n pi x) dx, midpoint rule
    total = 0.0
    h = 1.0 / N
    for k in range(N):
        x = (k + 0.5) * h
        total += x * (1 - x) * math.sin(n * math.pi * x)
    return 2 * total * h

b1 = b(1)
b2 = b(2)
print("b1 =", round(b1, 4))
print("b1 matches 8/pi^3:", abs(b1 - 8 / math.pi**3) < 1e-6)
print("b2 is about zero:", abs(b2) < 1e-6)
b1 = 0.258
b1 matches 8/pi^3: True
b2 is about zero: True

TrapThe exponent includes the square of the mode factor. For mode n, the decay exponent is -c2(nπ/L)2t, not -c2nπt/L. Forgetting the square makes the high modes decay too slowly.

Question: For the heat equation with u(0, t) = u(L, t) = 0, what happens to u as t tends to infinity?

Every exponential factor e-c2λnt goes to zero, because λn is positive. So u tends to zero, and the rod cools to the boundary temperature.

Question: If the initial shape is 2 sin(3πx), which mode is present with what coefficient?

Only mode n = 3, with coefficient b3 = 2. All other coefficients are zero. The solution is 2 sin(3πx) e-9π2c2t/L2, with L and c in their place.

Step 3: Wave equation and standing waves

The problem

Take a string of length L, fixed at both ends, like a guitar string between the bridge and the nut. Its displacement is u(x, t), the height of the string at position x and time t. The wave equation with fixed ends is:

utt = c2 uxx, for 0 < x < L and t > 0
boundary conditions: u(0, t) = 0 and u(L, t) = 0
initial conditions: u(x, 0) = f(x) and ut(x, 0) = g(x)

Notice that the wave equation needs two initial conditions. The string needs a starting shape f(x), and it also needs a starting velocity g(x). The heat equation needed only one, because heat has only one time derivative.

The method

  1. Assume a product. u = X(x)T(t). Then utt = XT'' and uxx = X''T.
  2. Separate. XT'' = c2X''T, so T''/(c2T) = X''/X = -λ.
  3. The x-part is the same as for heat. X'' + λX = 0 with X(0) = X(L) = 0 gives λn = (nπ/L)2 and Xn = sin(nπx/L).
  4. The t-part oscillates. T'' + c2λnT = 0. Write ωn = nπc/L. Then Tn(t) = An cos(ωnt) + Bn sin(ωnt).
  5. Add the modes. u(x, t) = Σ sin(nπx/L) [An cos(ωnt) + Bn sin(ωnt)].
  6. Use the initial conditions. Setting t = 0 gives An as the sine coefficients of f. Differentiating in t and setting t = 0 gives Bnωn as the sine coefficients of g.

Key ideaHeat modes decay, because the t-part is an exponential with a negative exponent. Wave modes keep oscillating, because the t-part is a cosine and sine with frequency ωn. Each mode vibrates without losing energy in this simple model.

Worked example 3: a plucked string

Solve the wave problem with L = 1, c = 1, u(x, 0) = sin(πx), and ut(x, 0) = 0. The string starts in the shape of mode 1 and is released from rest.

  1. Modes. With L = 1 and c = 1, ωn = nπ.
  2. Initial shape. sin(πx) is mode 1, so A1 = 1 and all other An = 0.
  3. Initial velocity. The velocity is zero, so every Bn = 0.
  4. Solution. u(x, t) = sin(πx) cos(πt).
  5. Check. utt = -π2 sin(πx) cos(πt), and c2uxx = -π2 sin(πx) cos(πt). They match. At t = 1, u = -sin(πx), so the string is flipped upside down. This is the half-period of the motion.

Worked example 4: a string set moving

Solve the wave problem with L = 1, c = 1, u(x, 0) = 0, and ut(x, 0) = 3 sin(2πx).

  1. Which mode. The velocity is sin(2πx), which is mode 2. So the only nonzero coefficient is B2.
  2. Frequency. ω2 = 2π · c/L = 2π.
  3. Coefficient. From ut(x, 0) = B2ω2 sin(2πx) = 3 sin(2πx), we get B2 = 3/(2π).
  4. Solution. u(x, t) = (3/(2π)) sin(2πx) sin(2πt).
  5. Check. At t = 0, u = 0, as required. Differentiating in t gives ut = 3 sin(2πx) cos(2πt), which equals 3 sin(2πx) at t = 0.

Frequencies and nodes

Each mode vibrates at frequency fn = ωn/(2π) = nc/(2L) cycles per unit time. The first mode is the fundamental, and higher modes are its harmonics. Mode n has n - 1 nodes inside the string, which are points that never move. For mode 2, the node is at x = L/2, because sin(2π(L/2)/L) = sin(π) = 0.

c = 340.0
L = 0.5

for n in [1, 2, 3]:
    print("mode", n, "frequency =", n * c / (2 * L), "Hz")
mode 1 frequency = 340.0 Hz
mode 2 frequency = 680.0 Hz
mode 3 frequency = 1020.0 Hz

GATE loves thisA standing wave has nodes where the string never moves and antinodes where it moves most. Questions often ask for the number of nodes or the position of a node for a given mode. Use sin(nπx/L): its zeros inside (0, L) are the nodes.

Question: For a string with fixed ends, how many nodes are inside the string in mode 3?

Two. The mode sin(3πx/L) is zero at x = L/3 and x = 2L/3, inside the string. In general, mode n has n - 1 interior nodes.

Question: What is the second mode's frequency if L = 2 m and c = 100 m/s?

Use fn = nc/(2L). For n = 2: f2 = 2 × 100/(2 × 2) = 50 Hz.

Step 4: Laplace's equation and steady states

What steady means

Suppose you heat one side of a metal plate and keep the other sides at fixed temperatures. At first the temperature changes. After a long time, it stops changing. The final temperature pattern is a steady state. Because nothing changes with time, ut = 0, and the heat equation reduces to Laplace's equation:

uxx + uyy = 0

Checking that a function is harmonic

To check a candidate, compute the two second derivatives and add them. The sum must be zero for every point.

  • u = x2 - y2. uxx = 2 and uyy = -2. The sum is 0. Harmonic.
  • u = xy. uxx = 0 and uyy = 0. The sum is 0. Harmonic.
  • u = ex cos y. uxx = ex cos y and uyy = -ex cos y. The sum is 0. Harmonic.
  • u = x2 + y2. uxx = 2 and uyy = 2. The sum is 4, not 0. Not harmonic.

The maximum principle, in plain words

A steady temperature cannot have a hot spot in the middle of a plate unless the edges are hotter still. This is the maximum principle: for a harmonic function on a region, the largest and smallest values occur on the boundary. An interior point always lies between them.

Example: u = x2 - y2 on the square with -1 ≤ x ≤ 1 and -1 ≤ y ≤ 1. On the edges, the largest value is 1 (at x = ±1, y = 0), and the smallest is -1 (at x = 0, y = ±1). At the centre, u = 0, which lies between -1 and 1, as the principle predicts.

Key ideaLaplace's equation has no initial condition. The boundary values alone determine the steady state, and the extreme values of the answer are always found on the boundary.

Worked example 5: a square plate with one hot edge

Solve uxx + uyy = 0 on the unit square 0 < x < 1, 0 < y < 1, with u = 0 on the three edges x = 0, x = 1, and y = 0, and u(x, 1) = sin(πx) on the top edge.

  1. Assume a product. u = X(x)Y(y). Then X''Y + XY'' = 0, so X''/X = -Y''/Y = -λ.
  2. The x-part. X'' + λX = 0 with X(0) = X(1) = 0 gives λ = π2 and X = sin(πx).
  3. The y-part. Y'' = λY = π2Y, with Y(0) = 0. The solution that is zero at y = 0 is Y = sinh(πy). Here sinh is the hyperbolic sine, defined as (ez - e-z)/2.
  4. Combine and fit the top edge. u = C sin(πx) sinh(πy). At y = 1, u = C sinh(π) sin(πx). This must equal sin(πx), so C = 1/sinh(π).
  5. Answer. u(x, y) = sin(πx) sinh(πy)/sinh(π).

The program below evaluates this solution at two points, and checks that the Laplacian is zero using finite differences. A finite difference replaces a derivative by a difference of nearby values.

import math

def u(x, y):
    return math.sin(math.pi * x) * math.sinh(math.pi * y) / math.sinh(math.pi)

h = 1e-4

def laplacian(f, x, y):
    fxx = (f(x + h, y) - 2 * f(x, y) + f(x - h, y)) / h**2
    fyy = (f(x, y + h) - 2 * f(x, y) + f(x, y - h)) / h**2
    return fxx + fyy

print("u(0.5, 0.5) =", round(u(0.5, 0.5), 4))
print("u(0.5, 1.0) =", round(u(0.5, 1.0), 4))
print("Laplacian is near zero:", abs(laplacian(u, 0.3, 0.6)) < 1e-4)
u(0.5, 0.5) = 0.1993
u(0.5, 1.0) = 1.0
Laplacian is near zero: True

TrapFor Laplace's equation with separation, the signs flip. X'' + λX = 0 gives sines, but Y'' - λY = 0 gives sinh and cosh. Students often use sin for both directions, which is wrong for the y-part.

Question: Is u = x2 + xy - y2 harmonic?

Compute uxx = 2 and uyy = -2. The sum is 0, so yes, it is harmonic. The xy term has no second derivative contribution to Laplace's equation.

Question: Where is the largest value of a harmonic function on a bounded region?

On the boundary of the region. The maximum principle says an interior point cannot be larger than the largest boundary value (unless the function is constant).

Step 5: Boundary and initial conditions

Two common kinds of boundary condition

The boundary conditions tell the solution what happens at the edges. There are two main kinds:

  • Dirichlet condition. The value of u is fixed on the boundary. Example: a rod end held at 0 degrees, or a string end clamped to zero height. Written u(0, t) = 0.
  • Neumann condition. The rate of change of u across the boundary is fixed. Example: an insulated end of a rod, where no heat flows out. Written ux(0, t) = 0.

For an insulated rod with both ends insulated, the sine modes are replaced by cosine modes cos(nπx/L), with n = 0, 1, 2, .... The mode n = 0 is a constant. It never decays, because its eigenvalue is zero. As t grows, the temperature of an insulated rod settles to the average of its starting temperature. This is a useful fact for conceptual questions.

How many conditions do you need?

Question: How many initial conditions does the wave equation need?

Two: the initial displacement u(x, 0) and the initial velocity ut(x, 0). The wave equation is second order in time, so it needs two time conditions.

Question: Which of the three equations needs only one initial condition?

The heat equation, which has one time derivative. Its initial shape u(x, 0) is enough.

Solved GATE-style problems

These problems are original, written in the style of GATE questions. They are not past exam questions. Try each one on paper before you read the answer.

Wave speed from the equation

A string obeys utt = 36 uxx. What is the wave speed c? (Answer type: NAT, a whole number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The wave speed c.
  2. Which idea applies. Compare the equation with the standard wave form utt = c2uxx.
  3. Work it out. The coefficient of uxx is c2, so c2 = 36. Since speed is positive, c = 6.
  4. Check. 6 squared is 36, which matches.

Answer: c = 6.

Which functions are harmonic?

Select all the functions that satisfy Laplace's equation uxx + uyy = 0.

(A) x2 - y2
(B) xy
(C) x2 + y2
(D) ex cos y

Answer type: MSQ, select all that apply.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which functions have second derivatives that cancel when added.
  2. Which idea applies. Compute uxx and uyy for each function, then add them.
  3. Work it out. (A) 2 + (-2) = 0. Harmonic. (B) 0 + 0 = 0. Harmonic. (C) 2 + 2 = 4. Not harmonic. (D) ex cos y + (-ex cos y) = 0. Harmonic.
  4. Check. Only (C) has a positive sum, which matches the idea that x2 + y2 curves up in every direction.

Answer: (A), (B) and (D).

Heat decay of a single mode

A rod of length 1 with c = 1 has u(0, t) = u(1, t) = 0 and initial shape u(x, 0) = sin(2πx). Find u(1/4, 1/10), rounded to four decimal places. (Answer type: NAT, a decimal.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The temperature at x = 1/4 and t = 1/10.
  2. Which idea applies. The initial shape is one mode, with n = 2. Its decay exponent is -c2(2π)2t = -4π2t.
  3. Work it out. The solution is u = sin(2πx) e-4π2t. At x = 1/4, sin(2π/4) = sin(π/2) = 1. At t = 1/10, the exponent is -0.4π2, which is about -3.9478. So u = e-0.4π2, which is about 0.0193.
  4. Check. The program evaluates the exponential directly.
import math

print(round(math.exp(-4 * math.pi**2 * 0.1), 4))
0.0193

Answer: u(1/4, 1/10) = e-0.4π2, which is 0.0193 to four decimal places.

Frequency of the second mode

A string of length 0.5 m, with wave speed 340 m/s, is fixed at both ends. What is the frequency in Hz of mode n = 2? (Answer type: NAT, a whole number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The frequency of mode 2 in hertz.
  2. Which idea applies. For fixed ends, fn = nc/(2L).
  3. Work it out. f2 = 2 × 340/(2 × 0.5) = 680/1 = 680 Hz.
  4. Check. The program repeats the calculation.
print(2 * 340 / (2 * 0.5))
680.0

Two modes in one initial shape

A rod of length π with c = 1 has u(0, t) = u(π, t) = 0 and initial shape u(x, 0) = 3 sin x - 2 sin 2x. Find u(π/2, 1), rounded to four decimal places. (Answer type: NAT, a decimal.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The temperature at the middle of the rod, after time 1.
  2. Which idea applies. The initial shape is a sum of two modes, sin x (n = 1) and sin 2x (n = 2). Each mode decays with its own exponent. For length π, the mode sin(nx) has eigenvalue n2.
  3. Work it out. u = 3e-t sin x - 2e-4t sin 2x. At x = π/2, sin(π/2) = 1 and sin(π) = 0. So the second term vanishes, and u = 3e-t. At t = 1, u = 3e-1, which is about 1.1036.
  4. Check. The program evaluates 3e-1.
import math

print(round(3 * math.exp(-1), 4))
1.1036

Answer: u(π/2, 1) = 3e-1, which is 1.1036 to four decimal places.

Steady temperature in a square plate

On the unit square, u satisfies Laplace's equation with u = 0 on the edges x = 0, x = 1, and y = 0, and u(x, 1) = sin(πx) on the top edge. Find u(1/2, 1/2), rounded to four decimal places. (Answer type: NAT, a decimal.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The steady temperature at the centre of the plate.
  2. Which idea applies. Separation of variables for Laplace's equation, as in Worked example 5.
  3. Work it out. The solution is u = sin(πx) sinh(πy)/sinh(π). It is zero on the three edges and equals sin(πx) on the top. At x = 1/2 and y = 1/2, sin(π/2) = 1. So u = sinh(π/2)/sinh(π). Using sinh(π/2) ≈ 2.3013 and sinh(π) ≈ 11.5487, the ratio is about 0.1993.
  4. Check. The program evaluates the ratio. A value between 0 and 1 is expected, because the top edge reaches 1 and the centre is lower.
import math

print(round(math.sinh(math.pi / 2) / math.sinh(math.pi), 4))
0.1993

Answer: u(1/2, 1/2) = sinh(π/2)/sinh(π), which is 0.1993 to four decimal places.

  3. Work it out. (A) Every heat mode has a decay factor e-c2λnt with positive λn, so every mode goes to zero. True. (B) sin(2πx/L) at x = L/2 is sin(π) = 0. True. (C) Heat spreads out and smooths away detail. Running it backwards would amplify the high modes, which decay fastest. So reversal is not possible in this exact sense. False. (D) The maximum principle says the largest value is on the boundary, unless the function is constant. False.
  4. Check. Statement (C) fails because the high modes decay fastest, so reversing them would make them grow without limit. This is a physical reason, and it matches the mode analysis.

Answer: (A) and (B).

Exam-level 3: Classifying PDEs (MSQ, 2 marks)

Classify by B² − 4AC for Auxx + Buxy + Cuyy. Which are TRUE? (A) uxx + uyy = 0 is elliptic. (B) ut = uxx is parabolic. (C) utt = uxx is hyperbolic. (D) uxx + 2uxy + uyy = 0 is elliptic.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The type of each equation.
  2. Which idea applies. Negative discriminant: elliptic. Zero: parabolic. Positive: hyperbolic.
  3. Work it out. (A) 0 − 4 < 0. Elliptic. (B) Only one second derivative: B² − 4AC = 0. Parabolic. (C) 0 + 4 > 0. Hyperbolic. (D) 4 − 4 = 0. Parabolic, not elliptic.
  4. Check. (D) factors as (∂x + ∂y)²u = 0, a repeated real direction.

Answer: (A), (B) and (C).

Summary

Partial derivative
A derivative with respect to one variable while the others are held fixed.
Partial differential equation (PDE)
An equation with partial derivatives of a function of several variables.
Heat equation
ut = c2uxx. It has one time derivative and describes spreading.
Wave equation
utt = c2uxx. It has two time derivatives and describes vibration.
Laplace's equation
uxx + uyy = 0. It describes a steady state.
Separation of variables
Assume u = X(x)T(t) (or X(x)Y(y)) to turn a PDE into two ordinary equations.
Eigenvalue
A special constant that allows a non-zero solution to a boundary problem. For fixed ends, λn = (nπ/L)2.
Mode
One simple pattern of a system, indexed by n.
Fourier sine coefficient
bn = (2/L) ∫ f(x) sin(nπx/L) dx, used to match an initial shape.
Heat decay of a mode
Each mode decays like e-c2λnt, so higher modes vanish first.
Standing wave
A vibration pattern with fixed nodes and a shape that oscillates in place.
Node
A point that stays still during a standing wave.
Wave frequency
fn = nc/(2L) for a string with fixed ends.
Harmonic function
A function that satisfies Laplace's equation.
Maximum principle
A harmonic function reaches its largest and smallest values on the boundary.
Dirichlet condition
A boundary condition that fixes the value of u.
Neumann condition
A boundary condition that fixes the normal derivative, such as an insulated end.
Steady state
A solution that no longer changes with time, so ut = 0.

Remember these

  1. Heat needs one initial condition. The wave equation needs two. Laplace needs none, only boundary values.
  2. Heat modes decay, and higher modes decay fastest. Wave modes oscillate.
  3. For fixed ends, the modes are sin(nπx/L), with eigenvalues (nπ/L)2.
  4. For Laplace's equation, the y-part uses sinh, not sin.
  5. Harmonic functions reach their extremes on the boundary.
  6. Check a candidate solution by computing its second derivatives, not by guessing.
