Displacement, velocity, acceleration analysis of plane mechanisms

A machine linkage is a set of rigid bars joined by pins that move together, like the arm of a car windscreen wiper or the piston of an engine. To design it we need the velocity and acceleration of its points at one instant. This page teaches the relative velocity method, the instantaneous centre method, and acceleration, step by step, for GATE Robotics and Automation.

The idea in plain words

Welcome. Think about the windscreen wiper on a car. A motor turns a short arm, the arm is joined by a pin to a long rod, and that rod is joined by another pin to the wiper blade. Each piece is rigid, and the pins let the pieces turn relative to each other. The whole thing is a linkage. When the motor turns, every point on the linkage moves in a particular way. A designer wants to know how fast each point moves, and how quickly that speed changes. That is what kinematics is about.

The word "kinematics" means the study of motion without asking what forces cause it. In this topic we ask two questions. First, how fast does a point on a link move? That is the velocity. Second, how quickly does the velocity change? That is the acceleration. We only look at one instant, one picture of the mechanism, and we ask for the speeds and accelerations in that picture.

Here is a simple everyday analogy. Imagine you are holding one end of a stick and a friend holds the other end. If you spin your end, the friend's end moves too, but the speed of their end depends on how far it is from the point you are spinning around. Points far from the pivot move faster. A linkage is many sticks joined together, and the same rule applies to every pair of connected points.

Why does this matter for GATE? In Robotics and Automation (RA) papers, robot arms are linkages, and their joints are designed by checking velocities and accelerations. GATE questions usually give a mechanism at one instant, with its link lengths, angles and one known angular velocity, then ask for the velocity of a point, the angular velocity of a link, or the acceleration of a point. Three methods appear: the relative velocity method, the instantaneous centre method, and the relative acceleration equation. We learn each one here.

Key terms

Read this list now, and come back to it whenever a word feels unfamiliar. Each entry gives the plain meaning first, then the precise definition.

Link
A rigid bar in a mechanism. Plain meaning: one stick in the linkage. Precise definition: a rigid body that is part of a mechanism and connected to other links at joints.
Joint (pin or revolute joint)
A connection that lets two links rotate relative to each other about a common pin. Plain meaning: a hinge. Precise definition: a joint allowing one rotational degree of freedom between two links.
Ground (frame)
The fixed link of the mechanism, which does not move. Plain meaning: the table the machine is bolted to. Precise definition: the link that is taken as stationary for the analysis.
Four-bar linkage
A mechanism of four links joined by four pins, with one link fixed. Plain meaning: four sticks in a loop. Precise definition: a closed kinematic chain of four rigid links connected by four revolute joints.
Slider-crank mechanism
A mechanism with a crank, a connecting rod and a slider that moves in a straight guide. Plain meaning: the engine piston mechanism. Precise definition: a mechanism in which a rotating crank drives a sliding block through a connecting rod.
Crank
The link that rotates fully about a fixed pivot. Plain meaning: the arm that goes round. Precise definition: a link that makes complete revolutions about a fixed pivot.
Coupler
The link between two moving links, which is not fixed. Plain meaning: the middle link that connects the parts. Precise definition: the floating link of a four-bar mechanism.
Displacement
The change of position of a point. Plain meaning: how far it has moved. Precise definition: the vector from the initial to the final position of the point.
Velocity (v)
The rate of change of position with time. Plain meaning: how fast and in which direction a point moves. Precise definition: v = d(position) / dt, a vector in m/s.
Acceleration (a)
The rate of change of velocity with time. Plain meaning: how quickly the velocity changes. Precise definition: a = d(velocity) / dt, a vector in m/s².
Angular velocity (omega)
The rate at which a link turns. Plain meaning: how fast the link rotates. Precise definition: omega = d(theta) / dt, in rad/s, positive when counter-clockwise by convention.
Angular acceleration (alpha)
The rate of change of angular velocity. Plain meaning: how quickly the spin speeds up or slows down. Precise definition: alpha = d(omega) / dt, in rad/s².
Relative velocity
The velocity of one point as seen from another point on the same rigid link. Plain meaning: how one end moves compared with the other. Precise definition: the velocity of B relative to A, vBA, which for points on one link is perpendicular to the line AB.
Relative acceleration
The acceleration of one point compared with another on the same rigid link. Plain meaning: how one end accelerates compared with the other. Precise definition: aBA = alpha × rBA − omega² rBA, with two components.
Instantaneous centre (IC)
The point in the plane where two links have the same velocity at this instant. Plain meaning: the point that does not move for the moment, as seen from one link. Precise definition: a point about which a rigid body is rotating at the instant, with zero velocity of the body at that point.
Kennedy's theorem
For three bodies moving in a plane, their three instantaneous centres lie on one straight line. Plain meaning: the three special points line up. Precise definition: for any three bodies, the three pairwise instantaneous centres are collinear.
Radial (centripetal) acceleration
The part of acceleration pointing towards the centre of rotation, with magnitude omega² r. Plain meaning: the pull inward that keeps a point going round in a circle. Precise definition: the component of acceleration along the line joining the point to the pivot, directed towards the pivot.
Tangential acceleration
The part of acceleration perpendicular to the radius, with magnitude alpha r. Plain meaning: the change in speed along the circle. Precise definition: the component of acceleration perpendicular to the line joining the point to the pivot.
Velocity diagram
A drawing of velocity vectors to scale, used to find unknown velocities. Plain meaning: a picture where arrows show speeds. Precise definition: a vector polygon whose sides represent the velocities of the points.
Degree of freedom
An independent way in which the mechanism can move. Plain meaning: one input that you can set. Precise definition: the number of independent inputs needed to fix the position of a mechanism.

Concept 1: Links, joints and drawing a mechanism

Before any calculation, you must read the mechanism. A four-bar linkage has four links. One link is ground, which is fixed. The two links joined to ground are the crank (which may turn fully) and the rocker (which swings). The link between them is the coupler, which floats. A slider-crank replaces the rocker with a slider that moves in a straight guide, like an engine piston.

Here is a text drawing of the four-bar linkage that we will use in this page. The pivot O2 is the crank pivot, O4 is the rocker pivot, and both are on the ground. The crank is O2A, the coupler is AB, and the rocker is O4B. Coordinates are in metres, with O2 at the origin.

   |  |                          \
   |  |  crank O2A (length 2)     \  rocker O4B (length 3)
   |  |                            \
   |  |   coupler AB joins A and B  \
   |  |                              \
   O2 +-------------------------------- O4
   +-------------------------------------> x

   O2 = (0, 0) ground pivot, O4 = (4, 0) ground pivot.
   Crank length 2, rocker length 3, coupler AB floats.
   Sketch only: the crank is vertical, so A is directly above O2.

The drawing is only a sketch. The numbers in the example are chosen so that every step can be checked by hand. The crank is vertical, the pin A is at (0, 2), and the rocker pin B is at (1.6, 1.8). The distance from O4 to B is exactly 3, which matches the rocker length.

TipBefore any calculation, write down the coordinates of every pivot and every pin, and check the link lengths with Pythagoras. A wrong length is the most common reason an answer is wrong.

Question 1: What is the difference between the crank and the rocker in a four-bar linkage?

The crank can turn fully about its fixed pivot. The rocker only swings back and forth through a limited angle.

Question 2: Which link of a four-bar linkage is not connected to ground?

The coupler. It floats between the crank pin and the rocker pin.

Concept 2: Displacement of a slider-crank mechanism

Displacement tells us where a point is. For a slider-crank, the important output is the slider position. Let the crank have length r, the connecting rod have length l, and the crank angle from the line of stroke be theta. Measure the slider position x from the crank centre. The geometry gives:

x = r cos θ + square root of (l² − r² sin² θ)

Let us say what each symbol means. x is the slider position, in m, measured from the crank centre along the line of stroke. r is the crank radius, in m. l is the connecting rod length, in m. theta is the crank angle, in radians or degrees. The first term is the crank's own horizontal reach. The square-root term is the rod's contribution.

        crank pin
          *  ___
         /    r \           crank of length r, angle theta from the stroke line
        /        \
   ----O----------+------ slider at x (rod of length l connects crank pin and slider)
   crank centre              |<-------- x -------->|

   For the position: x = r cos(theta) + sqrt(l^2 - r^2 sin^2(theta))

The velocity follows by differentiating with respect to time. Because the crank turns at a steady angular velocity omega, the velocity of the slider is v = omega × dx / dθ. The derivative is:

v = − r omega sin θ − r² omega sin 2θ / (2 square root of (l² − r² sin² θ))

At a crank angle of 90 degrees, sin 2θ = 0, so the second term vanishes. The velocity is then simply v = −r omega. The minus sign means the slider moves towards the crank centre, which is the usual sign convention for this configuration.

Worked example. A slider-crank has crank radius r = 0.1 m, rod length l = 0.4 m, and angular velocity omega = 10 rad/s. At theta = 90 degrees, find the slider velocity.

  1. Use v = − r omega sin θ − r² omega sin 2θ / (2S), with S = √(l² − r² sin² θ).
  2. At 90 degrees, sin θ = 1 and sin 2θ = 0.
  3. So v = − 0.1 × 10 × 1 = −1.0 m/s.
  4. The slider moves at 1.0 m/s towards the crank centre.

TrapThe angle theta in the velocity formula must be in radians when you use the derivative directly. Most GATE problems give the angle in degrees; convert it before using sine or cosine of a double angle.

Question 3: At theta = 0, where is the slider relative to the crank centre?

At x = r + l, the farthest position. The crank and rod are in a straight line, so the slider is at its full extension.

Question 4: At theta = 90 degrees, what is the slider velocity in terms of r and omega?

v = −r omega. The slider moves at the crank pin speed, because the rod is momentarily perpendicular to the stroke line.

Concept 3: Velocity by the relative velocity method

This is the workhorse method. Its key idea is simple: the velocity of one point on a rigid body equals the velocity of another point on that body, plus the velocity of the first point relative to the second. Written as an equation:

vB = vA + vBA

Each symbol has a plain meaning. vB is the velocity of point B. vA is the velocity of point A, which may be known. vBA is the velocity of B as seen from A. For two points on one rigid link, vBA is perpendicular to the line AB, and its size is omegaAB × AB, where omegaAB is the angular velocity of the link.

For a point on a crank that turns about its fixed pivot, the velocity is simply v = omega × r, perpendicular to the crank, where r is the distance from the pivot. This is the first velocity we know. In our four-bar example, the crank pin A is at (0, 2) and the pivot O2 is at the origin. The crank turns counter-clockwise at omega2 = 2 rad/s, so the velocity of A is perpendicular to the crank, which is horizontal:

vA = omega2 × (length of crank) = 2 × 2 = 4 m/s, directed to the left, so vA = (−4, 0) m/s

Now the rocker pin B is on the rocker O4B, which swings about the fixed pivot O4 at (4, 0). So the velocity of B must be perpendicular to O4B. Also B is on the coupler, which joins it to A. So:

vB = vA + vBA, with vB perpendicular to O4B and vBA perpendicular to AB

We know the direction of both unknown vectors, so we can solve. Let us do it in components, step by step.

  1. The direction of O4B is (B − O4) = (1.6 − 4, 1.8) = (−2.4, 1.8). A perpendicular direction is (1.8, 2.4), which simplifies to (3, 4). So vB is a multiple of (3, 4).
  2. The direction of AB is (B − A) = (1.6, −0.2). A perpendicular direction is (0.2, 1.6), which simplifies to (1, 8). So vBA is a multiple of (1, 8), say μ(1, 8).
  3. Write vB = (−4, 0) + μ(1, 8) = (−4 + μ, 8μ).
  4. Require vB to be a multiple of (3, 4): (−4 + μ)/3 = 8μ/4, which gives 4(−4 + μ) = 24μ, so −16 = 20μ and μ = −0.8.
  5. So vB = (−4 − 0.8, −6.4) = (−4.8, −6.4) m/s, and vBA = (−0.8, −6.4) m/s.
  6. The coupler angular velocity: |vBA| = 0.8 × √65 = 6.45 m/s, and AB = √2.6 = 1.612 m, so omega3 = 6.45 / 1.612 = 4.0 rad/s in magnitude.
  7. The rocker angular velocity: |vB| = √(4.8² + 6.4²) = 8.0 m/s, and O4B = 3 m, so omega4 = 8.0 / 3 = 2.67 rad/s.
import math
O2 = (0.0, 0.0)
O4 = (4.0, 0.0)
A = (0.0, 2.0)
B = (1.6, 1.8)
IC = (0.0, 3.0)
w2 = 2.0
vA = (-w2 * A[1], w2 * A[0])
w3 = vA[0] / (-(A[1] - IC[1]))
vB = (w3 * -(B[1] - IC[1]), w3 * (B[0] - IC[0]))
w4 = vB[0] / (-(B[1] - O4[1]))
print("vA =", (round(vA[0], 2), round(vA[1], 2)), "m/s")
print("w3 =", round(w3, 2), "rad/s")
print("vB =", (round(vB[0], 2), round(vB[1], 2)), "m/s")
print("w4 =", round(w4, 2), "rad/s")
IB = math.hypot(B[0] - IC[0], B[1] - IC[1])
print("IB =", round(IB, 2), "m")
print("speed of B =", round(math.hypot(*vB), 2), "m/s")

vA = (-4.0, 0.0) m/s
w3 = -4.0 rad/s
vB = (-4.8, -6.4) m/s
w4 = 2.67 rad/s
IB = 2.0 m
speed of B = 8.0 m/s

The Python block also computes the instantaneous centre, which the next concept explains. It gives the same velocity of B, so the two methods agree. The negative sign of omega3 means the coupler turns clockwise, which is opposite to the sign convention for the crank.

Key ideaWrite the relative velocity equation with the known velocity first. Then use the perpendicular directions to find the unknown velocities. Always check the answer by a second route.

TrapForgetting that vBA is perpendicular to AB, not to the crank. The perpendicular is always to the line joining the two points on the same link.

Question 5: Why is vBA perpendicular to AB?

Because A and B are on the same rigid link. The distance between them does not change, so the only motion of B relative to A is a rotation about A, which is perpendicular to AB.

Question 6: What is the velocity of a point 0.5 m from the pivot of a link turning at 4 rad/s?

v = omega × r = 4 × 0.5 = 2 m/s, perpendicular to the radius.

Concept 4: Velocity by the instantaneous centre method

The instantaneous centre is a point where a body has zero velocity at this instant. Think of a bicycle wheel rolling on the road. At the instant the tyre touches the road, the contact point has zero velocity. The wheel is rotating about that point, and every other point on the wheel moves as if the wheel were rotating about it. The contact point is the instantaneous centre of the wheel relative to the road.

For a rigid link, if you know the instantaneous centre I and the angular velocity omega of the link, then the velocity of any point P on that link is:

vP = omega × IP, perpendicular to the line IP

The size is omega times the distance IP. The direction is perpendicular to IP, in the sense of the rotation.

How do we find the instantaneous centre of the coupler? Use the rule of three centres. The crank and the coupler share pin A, so their instantaneous centre is A. The coupler and the rocker share pin B, so their instantaneous centre is B. The crank is pinned to ground at O2, and the rocker is pinned to ground at O4. The instantaneous centre of the coupler with the ground is the third one, and Kennedy's theorem gives its position: it is where the line through O2 and A meets the line through O4 and B. In our example, the line through O2 and A is the vertical line x = 0. The line through O4 and B has direction (−2.4, 1.8), which is the same as (−4, 3). Going from O4 in that direction until x = 0 takes us to the point (0, 3). So the coupler's instantaneous centre is at I = (0, 3). Here is the text picture.

   y
   ^
 3 |  I = (0, 3)   instantaneous centre of the coupler
   |  |
 2 |  A (0, 2)      line O2-A-I is the vertical line x = 0
   |  |  \
   |  |   \  coupler AB
   |  |    \
   |  |     B (1.6, 1.8)    line O4-B-I passes through B and I
   |  |    /
   O2 +---------------------O4
   +-------------------------------> x

   Line 1: O2 to A, extended to I: the vertical line x = 0.
   Line 2: O4 to B, extended to I: direction (-4, 3). The two lines meet at I.

Now use the IC. The velocity of the coupler point A is given by the crank, vA = (−4, 0) m/s. Since A is on the coupler and the coupler rotates about I, vA must be perpendicular to IA. The distance IA = 3 − 2 = 1 m. So omega3 = vA / IA = 4 / 1 = 4 rad/s, with the sense that moves A to the left. The sign convention gives omega3 = −4 rad/s (clockwise), matching the relative velocity result.

Then the velocity of B is vB = omega3 × IB. The distance IB = √(1.6² + 1.2²) = √4 = 2 m. So |vB| = 4 × 2 = 8 m/s, which again agrees with the relative velocity method.

GATE loves thisUse the IC method when a question asks for the angular velocity of a coupler or the velocity of a point on it. Always check the IC by a second method, such as the relative velocity equation, because a small slip in drawing the lines changes the answer.

TrapUsing the distance from a pivot instead of the distance from the instantaneous centre. The velocity of a point on a rigid body is omega times the distance from the IC, not from the fixed pivot.

Question 7: If the coupler's instantaneous centre is at a distance 2 m from B and the coupler turns at 4 rad/s, what is the speed of B?

vB = 4 × 2 = 8 m/s.

Question 8: What does Kennedy's theorem say about three bodies?

The three instantaneous centres of the three pairs of bodies lie on one straight line.

Concept 5: Acceleration by the relative acceleration equation

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. For a point moving on a circle of radius r about a fixed pivot, with the link having angular velocity omega and angular acceleration alpha, the acceleration has two parts:

  • Radial part, along the radius towards the pivot, of size omega² r. This is the centripetal acceleration, the pull that keeps the point on its circle.
  • Tangential part, perpendicular to the radius, of size alpha r. This is the change of speed along the circle.

Points on the same rigid link obey the relative acceleration equation:

aB = aA + aBA, where aBA = alpha × rBA − omega² rBA

Here rBA is the vector from A to B. The first term, alpha × rBA, is the tangential part, perpendicular to AB. The second term, −omega² rBA, is the radial part, directed from B towards A. Each of these has a known direction, which is what makes the problem solvable.

The acceleration of a point on a crank is particularly simple. If the crank pivot is fixed and the crank turns at omega with angular acceleration alpha, then the crank pin has radial acceleration omega² r towards the pivot, and tangential acceleration alpha r perpendicular to the crank.

For the slider-crank of Concept 2, the slider acceleration at 90 degrees can be found by differentiating the velocity once more with respect to time. At 90 degrees the sin 2θ terms vanish, and the result is the term r²/S, where S = √(l² − r²). The result is:

a = omega² r² / √(l² − r²) at θ = 90°

Worked example. Take r = 0.1 m, l = 0.4 m and omega = 10 rad/s, at theta = 90 degrees. Find the slider acceleration.

  1. Compute l² − r² = 0.16 − 0.01 = 0.15.
  2. Compute √0.15 = 0.3873 m.
  3. Compute omega² r² = 100 × 0.01 = 1.0 m²/s⁴.
  4. Divide: a = 1.0 / 0.3873 = 2.58 m/s². The acceleration is positive, which is opposite to the velocity of −1.0 m/s from Concept 2, so the slider is slowing down at this instant.
import math
r, l, w = 0.1, 0.4, 10.0
def x(th):
    return r * math.cos(th) + math.sqrt(l**2 - (r * math.sin(th))**2)
th = math.pi / 2
h = 1e-5
dx = (x(th + h) - x(th - h)) / (2 * h)
d2x = (x(th + h) - 2 * x(th) + x(th - h)) / h**2
print("slider velocity =", round(w * dx, 2), "m/s")
print("slider acceleration =", round(w**2 * d2x, 2), "m/s^2")

slider velocity = -1.0 m/s
slider acceleration = 2.58 m/s^2

Key ideaAcceleration always has a radial part pointing to the pivot, and this part is often the larger one. Do not forget it, because many GATE answers miss it.

TrapWriting the radial acceleration as omega r instead of omega² r. The radial term has omega squared, because acceleration has units of length per time squared.

Question 9: What is the radial acceleration of a point 0.2 m from a pivot, on a link turning at 5 rad/s?

omega² r = 25 × 0.2 = 5 m/s², directed towards the pivot.

Question 10: Which part of a point's acceleration is zero when the link turns at a constant speed?

The tangential part alpha r is zero, because alpha is zero. The radial part omega² r remains.

Concept 6: A routine for velocity and acceleration numericals

Most GATE mechanism questions follow the same route. Use this checklist, and the answer comes out in order.

  1. Sketch and label. Mark the ground pivots, the link lengths, the crank angle and the known angular velocity.
  2. Find the known velocity. Compute the velocity of the point on the driven crank, v = omega × r, and its direction.
  3. Write the relative velocity equation. Use vB = vA + vBA, with the perpendicular directions for each unknown.
  4. Solve the unknowns. Use components, or a scale velocity diagram.
  5. Check with a second method. The instantaneous centre method is a good check for velocities.
  6. Move to acceleration. Use the relative acceleration equation with radial and tangential parts. Remember omega² in the radial term.

Notice that the relative acceleration equation has two unknown magnitudes, the tangential part of the coupler and the tangential part of the rocker, and two unknown directions. In practice the radial parts are known, and the unknowns are found by component equations, just as with velocity.

TipIn a GATE numerical, write the directions of every vector before you put in any numbers. Half of the marks in a mechanism question come from getting the directions right.

Solved GATE-style problems

These problems are original GATE-style questions written for practice. They are not past exam questions. Try each one on paper first.

Statements about the instantaneous centre

Which of the following statements about the instantaneous centre of a link are correct? Select all that apply.

(A) At the instantaneous centre, the velocity of the link is zero.
(B) The velocity of a point on the link is proportional to its distance from the instantaneous centre.
(C) The instantaneous centre is always at a fixed pivot.
(D) The velocity of a point is parallel to the line joining it to the instantaneous centre.

Select all that apply.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements are correct. This is an MSQ.
  2. Which idea applies. The definition of the instantaneous centre, and vP = omega × IP.
  3. Work it out. (A) is true, by definition. (B) is true, because the size is omega times IP. (C) is false, because the coupler's IC is generally not at a fixed pivot. (D) is false, because the velocity is perpendicular to IP.
  4. Check. The two true statements describe the size and the zero point, and the two false ones use the wrong geometry.

Answer: (A) and (B) only.

Velocity of a crank pin

A crank of length 0.05 m turns at 20 rad/s about a fixed pivot. What is the speed of the crank pin, in m/s? This is a NAT question.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The crank pin speed in m/s.
  2. Which idea applies. v = omega × r.
  3. Work it out. v = 20 × 0.05 = 1.0 m/s.
  4. Check. A point at 0.05 m from the pivot, spinning at 20 rad/s, moves at 1 m/s, which is a sensible size for a small crank.

Answer: 1.0 m/s.

Slider velocity at 90 degrees

A slider-crank has crank radius 0.1 m, connecting rod length 0.4 m, and the crank turns at 10 rad/s. At the instant the crank is at 90 degrees, what is the speed of the slider, in m/s? This is a NAT question.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The slider speed, as a magnitude, in m/s.
  2. Which idea applies. At 90 degrees, v = −r omega, so the speed is r omega.
  3. Work it out. Speed = 0.1 × 10 = 1.0 m/s.
  4. Check. The rod length does not appear, because at 90 degrees the second term in the velocity formula vanishes.

Answer: 1.0 m/s.

Speed of a point on a coupler

In the four-bar linkage of this page, the coupler turns at 4 rad/s about its instantaneous centre, which lies 2 m from point B. What is the speed of B, in m/s? This is a NAT question.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The speed of B.
  2. Which idea applies. vB = omega3 × IB.
  3. Work it out. vB = 4 × 2 = 8 m/s.
  4. Check. The same value came from the relative velocity method in Concept 3.

Answer: 8 m/s.

Which velocities are perpendicular to what

For a four-bar linkage, which statements about velocities are correct? Select all that apply.

(A) The velocity of the crank pin is perpendicular to the crank.
(B) The relative velocity vBA is perpendicular to the line AB.
(C) The velocity of the rocker pin is parallel to the rocker.
(D) The velocity of the crank pin is parallel to the crank.

Select all that apply.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements are correct. This is an MSQ.
  2. Which idea applies. The direction of velocities about a pivot and along a link.
  3. Work it out. (A) is true: a point on a rotating crank moves perpendicular to its radius. (B) is true, as shown in Concept 3. (C) is false: the rocker pin moves perpendicular to the rocker, because the rocker rotates about O4. (D) is false, for the same reason as (A).
  4. Check. Perpendicular motion about a pivot is the rule for any rotating point, so (A) and (B) are the correct pair.

Answer: (A) and (B) only.

Angular velocity of the rocker

In the four-bar linkage of this page, the crank O2A has length 2 m and turns counter-clockwise at 2 rad/s. The rocker O4B has length 3 m, and the instantaneous centre of the coupler is at (0, 3). Find the magnitude of the angular velocity of the rocker, in rad/s, to two decimal places. This is a NAT question.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The magnitude of omega4 in rad/s.
  2. Which idea applies. Find vB from the IC, then omega4 = |vB| / O4B.
  3. Work it out. vA = 2 × 2 = 4 m/s, and omega3 = 4 rad/s from IA = 1 m. The speed of B is 4 × IB = 4 × 2 = 8 m/s. Then omega4 = 8 / 3 = 2.67 rad/s.
  4. Check. The Python block in Concept 3 prints w4 = 2.67 rad/s, and it matches this hand value.

Answer: 2.67 rad/s.

Slider acceleration at 90 degrees

A slider-crank has crank radius 0.2 m and connecting rod length 0.5 m. The crank turns at a constant speed of 5 rad/s. At the instant the crank is at 90 degrees, find the magnitude of the slider acceleration, in m/s², to two decimal places. Use a = omega² r² / √(l² − r²). This is a NAT question.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The slider acceleration magnitude in m/s².
  2. Which idea applies. The slider acceleration formula at 90 degrees.
  3. Work it out. omega² r² = 25 × 0.04 = 1.0. l² − r² = 0.25 − 0.04 = 0.21, and √0.21 = 0.45826. So a = 1.0 / 0.45826 = 2.18 m/s².
  4. Check. The acceleration is positive, and about 2 m/s² is consistent with a crank of radius 0.2 m at 5 rad/s. The Python block below confirms the number.

Answer: 2.18 m/s².

Statements about acceleration of rotating points

A link rotates about a fixed pivot with angular velocity omega and angular acceleration alpha. Which statements about a point P on the link at distance r from the pivot are correct? Select all that apply.

(A) The radial acceleration of P is omega² r and points towards the pivot.
(B) The tangential acceleration of P is alpha r and is perpendicular to the radius.
(C) If omega is constant, the tangential acceleration is zero.
(D) The radial acceleration is proportional to omega, not omega².

Select all that apply.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements are correct. This is an MSQ.
  2. Which idea applies. The radial and tangential parts of acceleration on a circle.
  3. Work it out. (A) is true. (B) is true. (C) is true, because alpha is zero when omega is constant. (D) is false, because the radial part is proportional to omega squared.
  4. Check. Three statements match the standard parts of circular motion, and (D) confuses the power of omega.

Answer: (A), (B) and (C) only.

Radial acceleration of a rotating pin

A pin is 0.3 m from the pivot of a link that turns at 4 rad/s. What is the radial acceleration of the pin, in m/s²? This is a NAT question.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The radial acceleration in m/s².
  2. Which idea applies. Radial acceleration = omega² r.
  3. Work it out. omega² = 16, and 16 × 0.3 = 4.8 m/s².
  4. Check. Squaring the angular velocity makes the answer larger than omega times r, which is 1.2 m/s, as expected.

Answer: 4.8 m/s².

How many instantaneous centres does a six link mechanism have?

Exam-level 2: Coriolis acceleration (NAT, 2 marks)

A slider moves outward at 2 m/s along a link rotating at 5 rad/s. Find the Coriolis acceleration.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. 2ωv.
  2. Which idea applies. The Coriolis component appears when a point slides on a rotating link.
  3. Work it out. 2 · 5 · 2 = 20 m/s².
  4. Check. It acts perpendicular to the link.

Answer: 20 m/s².

Exam-level 3: Four bar velocity (NAT, 2 marks)

In a four bar linkage, the crank (40 mm) turns at 10 rad/s. At an instant, the IC method gives the distance from the coupler's IC to the crank pin as 120 mm and to the rocker pin as 90 mm. The rocker is 60 mm. Find the rocker angular speed.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. ωrocker.
  2. Which idea applies. Pin speeds about the coupler IC: v = ωcr.
  3. Work it out. vA = 0.4 m/s, ωcoupler = 0.4/0.12 = 3.333 rad/s. vB = 3.333 · 0.09 = 0.3 m/s. ωrocker = 0.3/0.06 = 5 rad/s.
  4. Check. Units check: m/s divided by m.

Answer: 5 rad/s.

Summary

Link
A rigid bar in a mechanism. Precise definition: a rigid body joined to others at pins.
Four-bar linkage
Four links in a loop, one fixed. Precise definition: a closed chain of four revolute joints.
Slider-crank
Crank, rod and slider. Precise definition: rotary motion converted to straight-line sliding.
Velocity
Rate of change of position. Precise definition: v = d(position)/dt.
Acceleration
Rate of change of velocity. Precise definition: a = d(velocity)/dt.
Relative velocity equation
Velocity of one point equals another plus the relative part. Precise definition: vB = vA + vBA.
Relative acceleration equation
Acceleration of one point equals another plus the relative part. Precise definition: aB = aA + aBA.
Instantaneous centre
Point with zero velocity for a body at this instant. Precise definition: velocity of P equals omega times IP.
Kennedy's theorem
Three instantaneous centres of three bodies are collinear. Precise definition: the three ICs lie on one straight line.
Radial acceleration
Pull towards the pivot. Precise definition: omega² r.
Tangential acceleration
Change of speed along the circle. Precise definition: alpha r.
Angular velocity
Rate of turning. Precise definition: omega = d(theta)/dt.
Angular acceleration
Rate of change of turning speed. Precise definition: alpha = d(omega)/dt.
Coupler
Floating link between two moving links. Precise definition: the link not connected to ground.
Slider position
Distance of the slider from the crank centre. Precise definition: x = r cos θ + √(l² − r² sin² θ).

Remember these

  1. v = omega × r, perpendicular to the radius, for a point on a link turning about a fixed pivot.
  2. In the relative velocity equation, vBA is perpendicular to AB.
  3. The instantaneous centre has zero velocity, and vP = omega × IP.
  4. Kennedy's theorem: three instantaneous centres lie on one line.
  5. Acceleration has a radial part omega² r towards the pivot, and a tangential part alpha r.
  6. Always check a velocity answer by a second method.
  7. At 90 degrees, the slider speed is r omega, and the acceleration is omega² r² / √(l² − r²).
