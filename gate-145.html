Kinematics of rigid bodies in plane motion

A rigid body is a solid object whose shape never changes. This topic shows how every point on such a body moves when the body slides and spins in a flat plane, using only angles, distances and speeds. It is the first step before forces are added, and GATE Robotics questions use it for wheels, links and rotating arms.

The idea in plain words

Think of a dinner plate on a table. You can slide it across the table, spin it around its middle, or do both at once while it skids and turns. In every case the plate keeps its shape. Its edge never bends. A solid object that keeps its shape like this is a rigid body.

Describing how a body moves, without asking what forces cause the motion, is called kinematics. Kinematics answers three questions: how fast is each point moving, how quickly is the body turning, and where will the body be a moment later? Forces come later.

Everyday analogy. A coin rolling on a table moves forward in a straight line and turns over and over at the same time. The point touching the table seems to stay still for a moment. Kinematics gives you the tools to describe all of this with numbers.

Why it matters. A robot arm is a chain of rigid links. A mobile robot wheel is a rigid disc. Before a designer can choose a motor, they must know how fast each point of each link moves and speeds up. Those speeds are found with the methods on this page.

Where it appears in GATE. In the Robotics and Automation (RA) paper you may be asked for the velocity of a point on a rotating or rolling body, the angular velocity of a link, or the acceleration of the contact point of a rolling wheel. The questions are short, but signs and directions matter.

Key terms

Read this list once now, and come back to it whenever a word feels unfamiliar.

Rigid body
A solid object whose distances between points never change. Precisely: the distance between any two points of the body stays constant during motion.
Kinematics
The part of mechanics that describes motion (position, velocity, acceleration) without considering the forces that cause it.
Plane motion
Motion in which every point moves in a plane parallel to a fixed plane. We can draw the body as a flat shape and work in x and y.
Translation
Motion in which the body moves but does not turn. Plain meaning: a drawer sliding in and out. Precisely: every line in the body keeps its original direction.
Rotation about a fixed axis
Motion in which one line of the body stays still and the rest turns around it. Plain meaning: a door on its hinges.
General plane motion
Motion in a plane that is neither pure translation nor pure rotation about a fixed point. Plain meaning: a rolling coin.
Position vector rB/A
The arrow from point A to point B on the same body. Read it as "r of B relative to A".
Angular velocity (ω)
How fast the body turns, in radians per second (rad/s). Precisely: ω = dθ/dt, where θ is the angle.
Angular acceleration (α)
How fast the angular velocity changes, in rad/s². Precisely: α = dω/dt.
Counterclockwise positive
The sign convention on this page. A counterclockwise turn gives a positive ω, and a clockwise turn gives a negative ω.
Cross product (in 2D)
A way of multiplying a turning rate and a position arrow to get a velocity arrow at right angles to the position arrow.
Relative velocity
The velocity of one point as seen from another point on the same body. Precisely: vB = vA + ω × rB/A.
Instantaneous center of zero velocity (IC)
The point whose velocity is zero at this instant. For this moment, the body turns about that point.
Rolling without slipping
A wheel touches the ground and the contact point does not skid. Precisely: the velocity of the contact point is zero.
Tangential and normal parts
The two parts of a point's acceleration on a circle. The tangential part is along the circle and changes the speed. The normal part points to the centre and changes the direction (also called centripetal acceleration).

Three kinds of plane motion

Why we sort motion into types

A wheel rolling down a road seems to do many things at once. Engineers simplify by sorting every plane motion into one of three types. Once you know the type, you know which formula to use.

Type 1: translation

In translation the whole body moves in the same way. Any two points have the same velocity and the same acceleration at every instant. The body does not turn, so ω = 0.

  Translation: the block moves, but its edges stay parallel

   ____          ____
  |    |  --->  |    |   each point moves by the same amount
  |____|        |____|   edges keep the same direction

Type 2: rotation about a fixed axis

One line of the body, the axis, stays in place. Every other point moves on a circle around it. A point at distance r from the axis moves on a circle of radius r.

        axis O (fixed, does not move)
             O
            /|\
           / | \      point P is at distance r from O
          /  |  \     P moves on a circle of radius r
         /   |   \
            P

Type 3: general plane motion

General plane motion is everything else in a plane. A rolling wheel is the classic example. Its centre moves along a straight line, and the wheel also turns. There is no fixed axis.

A useful fact holds for all three types: at any single instant, every point of a rigid body in plane motion has the same ω and the same α. Only the linear velocities and accelerations of the points differ.

Key ideaAsk two questions. Does the body turn at all? Is there a fixed axis? The answers tell you the type of motion and the right method.

If a body is in pure translation, what is its angular velocity?

It is zero. Nothing turns, so ω = 0 and all points have the same velocity.

Is a rolling coin in translation, in rotation about a fixed axis, or in general plane motion?

General plane motion. Its centre moves in a straight line and the coin also turns, with no fixed axis.

Angular velocity and angular acceleration

Measuring turning with an angle

Suppose a line on the body makes angle θ with a reference line. As the body turns, θ changes with time. The rate of change is the angular velocity.

ω = dθ/dt

Here θ is in radians, t is in seconds and ω is in rad/s. The symbol d means "a small change in". If a line turns through 2 radians in 1 second, then ω = 2 rad/s. The angular acceleration tells how ω itself changes.

α = dω/dt

If ω goes from 2 rad/s to 6 rad/s in 2 seconds, then α = (6 − 2)/2 = 2 rad/s².

From angular speed to linear speed

A point at distance r from the axis travels a circle. Its speed is the arc it covers per second. One full turn takes 2π/ω seconds and covers a length 2πr, so the speed is v = ωr.

v = ω r

Here v is the speed in m/s, ω the angular speed in rad/s, and r the distance from the axis in metres.

Worked example. A wheel of radius 0.3 m turns at ω = 10 rad/s. A point on its rim moves at v = 10 × 0.3 = 3 m/s. A point halfway out, at r = 0.15 m, moves at 1.5 m/s.

r = 0.3                  # radius of the wheel in metres
w = 10                   # angular velocity in rad/s
v = r * w                # speed of a point on the rim
print(f"speed = {v:.1f} m/s")
speed = 3.0 m/s

Tangential and normal acceleration of a point

A point on a circle has two acceleration parts at right angles to each other.

  • Tangential part: at = αr. It is along the circle and changes the speed.
  • Normal part: an = ω²r. It points toward the axis and changes the direction.

The total magnitude is a = √(at² + an²), which is Pythagoras at work.

Worked example. Take α = 2 rad/s², ω = 3 rad/s and r = 0.5 m. Then at = 2 × 0.5 = 1 m/s². Next, an = 3² × 0.5 = 9 × 0.5 = 4.5 m/s². The total is √(1² + 4.5²) = √21.25, which is about 4.61 m/s².

import math
alpha = 2.0                      # angular acceleration in rad/s^2
omega = 3.0                      # angular velocity in rad/s
r = 0.5                          # distance from the axis in metres
a_t = alpha * r                  # tangential part
a_n = omega ** 2 * r             # normal (centripetal) part
a = math.sqrt(a_t ** 2 + a_n ** 2)
print(f"a_t = {a_t:.1f}, a_n = {a_n:.1f}, total = {a:.2f} m/s^2")
a_t = 1.0, a_n = 4.5, total = 4.61 m/s^2

TrapThe normal part depends on ω squared, not on ω. Doubling the angular speed makes the normal acceleration four times bigger. Many students forget the square.

TipPut angles in radians. If a question gives revolutions per minute (rpm), convert first: ω in rad/s equals rpm × 2π / 60.

A wheel of radius 0.2 m turns at 5 rad/s. What is the speed of a point on its rim?

v = ωr = 5 × 0.2 = 1 m/s.

Why is the normal acceleration called centripetal?

"Centripetal" means "centre-seeking". The normal acceleration always points toward the axis of rotation.

Velocity of a point on a rigid body

The problem

Suppose you know the velocity of point A and how fast the body turns. How do you find the velocity of point B? You cannot copy vA, because B also turns about A. The rigid body formula gives the answer.

vB = vA + ω × rB/A
  • vB is the velocity we want.
  • vA is the known velocity of A. Every point shares this part.
  • ω is the angular velocity of the body.
  • rB/A is the arrow from A to B. It is the turning part of the motion.
  • × is the cross product. In a plane it gives a vector at right angles to r, with size ω times the length of r.

In words: the velocity of B equals the velocity of A, plus the extra velocity B gets because the body turns about A.

Doing the cross product in 2D

Let the body turn counterclockwise with ω, and let rB/A = (x, y), meaning x steps right and y steps up. Then

ω × r = (−ω y, ω x)

Check with r = (1, 0), so B is directly to the right of A. Then ω × r = (0, ω). For a counterclockwise turn, the point on the right moves up. That is correct.

Key ideaThe turning part ω × r is always perpendicular to r, and its size is ω times the distance from A to B.

Worked example

Point A is at the origin and moves with vA = (2, 0) m/s. The body turns counterclockwise with ω = 3 rad/s. Point B is at rB/A = (1, 2) m from A. Find vB.

  1. Turning part: ω × r = (−3 × 2, 3 × 1) = (−6, 3) m/s.
  2. Add the shared part: vB = (2, 0) + (−6, 3) = (2 − 6, 0 + 3) = (−4, 3) m/s.
  3. Speed: √((−4)² + 3²) = √(16 + 9) = √25 = 5 m/s.
vA = (2.0, 0.0)                        # velocity of A in m/s
omega = 3.0                            # counterclockwise, rad/s
r = (1.0, 2.0)                         # position of B relative to A, m
turn = (-omega * r[1], omega * r[0])   # omega x r in 2D
vB = (vA[0] + turn[0], vA[1] + turn[1])
speed = (vB[0] ** 2 + vB[1] ** 2) ** 0.5
print(f"omega x r = ({turn[0]:.1f}, {turn[1]:.1f}) m/s")
print(f"vB = ({vB[0]:.1f}, {vB[1]:.1f}) m/s")
print(f"speed of B = {speed:.1f} m/s")
omega x r = (-6.0, 3.0) m/s
vB = (-4.0, 3.0) m/s
speed of B = 5.0 m/s

TrapThe formula is a vector equation. Keep the signs of the components. Do not add speeds as plain numbers, because the turning part may point in a different direction.

In the worked example, is the turning part perpendicular to r?

Yes. The dot product (−6)(1) + (3)(2) = 0, so the two arrows are at right angles.

Two points on a body have the same velocity. What is ω × r for the pair?

It is zero. Either ω = 0 (pure translation), or the two points are the same point.

The instantaneous center and rolling

What the instantaneous center is

At any instant there is always a point of a body in plane motion whose velocity is zero. This is the instantaneous center of zero velocity, or IC. For this moment only, the body can be thought of as rotating about the IC. The IC is not a fixed pin: it moves from moment to moment.

Everyday analogy. A door pivots about its hinge. For one tiny moment, a wheel of a skateboard turning on the ground pivots about its contact point in the same way.

How to find the IC

  1. Pick two points A and B on the body whose velocity directions you know.
  2. At A, draw a line perpendicular to vA.
  3. At B, draw a line perpendicular to vB.
  4. The two lines cross at the IC. If they are parallel, the IC is far away and the body is in translation at this instant.

Why? Every point turns about the IC, so each velocity is perpendicular to the line joining the point to the IC. Both perpendiculars must pass through the IC.

Once the IC is known, the speed of any point P follows directly.

|vP| = ω dP

Here dP is the distance from P to the IC. Points far from the IC move fast, and points close to it move slowly.

Rolling without slipping

A wheel that rolls without skidding has a contact point with zero velocity. That contact point is the IC, and this one fact solves most rolling problems.

Let a disc of radius R roll to the right with its centre moving at speed v. The centre is at height R above the contact point, so the distance from the centre to the IC is R. Therefore v = ωR, and ω = v/R.

       centre C, moves right at speed v
          o
        /   \
       |  C  |  ---->  v  (rolling to the right)
        \   /
   ------X------  ground
          IC = contact point, velocity zero

The top point is at distance 2R from the IC, so its speed is ω × 2R = 2v. The top of a rolling wheel moves at twice the speed of its centre, in the same direction.

Worked example: rolling disc

A disc of radius 0.2 m rolls to the right with centre speed 1.5 m/s. Find ω and the velocity of the top point.

  1. Angular velocity: ω = v/R = 1.5/0.2 = 7.5 rad/s. Rolling to the right means clockwise, so ω = −7.5 rad/s with counterclockwise positive.
  2. Top point: it is 2R = 0.4 m from the IC. Its speed is 7.5 × 0.4 = 3 m/s, to the right.
  3. Check with the rigid body formula: vtop = vC + ω × rtop/C = 1.5 + 1.5 = 3 m/s. The two methods agree.
R = 0.2                      # radius of the disc in metres
v_center = 1.5               # speed of the centre in m/s, to the right
omega = v_center / R         # size of the angular velocity, rad/s
w = -omega                   # clockwise, so negative
print(f"angular speed = {omega:.1f} rad/s")

def x_velocity(r_y):
    # point at (0, r_y) from the centre; velocity = v_center + w * (k x r)
    # and k x (x, y) = (-y, x)
    return round(v_center + w * (-r_y), 6) + 0.0

print(f"contact point, x-velocity = {x_velocity(-R):.1f} m/s")
print(f"top point, x-velocity = {x_velocity(R):.1f} m/s")
angular speed = 7.5 rad/s
contact point, x-velocity = 0.0 m/s
top point, x-velocity = 3.0 m/s

GATE loves thisWhen a question says "rolls without slipping", write at once "velocity of contact point = 0" and "v = ωR". Most rolling questions are solved by these two lines.

TrapThe IC has zero velocity now, but it is not fixed, and its acceleration is not zero. Do not use the IC for accelerations. Use the acceleration formula in the next unit.

A wheel of radius 0.5 m rolls without slipping with its centre at 2 m/s. What is the speed of its top point?

ω = v/R = 4 rad/s, and the top point is 2R = 1 m from the IC. Its speed is 4 × 1 = 4 m/s, twice the centre speed.

Why is the contact point of a rolling wheel at rest?

The wheel turns about the contact point, and the turning velocity there cancels the forward velocity of the centre exactly, since v = ωR. That cancellation is what "no slipping" means.

Acceleration of a point on a rigid body

The extra term for acceleration

A body that speeds up or slows its turning, or that turns at all, gives its points extra acceleration. The rigid body acceleration formula is

aB = aA + α × rB/A − ω² rB/A

Here aB and aA are the accelerations of B and A, α is the angular acceleration, and ω is the angular velocity.

  • α × r is the tangential part. Its size is αr, and it is perpendicular to r.
  • −ω² r is the normal part. Its size is ω²r, and it points from B toward A.

In 2D, with α and ω counterclockwise positive and r = (x, y), we have α × r = (−αy, αx).

Worked example

Point A is fixed, so aA = (0, 0). The body has α = 3 rad/s² and ω = 2 rad/s, both counterclockwise. Point B is at rB/A = (1, 0) m. Find aB.

  1. Tangential part: α × r = (−3 × 0, 3 × 1) = (0, 3) m/s².
  2. Normal part: −ω² r = −4 × (1, 0) = (−4, 0) m/s².
  3. Add (aA is zero): aB = (0 − 4, 3) = (−4, 3) m/s².
  4. Size: √((−4)² + 3²) = √25 = 5 m/s².
alpha = 3.0                                # angular acceleration, counterclockwise
omega = 2.0                                # angular velocity, counterclockwise
r = (1.0, 0.0)                             # B relative to A, in metres
aA = (0.0, 0.0)                            # A is fixed
tang = (-alpha * r[1], alpha * r[0])       # alpha x r
norm = (-omega ** 2 * r[0], -omega ** 2 * r[1])   # -omega^2 r
aB = (aA[0] + tang[0] + norm[0], aA[1] + tang[1] + norm[1])
size = (aB[0] ** 2 + aB[1] ** 2) ** 0.5
print(f"tangential part = ({tang[0] + 0.0:.1f}, {tang[1]:.1f})")
print(f"normal part = ({norm[0]:.1f}, {norm[1] + 0.0:.1f})")
print(f"aB = ({aB[0]:.1f}, {aB[1]:.1f}), size = {size:.1f} m/s^2")
tangential part = (0.0, 3.0)
normal part = (-4.0, 0.0)
aB = (-4.0, 3.0), size = 5.0 m/s^2

TrapThe tangential part α × r and the normal part −ω² r are different vectors. The normal part always points from B toward A, even when the body is slowing down.

Key ideaAcceleration formulas use the accelerations of points. The IC has zero velocity but generally not zero acceleration.

Why is the normal part (−4, 0) and not (4, 0)?

The normal part is −ω² r = −4 × (1, 0). It points from B back toward the fixed point A, as a centripetal acceleration should.

A rod turns at a constant ω = 2 rad/s with α = 0. What is the acceleration of a point 1 m from the fixed end?

Only the normal part is present, so its size is ω²r = 4 × 1 = 4 m/s², directed toward the fixed end.

Solved GATE-style problems

These problems are original, written in the style of GATE numerical and multiple select questions. They are not past exam questions. Try each one on paper before reading the steps.

Speed of a point on a turning link

A link turns about a fixed axis O with angular velocity 5 rad/s, counterclockwise. A point P on the link is 0.4 m from O. What is the speed of P, in m/s? (Numerical answer.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The speed of one point on a link that turns about a fixed axis.
  2. Which idea applies. For rotation about a fixed axis, v = ωr.
  3. Work it out. v = 5 rad/s × 0.4 m = 2 m/s.
  4. Check. The units are rad/s times m, giving m/s. At 5 rad/s the point turns about 0.8 of a full circle each second, and each circle is about 2.5 m long, so it covers about 2 m each second.

Answer: 2 m/s.

Pure translation: which statements hold

A rigid body is in pure translation at one instant. Select all that apply.

(A) All its points have the same velocity.
(B) Its angular velocity ω is nonzero.
(C) All its points have the same acceleration.
(D) Every point moves on a circle with the same centre.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Choose every statement that is true for pure translation.
  2. Which idea applies. In pure translation ω = 0 and α = 0, so every point shares the same motion.
  3. Work it out. (A) is true, since vB = vA. (B) is false, because ω must be zero. (C) is true, since aB = aA. (D) is false, since a translating body can move along straight lines, like a block sliding down a ramp.
  4. Check. In the drawer example, every point moves the same way and no point traces a circle.

Answer: (A) and (C).

Top point of a rolling wheel

A wheel of radius 0.5 m rolls without slipping on a flat floor. Its centre moves at 2 m/s. What is the speed, in m/s, of the highest point of the wheel? (Numerical answer.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The speed of the top point, not the centre.
  2. Which idea applies. Rolling without slipping makes the contact point the IC. The top point is then 2R from it.
  3. Work it out. ω = v/R = 2/0.5 = 4 rad/s. The top is 2R = 1 m from the IC, so its speed is 4 × 1 = 4 m/s.
  4. Check. "Top speed is twice the centre speed" gives 2 × 2 = 4 m/s. Both methods agree.

Answer: 4 m/s.

Angular velocity of a rod from two end velocities

Rod AB is 2 m long. Point A is at the origin and point B is 2 m to the right of A on the x-axis. At this instant the velocity of A is (0, 4) m/s and the velocity of B is (0, 2) m/s, with positive y pointing up. What is the magnitude of the angular velocity of the rod, in rad/s? (Numerical answer.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The size of ω for the rod.
  2. Which idea applies. vB = vA + ω × rB/A, with rB/A = (2, 0).
  3. Work it out. For counterclockwise ω, the turning part is (0, 2ω). So (0, 2) = (0, 4) + (0, 2ω). The vertical parts give 2 = 4 + 2ω, so ω = −1 rad/s. The size is 1 rad/s, and the negative sign means clockwise.
  4. Check. The end velocities differ by 2 m/s over 2 m, which gives 1 rad/s.
vA_y = 4.0          # vertical velocity of A, m/s
vB_y = 2.0          # vertical velocity of B, m/s
L = 2.0             # length of the rod, m
omega = (vB_y - vA_y) / L
print(f"omega = {omega:.1f} rad/s, negative means clockwise")
omega = -1.0 rad/s, negative means clockwise

Answer: 1 rad/s (clockwise).

Total acceleration of a point on a disc

A point on a disc is 0.25 m from the axis. At this instant the disc has angular velocity 2 rad/s and angular acceleration 2 rad/s², both counterclockwise. What is the magnitude of the total acceleration of the point, in m/s²? Give your answer to two decimal places. (Numerical answer.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The size of the total acceleration.
  2. Which idea applies. at = αr and an = ω²r, at right angles to each other.
  3. Work it out. at = 2 × 0.25 = 0.5 m/s². an = 2² × 0.25 = 1 m/s². The total is √(0.5² + 1²) = √1.25, about 1.118, so 1.12 m/s².
  4. Check. The total is bigger than the larger part (1) and smaller than the sum of the parts (1.5). The value 1.118 fits.
import math
alpha, omega, r = 2.0, 2.0, 0.25
a_t = alpha * r
a_n = omega ** 2 * r
print(f"a_t = {a_t:.2f}, a_n = {a_n:.2f}")
print(f"total = {math.sqrt(a_t ** 2 + a_n ** 2):.2f} m/s^2")
a_t = 0.50, a_n = 1.00
total = 1.12 m/s^2

Answer: 1.12 m/s².

Acceleration of the contact point of a rolling disc

A disc of radius 0.5 m rolls to the right without slipping. At the instant shown, its centre has speed 2 m/s and acceleration 1 m/s² to the right. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the contact point with the floor, in m/s²? (Numerical answer.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The acceleration of the contact point, which has zero velocity but not zero acceleration.
  2. Which idea applies. aP = aC + α × rP/C − ω²rP/C. Rolling to the right is clockwise, so the signs are negative. The speed gives ω = −v/R = −4 rad/s, and the centre's acceleration gives α = −aC/R = −2 rad/s².
  3. Work it out. The contact point relative to the centre is (0, −0.5) m. The tangential part is α × r = (−1, 0) m/s². The normal part is −ω²r = (0, 8) m/s². Add: aP = (1 − 1, 0 + 8) = (0, 8) m/s². The size is 8 m/s², pointing up toward the centre.
  4. Check. For constant speed the known result is v²/R = 2²/0.5 = 8 m/s², toward the centre. This agrees.
import math
R = 0.5
v = 2.0
a_C = 1.0                       # centre accelerates to the right
omega = -v / R                  # clockwise, so negative
alpha = -a_C / R                # clockwise and speeding up, so negative
r = (0.0, -R)                   # contact point relative to the centre
tang = (-alpha * r[1], alpha * r[0])
norm = (-omega ** 2 * r[0], -omega ** 2 * r[1])
aP = (a_C + tang[0] + norm[0], tang[1] + norm[1])
print(f"aP = ({aP[0] + 0.0:.1f}, {aP[1] + 0.0:.1f}) m/s^2")
print(f"magnitude = {math.hypot(aP[0], aP[1]):.1f} m/s^2")
aP = (0.0, 8.0) m/s^2
magnitude = 8.0 m/s^2

Answer: 8 m/s², directed toward the centre of the disc.

Statements about a rolling disc

A disc rolls without slipping on a flat floor at a constant speed. Select all that apply.

(A) The contact point has zero velocity.
(B) The contact point has zero acceleration.
(C) The acceleration of the contact point points toward the centre of the disc.
(D) The instantaneous centre of zero velocity is the contact point.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements are true for a disc rolling at constant speed.
  2. Which idea applies. Rolling without slipping makes the contact point the IC. At constant speed the centre has no acceleration, so the contact point's acceleration is only the normal part, ω²R, toward the centre.
  3. Work it out. (A) is true. (B) is false, since the contact point has acceleration v²/R upward. (C) is true, since that acceleration points up toward the centre. (D) is true, since the contact point is the IC.
  4. Check. If (B) were true, the contact point would have no acceleration, but the wheel's path curves around the centre, so the point must accelerate even though its velocity is zero at each instant.

Answer: (A), (C) and (D).

Exam-level practice

Exam-level 1: Velocity of a point on a rolling wheel (NAT, 2 marks)

A wheel of radius 0.5 m rolls without slipping with centre speed 4 m/s. Find the speed of a point on the rim level with the centre.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The speed of that point.
  2. Which idea applies. The contact point is the instantaneous centre. Distance from it to the point is √(0.5² + 0.5²).
  3. Work it out. ω = 8 rad/s. r = 0.7071 m. v = 5.657 m/s.
  4. Check. The top point moves at 8 m/s, twice the centre speed.
import math
print("%.3f m/s" % (8 * math.hypot(0.5, 0.5)))

5.657 m/s

Answer: about 5.657 m/s.

Exam-level 2: Angular acceleration from rest (NAT, 2 marks)

A disc starts from rest with α = 3 rad/s². How many revolutions does it make in 10 s, correct to 2 decimal places?

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. θ in revolutions.
  2. Which idea applies. θ = (1/2)αt².
  3. Work it out. 150 rad / 2π = 23.87 rev.
  4. Check. The code computes it.
import math
print("%.2f" % (0.5 * 3 * 100 / (2 * math.pi)))

23.87

Answer: about 23.87 rev.

Exam-level 3: Slider-crank velocity (NAT, 2 marks)

A crank of 0.1 m turns at 10 rad/s. The connecting rod is 0.4 m. At crank angle 90° from inner dead centre, find the slider speed.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The slider velocity.
  2. Which idea applies. v = rω(sin θ + sin 2θ/(2n)) with n = l/r.
  3. Work it out. At θ = 90°, sin 2θ = 0, so v = 0.1 · 10 = 1 m/s.
  4. Check. At 90° the rod velocity relation makes the slider speed equal the crank tip speed.

Answer: 1 m/s.

Summary

Rigid body
A solid whose distances between points never change.
Kinematics
Describing motion without the forces that cause it.
Translation
Motion with no turning. All points share the same velocity.
Rotation about a fixed axis
Points move on circles around a line that stays still.
General plane motion
A mix of translation and rotation, such as a rolling wheel.
Angular velocity ω
Rate of change of angle, in rad/s. Counterclockwise is positive here.
Angular acceleration α
Rate of change of ω, in rad/s².
Speed on a circle
v = ωr for a point at distance r from the axis.
Tangential acceleration
αr, along the circle, changes the speed.
Normal acceleration
ω²r, toward the axis, changes the direction.
Relative velocity
vB = vA + ω × rB/A.
Cross product in 2D
ω × (x, y) = (−ωy, ωx) for counterclockwise ω.
Relative acceleration
aB = aA + α × rB/A − ω²rB/A.
Instantaneous center (IC)
The point with zero velocity at this instant, where the perpendiculars to two velocities meet.
Distance to the IC
The speed of a point equals ω times its distance from the IC.
Rolling without slipping
The contact point has zero velocity, so it is the IC.
Rolling speed
v = ωR for a disc of radius R. The top point moves at 2v.
Contact point acceleration
For constant speed, v²/R toward the centre.

Remember these

  1. Every point of a rigid body in plane motion has the same ω and α at one instant.
  2. v = ωr for a point on a circle, where r is the distance from the axis.
  3. The normal acceleration is ω²r, so it grows with the square of the angular speed.
  4. vB = vA + ω × rB/A is a vector equation. Keep the signs.
  5. Find the IC by drawing perpendiculars to two velocities. A point's speed is ω times its distance from the IC.
  6. Rolling without slipping: the contact point is the IC, and v = ωR.
  7. The top point of a rolling wheel moves at twice the centre speed.
  8. The IC has zero velocity but not zero acceleration.
