Uniform, Bernoulli, Binomial distributions

Three simple discrete distributions cover a huge share of GATE probability questions: the uniform distribution (every value equally likely), the Bernoulli distribution (one yes or no trial) and the binomial distribution (the number of yes results in several trials).

The idea in plain words

Many real situations have a simple structure. A coin lands heads or tails. A die shows one of six faces, all equally likely. A quality inspector checks ten items and counts how many are faulty. Each of these has a standard probability pattern, called a distribution. Once you know the pattern, you can write down the chance of any outcome and compute its mean and variance with a ready formula.

Think of a bag of marbles. Drawing one marble and checking whether it is red is a single yes or no trial. That is a Bernoulli trial. Drawing ten marbles, with replacement, and counting the reds is a binomial experiment. A fair die is a discrete uniform experiment, because all six faces are equally likely.

Why learn these three? Because GATE gives you a situation in words, and your job is to recognise which distribution fits. After that, the formulas do most of the work. In the Data Science and AI (DA) paper, these distributions appear in the probability section and in later statistics topics. In the Robotics and Automation (RA) paper, they appear in questions about reliability, sensor outcomes and random trials.

The main skill on this page is recognition. Ask yourself three questions: How many outcomes are there? Are they equally likely? Is there a fixed number of independent yes or no trials? The answers point you to the right distribution.

Key terms

These words appear again and again on this page.

Trial
Plain meaning: one attempt at an experiment, such as one coin toss. Precise definition: a single repetition of a random experiment.
Success and failure
Plain meaning: the two results of a yes or no trial. Precise definition: "success" is the outcome we count, "failure" is the other outcome. They are not moral words, a "failure" can be the result you want.
Success probability p
Plain meaning: the chance of a success on one trial. Precise definition: P(success) = p, with 0 ≤ p ≤ 1.
Independent trials
Plain meaning: one trial does not affect the next. Precise definition: the outcome of each trial has no effect on the probabilities of the others.
Parameter
Plain meaning: a number that sets the exact shape of a distribution. Precise definition: a fixed constant in a distribution's formula, such as p or n.
Discrete uniform distribution
Plain meaning: every value in a list is equally likely. Precise definition: a distribution on n values where each has probability 1/n.
Bernoulli distribution
Plain meaning: the distribution of one yes or no trial. Precise definition: X = 1 with probability p and X = 0 with probability 1 - p.
Binomial distribution
Plain meaning: the distribution of the number of successes in n independent trials. Precise definition: X takes values 0, 1, ..., n with P(X = x) = C(n, x) px (1 - p)n - x.
Combination C(n, x)
Plain meaning: the number of ways to choose x items from n when order does not matter. Precise definition: C(n, x) = n! / (x! (n - x)!), where n! is the product 1 × 2 × ... × n.
Mean and variance
Plain meaning: the average result, and the spread around it. Precise definition: μ = E[X] and Var(X) = E[(X - μ)²].

Concept 1: The Bernoulli trial

Why we start here

The Bernoulli trial is the smallest building block. It has exactly two results. Whether a machine passes an inspection, whether a sensor reading is above a threshold, or whether a coin shows heads: each is one yes or no trial. Every binomial result is built from many of these, so we study one trial first.

The distribution

Let X = 1 if the trial is a success, and X = 0 if it is a failure. Let p be the chance of success. Then:

P(X = 1) = p and P(X = 0) = 1 - p

The two probabilities add to p + (1 - p) = 1, as they must.

Mean and variance

The mean is found with the expectation formula from earlier work:

E[X] = 0 × (1 - p) + 1 × p = p

For the variance, note that X² = X, because 0² = 0 and 1² = 1. So E[X²] = p, and:

Var(X) = E[X²] - (E[X])² = p - p² = p(1 - p)

The variance is largest when p = 1/2, which is the most uncertain case, and it is zero when p = 0 or p = 1, because then the result is certain.

Worked example: a shot at a target

A robot arm hits a target with probability 0.3 on each try. Let X = 1 for a hit and 0 for a miss.

  1. Write the distribution. P(X = 1) = 0.3 and P(X = 0) = 0.7.
  2. Find the mean. E[X] = p = 0.3. Over many tries, the arm hits 30 percent of the time.
  3. Find the variance. Var(X) = p(1 - p) = 0.3 × 0.7 = 0.21.
  4. Check. The variance is largest at p = 1/2 with value 0.25. Our value 0.21 is a bit smaller, which is consistent with p being away from 1/2.

Key ideaFor any Bernoulli trial, the mean is p and the variance is p(1 - p). If a GATE question describes a single yes or no event, write these two formulas down first.

TrapThe variance is p(1 - p), not p. Students often write the mean in the variance formula by mistake. The variance uses the failure chance 1 - p as well.

Self-check: a Bernoulli trial has p = 0.5. What is its variance?

Var(X) = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25, which is the largest possible variance for a Bernoulli trial.

Self-check: why is E[X²] = p for a Bernoulli trial?

Because X can only be 0 or 1, and both 0 squared and 1 squared equal the original value. So X² takes the same values as X, and E[X²] = E[X] = p.

Concept 2: The discrete uniform distribution

Why it matters

Some experiments give every outcome the same chance. A fair die, a random card from a deck, or a random integer chosen from 1 to 100 all behave this way. This is the discrete uniform distribution. "Uniform" means "even", and "discrete" means the values can be listed.

The distribution

Suppose X can take the n values 1, 2, ..., n. Each value has the same probability:

P(X = x) = 1/n, for x = 1, 2, ..., n

Notice that the values do not have to start at 1. A uniform variable on 0 to 9 has n = 10 values, each with probability 1/10. The count n is the number of possible values, not the largest value.

Mean and variance

For the values 1 to n, the two results are:

E[X] = (n + 1) / 2 and Var(X) = (n² - 1) / 12

The mean is the midpoint of the values. The formula for the variance is worth memorising, because it appears often in GATE questions.

Worked example: a fair die

Let X be the number shown by a fair six-sided die. Here n = 6.

  1. Each face has probability 1/6.
  2. Mean: (6 + 1)/2 = 7/2 = 3.5. This is the midpoint of 1 and 6.
  3. Variance: (6² - 1)/12 = (36 - 1)/12 = 35/12, which is about 2.92.
  4. Check: the standard deviation is √(35/12), about 1.71, which is a sensible spread for values from 1 to 6.

The Python check below verifies both formulas by summing directly over the six faces.

from fractions import Fraction as F

n = 6                                  # a fair die has faces 1 to 6
values = range(1, n + 1)
p = F(1, n)                            # every face has the same chance

mean = sum(x * p for x in values)
var = sum(x * x * p for x in values) - mean ** 2

print("mean =", mean, "formula (n+1)/2 =", F(n + 1, 2))
print("variance =", var, "formula (n^2-1)/12 =", F(n * n - 1, 12))
mean = 7/2 formula (n+1)/2 = 7/2
variance = 35/12 formula (n^2-1)/12 = 35/12

TipIf the values are not 1 to n but start from a, shift them. A uniform variable on a to b has mean (a + b)/2. The spread depends only on the count of values, so the variance formula still uses the number of values n.

TrapDo not use n in the mean formula when the list does not start at 1. For a die, n = 6 and the mean is 3.5. For the numbers 10 to 20, there are 11 values, so n = 11, and the mean is 15, not 6.

Self-check: X is uniform on 1 to 4. What is P(X = 3)?

P(X = 3) = 1/4.

Self-check: X is uniform on 1 to 100. What is its mean?

E[X] = (100 + 1)/2 = 50.5.

Concept 3: The binomial distribution

Why it matters

Suppose you flip a coin 5 times and count the heads. Or you test 20 chips and count the faulty ones. These are binomial situations. Three conditions must hold:

  • There is a fixed number of trials, n.
  • Each trial is a yes or no result, with the same success chance p on every trial.
  • The trials are independent, so one result does not change the others.

The random variable X counts the successes. Its values are 0, 1, 2, ..., n.

The PMF

P(X = x) = C(n, x) px (1 - p)n - x

Read the formula in three parts:

  • px is the chance that the x chosen trials are all successes. Multiplying independent chances gives this.
  • (1 - p)n - x is the chance that the other n - x trials are all failures.
  • C(n, x) is the number of different ways to choose which x trials are the successes. Every arrangement has the same probability, so we multiply by this count.

The combination C(n, x) is computed as n! / (x! (n - x)!). For small numbers you can count by hand, for example C(5, 2) = 5 × 4 / (2 × 1) = 10.

Mean and variance

E[X] = np and Var(X) = np(1 - p)

The mean is "number of trials times chance of success", which is the intuitive answer. The variance is smaller than the mean when p is small, because the successes are rare and tightly grouped.

Worked example: five trials

A test has success chance p = 2/5 on each of 5 independent trials. Find P(X = 2).

  1. Identify the parameters. n = 5, p = 2/5, x = 2, so 1 - p = 3/5 and n - x = 3.
  2. Count the arrangements. C(5, 2) = 5! / (2! × 3!) = 120 / (2 × 6) = 10.
  3. Find the probability of one arrangement. (2/5)² × (3/5)³ = (4/25) × (27/125) = 108/3125.
  4. Multiply. 10 × 108/3125 = 1080/3125 = 216/625, which is 0.3456.
  5. Check. P(X = 2) is the largest of the six probabilities for n = 5, p = 0.4, which fits the idea that values near the mean np = 2 are most likely.

The Python check below computes the whole PMF, confirms that it sums to 1, and compares the mean and variance with the formulas.

from fractions import Fraction as F
from math import comb

n, p = 5, F(2, 5)                     # five trials, success chance 2/5
pmf = {x: comb(n, x) * p**x * (1 - p)**(n - x) for x in range(n + 1)}

print("P(X = 2) =", pmf[2])
print("total of PMF =", sum(pmf.values()))
mean = sum(x * q for x, q in pmf.items())
var = sum((x - mean) ** 2 * q for x, q in pmf.items())
print("mean =", mean, "formula n*p =", n * p)
print("variance =", var, "formula n*p*(1-p) =", n * p * (1 - p))
P(X = 2) = 216/625
total of PMF = 1
mean = 2 formula n*p = 2
variance = 6/5 formula n*p*(1-p) = 6/5

GATE loves thisMost binomial questions reduce to one of three asks: P(X = x) for a single x, P(X ≤ x) which is a sum of several terms, or P(X ≥ 1) which is 1 - P(X = 0). The last trick saves a lot of work whenever "at least one" appears in the question.

TrapStudents often forget the combination factor C(n, x). Without it, you have only the probability of one specific arrangement, not of all the arrangements that give x successes.

Self-check: what is C(4, 1)?

C(4, 1) = 4, because there are 4 ways to choose one trial out of 4 to be the success.

Self-check: X is binomial with n = 10 and p = 0.5. What is its mean?

E[X] = np = 10 × 0.5 = 5.

Concept 4: A binomial is a sum of Bernoulli trials

The big connection

Here is a useful way to see the binomial. Let X1 be the result of the first trial, X2 the second, and so on up to Xn. Each Xi is a Bernoulli trial with success chance p, and the trials are independent. The binomial count is simply their sum:

X = X1 + X2 + ... + Xn

This connection explains the mean and variance formulas without memorising them. The expectation of a sum is the sum of the expectations, so E[X] = p + p + ... + p = np. For independent trials, the variances also add, so Var(X) = p(1 - p) + ... + p(1 - p) = np(1 - p).

Worked example: counting the sum

  1. Take n = 3 trials with p = 0.5 each.
  2. Each Bernoulli trial has mean 0.5 and variance 0.25.
  3. Sum of three means: 3 × 0.5 = 1.5. This matches np = 3 × 0.5 = 1.5.
  4. Sum of three variances: 3 × 0.25 = 0.75. This matches np(1 - p) = 3 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.75.

How to recognise a binomial in a GATE question

Use this checklist. If all four answers are yes, the distribution is binomial.

  1. Is the number of trials fixed in advance?
  2. Does each trial have only two results?
  3. Is the success chance the same on every trial?
  4. Are the trials independent?

If the number of trials changes, or the chance changes from trial to trial, the binomial formula does not apply directly. For example, drawing cards from a deck without replacement changes the chance at each draw, so it is not binomial.

Key ideaA binomial variable is a count of successes in independent Bernoulli trials. This single idea gives you the mean np and the variance np(1 - p).

TrapSampling without replacement breaks independence. Drawing 2 balls from a bag of 10 without putting the first back does not give a binomial result, because the second draw depends on the first.

Self-check: why does the variance of a sum of independent trials equal the sum of the variances?

Because independent trials do not influence each other, the covariance terms vanish, so the variances simply add. This property is specific to independence.

Self-check: 20 independent light bulbs each fail with probability 0.1. Is the number of failures binomial?

Yes. There are 20 fixed trials, each has two results (fail or work), the chance 0.1 is the same each time, and the bulbs are assumed independent. The mean number of failures is 20 × 0.1 = 2.

Solved GATE-style problems

These problems are original GATE-style questions written for practice. They are not past paper questions. Work each one on paper first, then compare with the solution.

A single sensor reading

A sensor reports a fault with probability 0.3 on each reading, independently of other readings. Let X = 1 if a reading reports a fault and X = 0 otherwise. Find Var(X).

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The variance of one yes or no reading.
  2. Which idea applies. X is a Bernoulli trial with p = 0.3, so Var(X) = p(1 - p).
  3. Work it out. Var(X) = 0.3 × 0.7 = 0.21.
  4. Check. The variance must be at most 0.25, which is the maximum for a Bernoulli trial. 0.21 is below 0.25.

Answer: 0.21

Select all true statements about Binomial(10, 0.5)

Let X be binomial with n = 10 and p = 0.5. Select all that apply.

(A) E[X] = 5.
(B) Var(X) = 2.5.
(C) P(X = 0) = 1/1024.
(D) The standard deviation of X is 5.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Tick every true statement about the distribution.
  2. Which idea applies. The mean np, the variance np(1 - p), and the PMF at x = 0.
  3. Work it out. Option A: E[X] = 10 × 0.5 = 5, so A is true. Option B: Var(X) = 10 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 2.5, so B is true. Option C: P(X = 0) = C(10, 0) × 0.50 × 0.510 = 1 × 1 × 1/1024, so C is true. Option D: the standard deviation is √2.5, which is about 1.58, not 5. So D is false.
  4. Check. Option D confuses the variance with the standard deviation. The standard deviation is always the square root of the variance.

Answer: (A), (B) and (C) are true. Option (D) is false.

Uniform random integer

A random integer X is chosen uniformly from the integers 1 to 20. Find Var(X). Give the answer as a decimal.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The variance of a discrete uniform variable on 20 values.
  2. Which idea applies. The discrete uniform formula Var(X) = (n² - 1)/12 with n = 20.
  3. Work it out. Var(X) = (20² - 1)/12 = (400 - 1)/12 = 399/12 = 33.25.
  4. Check. The standard deviation is √33.25, about 5.77. Values run from 1 to 20, so a spread of about 6 around a mean of 10.5 is sensible.

Answer: 33.25

Two heads in four flips

A fair coin is flipped 4 times. Let X be the number of heads. Find P(X = 2). Give the answer as a fraction.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. One value of a binomial probability.
  2. Which idea applies. Binomial PMF with n = 4, p = 1/2, x = 2.
  3. Work it out. C(4, 2) = 4! / (2! × 2!) = 24 / 4 = 6. Then (1/2)² × (1/2)² = 1/16. Multiply: 6 × 1/16 = 6/16 = 3/8.
  4. Check. There are 24 = 16 equally likely sequences. Six of them have exactly 2 heads (HHTT, HTHT, HTTH, THHT, THTH, TTHH), and 6/16 = 3/8.

Answer: 3/8

Mean of a rare event count

NAT question. A batch has 20 independent items. Each item is defective with probability 0.1. Let X be the number of defective items. Find Var(X). Give the answer as a decimal.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The variance of the number of defectives.
  2. Which idea applies. The count is binomial with n = 20 and p = 0.1, so Var(X) = np(1 - p).
  3. Work it out. Var(X) = 20 × 0.1 × 0.9 = 2 × 0.9 = 1.8.
  4. Check. The mean is np = 2, and the variance 1.8 is smaller than the mean. This is typical when p is small, because rare successes are less spread out.

Answer: 1.8

Finding n and p from the mean and variance

A binomial random variable X has mean 6 and variance 4.8. Find n and p. Give n as an integer and p as a decimal.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The two parameters, given the two summary numbers.
  2. Which idea applies. Use np = 6 and np(1 - p) = 4.8. Divide the second equation by the first.
  3. Work it out. Dividing gives (1 - p) = 4.8 / 6 = 0.8, so p = 0.2. Then n = 6 / p = 6 / 0.2 = 30.
  4. Check. With n = 30 and p = 0.2: np = 6 and np(1 - p) = 6 × 0.8 = 4.8. Both numbers match the question.

Answer: n = 30 and p = 0.2

At most one defective

NAT question. An inspector checks 5 independent items. Each item is defective with probability 0.1. Find the probability that at most one item is defective. Give the answer correct to four decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. P(X ≤ 1), where X is binomial with n = 5 and p = 0.1.
  2. Which idea applies. Add the PMF values for x = 0 and x = 1.
  3. Work it out. P(X = 0) = 0.95 = 0.59049. P(X = 1) = C(5, 1) × 0.1 × 0.94 = 5 × 0.1 × 0.6561 = 0.32805. Sum: 0.59049 + 0.32805 = 0.91854, which is 0.9185 to four decimals.
  4. Check. The answer must be below 1 and above P(X = 0), which is 0.59049. The value 0.9185 satisfies both conditions.

Answer: 0.9185

Two heads given at least one head

A fair coin is flipped 3 times. Given that at least one head appears, find the probability that exactly 2 heads appear. Give the answer as a fraction.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. A conditional probability, P(X = 2 | X ≥ 1), where X is binomial with n = 3 and p = 1/2.
  2. Which idea applies. The conditional probability rule P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B). Here A and B are the same as "X = 2", because X = 2 already forces X ≥ 1.
  3. Work it out. P(X = 2) = C(3, 2)(1/2)³ = 3/8. P(X ≥ 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - (1/2)³ = 1 - 1/8 = 7/8. So P(X = 2 | X ≥ 1) = (3/8) / (7/8) = 3/7.
  4. Check. There are 7 equally likely sequences with at least one head, and 3 of them have exactly two heads (HHT, HTH, THH). So the answer is 3/7, which matches.

Answer: 3/7

Exam-level 1: A conditional binomial probability (NAT, 2 marks)

NAT question. A quality inspector tests 12 independent items. Each item is defective with probability 0.25, and defects occur independently of one another. Let X be the number of defective items found. The inspector reports the result only when the count is at most 4. Find the conditional probability P(X = 3 given X at most 4). Give the answer correct to two decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. P(X = 3 | X ≤ 4), to two decimal places.
  2. Which idea applies. The binomial probability C(n, k) p^k (1 - p)^(n - k), and the definition of conditional probability. The event X = 3 sits inside X ≤ 4, so the overlap is just X = 3.
  3. Work it out. With n = 12 and p = 1/4, P(X = 3) = 220 × (1/4)³ × (3/4)⁹ ≈ 0.2581. Also P(X ≤ 4) is the sum of P(X = 0) through P(X = 4), which is about 0.84236. The ratio is 0.2581 / 0.84236 ≈ 0.3064.
  4. Check. The answer is between 0 and 1. Among the five values 0 to 4, X = 3 is the most likely one, so a share of about 0.31 (a little under a third) is reasonable.

Answer: 0.31

from fractions import Fraction as F
from math import comb
n = 12
p = F(1, 4)
def pmf(k):
    return comb(n, k) * p ** k * (1 - p) ** (n - k)
num = pmf(3)
den = sum(pmf(k) for k in range(0, 5))
ans = num / den
print("P(X = 3) =", round(float(num), 5))
print("P(X at most 4) =", round(float(den), 5))
print("P(X = 3 given X at most 4) =", round(float(ans), 4))
print("decimal =", round(float(ans), 2))
P(X = 3) = 0.2581
P(X at most 4) = 0.84236
P(X = 3 given X at most 4) = 0.3064
decimal = 0.31

Exam-level 2: Conditioning a discrete uniform (MCQ, 2 marks)

A lottery machine picks a number X uniformly from the whole numbers 1 to 20, so every number has the same chance. The machine then tells the operator only that the number is at least 5. Which of the following is the probability that the number is at most 10, given that it is at least 5? (A) 0.30 (B) 0.375 (C) 0.50 (D) 0.60. Select one option.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. P(X ≤ 10 | X ≥ 5).
  2. Which idea applies. The discrete uniform PMF (each value has probability 1/20), and the conditional rule P(A | B) = P(A and B) / P(B).
  3. Work it out. P(X ≥ 5) = 16/20, since the values 5 to 20 are 16 numbers. P(5 ≤ X ≤ 10) = 6/20, since the values 5 to 10 are 6 numbers. So P(X ≤ 10 | X ≥ 5) = (6/20) / (16/20) = 6/16 = 3/8 = 0.375. The answer is option B.
  4. Check. Conditioning shrinks the list to 16 equally likely values, and 6 of them are at most 10, so about 0.375 is right. Why the other options fail: (A) is the unconditional probability 6/20, (C) counts the values 1 to 10 without conditioning, and (D) divides by 10 instead of 16.

Answer: (B) 0.375

from fractions import Fraction as F
S = range(1, 21)
B = [x for x in S if x >= 5]
A = [x for x in B if x <= 10]
print("P(X at least 5) =", F(len(B), len(S)))
print("P(5 to 10) =", F(len(A), len(S)))
print("P(X at most 10 given X at least 5) =", F(len(A), len(B)), "=", float(F(len(A), len(B))))
P(X at least 5) = 4/5
P(5 to 10) = 3/10
P(X at most 10 given X at least 5) = 3/8 = 0.375

Exam-level 3: Facts about two binomial counts (MSQ, 2 marks)

A factory runs two identical and independent production lines. Each line produces 10 items per shift, and each item is faulty independently with probability 0.3. Let X be the number of faulty items from line 1 and Y the number from line 2. So X and Y are independent binomial random variables with n = 10 and p = 0.3. Select all the statements that are true.

  • (A) E[X] = 3
  • (B) Var(X) = 2.1
  • (C) X + Y has a binomial distribution with n = 20 and p = 0.3
  • (D) P(X = 0) = 0.3^10

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which of the four statements are true. Select all that apply.
  2. Which idea applies. The binomial mean np and variance np(1 - p), the fact that independent binomials with the same p add to a binomial with the total n, and the binomial PMF.
  3. Work it out. E[X] = 10 × 0.3 = 3, so (A) is true. Var(X) = 10 × 0.3 × 0.7 = 2.1, so (B) is true. Adding two independent binomials with the same p gives n = 10 + 10 = 20 and the same p = 0.3, so (C) is true. For (D), P(X = 0) = (1 - p)^10 = 0.7^10, which is about 0.028. The value 0.3^10 is about 0.0000059, so (D) is false.
  4. Check. Three of the four statements agree with the formulas, and the only wrong one swaps p with 1 - p. Option (D) is the usual trap: P(X = 0) uses the chance of a good item, not a faulty one.

Answer: A, B and C

from fractions import Fraction as F
from math import comb
n = 10
p = F(3, 10)
def pmf(k, m):
    return comb(m, k) * p ** k * (1 - p) ** (m - k)
print("E[X] =", n * p)
print("Var(X) =", n * p * (1 - p))
print("P(X = 0) =", pmf(0, n))
print("0.3^10 =", p ** 10)
ok = all(sum(pmf(i, n) * pmf(k - i, n) for i in range(0, k + 1) if i <= n and k - i <= n) == pmf(k, 2 * n) for k in range(0, 2 * n + 1))
print("X + Y has the Bin(20, 0.3) distribution:", ok)

Var(X) = 21/10
P(X = 0) = 282475249/10000000000
0.3^10 = 59049/10000000000

Exam-level 4: A conditional mean from a binomial (NAT, 2 marks)

NAT question. A bakery checks 5 independent loaves. Each loaf is underweight with probability 0.2, independently of the others. Let X be the number of underweight loaves among the 5. The bakery reports the average count only when at least 2 loaves are underweight. Find E[X given X at least 2], the expected value of X under this condition. Give the answer correct to two decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. E[X | X ≥ 2], to two decimal places.
  2. Which idea applies. Conditional expectation: E[X | X ≥ 2] = E[X; X ≥ 2] / P(X ≥ 2), where E[X; X ≥ 2] means the sum of x P(X = x) over x ≥ 2.
  3. Work it out. X is binomial with n = 5 and p = 0.2, so E[X] = 1. P(X = 1) = 5 × 0.2 × 0.8⁴ = 0.4096. Then E[X; X ≥ 2] = E[X] - 1 × P(X = 1) = 1 - 0.4096 = 0.5904. Also P(X ≥ 2) = 1 - 0.8⁵ - 0.4096 = 1 - 0.32768 - 0.4096 = 0.26272. So E[X | X ≥ 2] = 0.5904 / 0.26272 ≈ 2.2473, which is 2.25 to two decimal places.
  4. Check. If X is at least 2, its mean must be at least 2, and 2.25 is above 2. It is also below the largest value 5. Both checks pass.

Answer: 2.25

from fractions import Fraction as F
from math import comb
n = 5
p = F(1, 5)
def pmf(k):
    return comb(n, k) * p ** k * (1 - p) ** (n - k)
EX = sum(k * pmf(k) for k in range(n + 1))
P2 = sum(pmf(k) for k in range(2, n + 1))
E_part = sum(k * pmf(k) for k in range(2, n + 1))
ans = E_part / P2
print("E[X] =", EX)
print("P(X at least 2) =", P2)
print("E[X; X at least 2] =", E_part)
print("E[X given X at least 2] =", round(float(ans), 4))
print("decimal =", round(float(ans), 2))

decimal = 2.25

Summary

Trial
One attempt at a random experiment, with a result you can record.
Independent trials
Trials whose results do not affect each other.
Discrete uniform
Every value in a list of n values has probability 1/n.
Uniform mean and variance
For values 1 to n: mean (n + 1)/2, variance (n² - 1)/12.
Bernoulli
A single yes or no trial with success chance p.
Bernoulli mean and variance
E[X] = p and Var(X) = p(1 - p).
Binomial
The number of successes in n independent trials, each with success chance p.
Binomial PMF
P(X = x) = C(n, x) px (1 - p)n - x.
Combination
C(n, x) = n! / (x! (n - x)!), the number of ways to choose x items from n.
Binomial mean and variance
E[X] = np and Var(X) = np(1 - p).
Sum of Bernoullis
A binomial variable is the sum of n independent Bernoulli variables.
At least one
P(X ≥ 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) for a binomial variable.
Parameter
A fixed number such as n or p that sets the exact distribution.
Recognition checklist
Fixed trials, two results, same chance each time, and independence means binomial.

Remember these

  1. A Bernoulli trial has mean p and variance p(1 - p).
  2. A discrete uniform on 1 to n has mean (n + 1)/2 and variance (n² - 1)/12.
  3. A binomial count has mean np and variance np(1 - p).
  4. The binomial PMF includes the combination C(n, x), which counts the arrangements.
  5. A binomial variable is a sum of independent Bernoulli trials, which explains its mean and variance.
  6. For "at least one" questions, use the complement 1 - P(X = 0).
  7. Sampling without replacement breaks independence, so the binomial formula does not apply.
