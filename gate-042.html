Mean, median, mode, standard deviation

Mean, median and mode are three ways to say what a typical value looks like. Standard deviation tells you how far values usually sit from the mean. GATE DA questions often give grouped frequency tables, so this page shows you how to handle those step by step.

The idea in plain words

Suppose a friend asks, "How many hours do students in your class sleep?" You could answer in three different ways. You could share out the total hours equally among everyone, which gives the mean. You could line everyone up by hours and point to the middle person, which gives the median. Or you could say which number of hours the most students have, which gives the mode.

Each answer describes "a typical value", but they can differ. A few very large or very small values pull the mean, while the median ignores how extreme the values are. Once you know the typical value, the next question is how much the values spread around it. That is the job of the standard deviation.

These ideas appear across GATE DA, from describing a dataset to checking the assumptions of a model. Expect numerical questions that give raw numbers, grouped tables, or a dataset after a transformation.

Key terms

Read these now, and come back when a word feels unfamiliar. Each entry gives the plain meaning first and the precise definition second.

Mean
The fair share of each value. Precise definition: the sum of the values divided by the number of values.
Median
The middle value when the data are sorted. Precise definition: the value at position (n + 1) / 2 of the sorted data, or the average of the two middle values when n is even.
Mode
The most common value. Precise definition: the value with the highest frequency. A dataset can have no mode, one mode, or several.
Frequency
How many times a value, or a class of values, occurs. Written f.
Class interval
A range of values in a grouped table, such as 10 to 20. Its width is h.
Midpoint
The centre of a class interval, found as (lower + upper) / 2. It stands in for the values inside the class.
Deviation
How far a value is from the mean: x minus the mean.
Variance
The average of the squared deviations. Population variance divides by N. Sample variance divides by n - 1.
Standard deviation (SD)
The square root of the variance. It is in the same units as the data.
Skewness
The lean of a distribution. Right-skewed means a long tail to the high side.
Linear transformation
A rule of the form Y = aX + b, where a and b are constants.
Coefficient of variation (CV)
The SD divided by the mean. It has no units, so it compares spread across datasets.

Mean, median and mode for raw data

The mean

Take the data 2, 3, 3, 5, 7 and 10. There are n = 6 values. Their sum is 2 + 3 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 10 = 30. The mean is:

mean = (sum of x) / n = 30 / 6 = 5

Symbols: x is one value, the sum runs over all values, and n is how many values there are. The mean is the balance point of the data. If you placed the values on a see-saw, it would balance at 5.

The median

The median needs sorted data. The data above are already sorted. With n = 6 (even), there is no single middle value, so take the two middle values, 3 and 5, and average them:

median = (3 + 5) / 2 = 4

With an odd count, such as 2, 3, 5, 7, 10 (n = 5), the median is the middle value, which is position (n + 1) / 2 = 3, giving 5.

The mode

The value 3 appears twice, and every other value appears once. So the mode is 3. A dataset where every value appears once has no mode.

Key ideaMean uses every value, so one extreme value moves it. Median depends only on the middle, so it resists extreme values. Mode only counts how often values occur.

TrapAlways sort the data before finding the median. Taking the middle of the list as written is a very common error.

Self-check: what are the mean and median of 4, 8, 6, 10?

Sum is 28, so the mean is 28 / 4 = 7. Sorted, the data are 4, 6, 8, 10. The median is (6 + 8) / 2 = 7.

Self-check: if 10 is changed to 100 in the data above, which measure changes?

The mean changes a lot, because it uses every value. The median stays at 4, because the middle two values are still 3 and 5.

A Python check of the raw-data statistics

The standard library statistics module computes these for you. We check the hand results with it, and the printed output is exactly what the program produces.

import statistics as st

data = [2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 10]
print("mean =", st.mean(data))
print("median =", st.median(data))
print("mode =", st.mode(data))
print("population variance =", st.pvariance(data))
print("sample variance =", st.variance(data))
print("population SD =", round(st.pstdev(data), 4))
print("sample SD =", round(st.stdev(data), 4))

Grouped frequency data

Why grouping happens

When a survey has hundreds of values, a table of classes is easier to read. The exact values are lost, so every formula below uses an approximation. The midpoint of each class stands in for the values inside it.

Consider marks of 20 students in four classes:

ClassFrequency fMidpoint mCumulative frequency
0 to 10454
10 to 2061510
20 to 3082518
30 to 4023520

The total is N = 20.

Grouped mean

mean = sum of (f times m) / N = (4 times 5 + 6 times 15 + 8 times 25 + 2 times 35) / 20
mean = (20 + 90 + 200 + 70) / 20 = 380 / 20 = 19

Grouped median

The median is the value with half the data below it, which is position N / 2 = 10. Find the first class where the cumulative frequency reaches 10. That is the median class, here 10 to 20, which has lower limit L = 10, frequency f = 6, width h = 10, and cumulative frequency before it CF = 4. Then:

median = L + ((N/2 - CF) / f) times h = 10 + ((10 - 4) / 6) times 10 = 10 + 10 = 20

Grouped mode

The modal class is the class with the largest frequency, here 20 to 30 with f = 8. Let f1 be the excess of this frequency over the class before it, and f2 the excess over the class after it. Here f1 = 8 - 6 = 2 and f2 = 8 - 2 = 6. Then:

mode = L + (f1 / (f1 + f2)) times h = 20 + (2 / 8) times 10 = 22.5

TipWrite the cumulative frequency column before you start. It shows the median class in one glance.

TrapThe median formula uses N / 2, not the middle class. Also, the mode formula uses differences with the neighbouring classes, not the raw frequencies.

Self-check: which class contains the median if N = 30 and the cumulative frequencies are 5, 14, 22?

N / 2 = 15. The cumulative frequency first reaches 15 at the class with cumulative frequency 22, which is the third class.

Self-check: why is the grouped mean only an approximation?

Each class is replaced by its midpoint, so the true values inside the class are lost. The error is small when the classes are narrow.

Variance and standard deviation

Spread around the mean

Two datasets can share the same mean but differ in spread. The variance measures spread by averaging the squared deviations from the mean. Squaring removes the signs, so positive and negative deviations do not cancel.

population variance = sum of (x - mean)^2 / N

Using the raw data 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 10 with mean 5, the deviations are -3, -2, -2, 0, 2, 5. Their squares are 9, 4, 4, 0, 4, 25, which add to 46. So the population variance is 46 / 6 = 7.667. The standard deviation is its square root, sqrt(7.667) = 2.769, in the same units as the data.

Sample variance: why divide by n - 1

When you have only a sample, you measure deviations from the sample mean. That mean is calculated from the same data, so the deviations come out slightly smaller than the true spread. Dividing by n - 1 instead of n corrects this. The result is the sample variance:

sample variance = sum of (x - mean)^2 / (n - 1) = 46 / 5 = 9.2

The sample SD is sqrt(9.2) = 3.033. If a question says "the population" or gives every value of the group, divide by N. If it says "a sample" or gives only part of the group, divide by n - 1.

A grouped variance worked out

Use the same grouped table. The mean is 19, and the midpoints are 5, 15, 25 and 35. The deviations are -14, -4, 6 and 16. Multiply each squared deviation by its frequency:

sum of f times (m - mean)^2 = 4 times 196 + 6 times 16 + 8 times 36 + 2 times 256 = 784 + 96 + 288 + 512 = 1680
population variance = 1680 / 20 = 84, so SD = sqrt(84) = 9.165

Key ideaStandard deviation is the square root of variance. Variance is in squared units, so the SD is the number to report in the original units.

TrapDo not forget the square root at the end. Variance is 84, but the standard deviation is about 9.165.

Self-check: data are 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. What is the population variance?

The mean is 5. The squared deviations are 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, 16, which add to 32. Divide by 8 to get 4.

Self-check: is the sample variance always larger than the population variance?

It is larger for the same data, because dividing by n - 1 instead of n makes the result bigger by the factor n / (n - 1).

A Python check of the grouped statistics

This program uses exact fractions for the grouped calculation, and floats only for printing. The printed output is exactly what the program produces.

from fractions import Fraction as F
import math

classes = [(0, 10, 4), (10, 20, 6), (20, 30, 8), (30, 40, 2)]
N = sum(f for _, _, f in classes)
mids = [F(lo + hi, 2) for lo, hi, _ in classes]
mean = sum(m * f for m, (_, _, f) in zip(mids, classes)) / N
var = sum(f * (m - mean) ** 2 for m, (_, _, f) in zip(mids, classes)) / N
print("N =", N)
print("grouped mean =", float(mean))
print("population variance =", var)
print("population SD =", round(math.sqrt(var), 4))
print("sample variance =", var * N / (N - 1))

half = F(N, 2)
cum = 0
for lo, hi, f in classes:
    if cum + f >= half:
        median = lo + (half - cum) / f * (hi - lo)
        break
    cum += f
print("median =", float(median))

i = max(range(len(classes)), key=lambda k: classes[k][2])
lo, hi, f = classes[i]
f1 = f - classes[i - 1][2]
f2 = f - classes[i + 1][2]
mode = lo + F(f1, f1 + f2) * (hi - lo)
print("mode =", float(mode))

Skewness, linear transformations and coefficient of variation

Skewness and the order of the three averages

A right-skewed dataset has a long tail to the high side. A few large values pull the mean upward, the median stays nearer the bulk of the data, and the mode sits at the peak. For right skew the usual order is mode, then median, then mean, from smallest to largest. Incomes are a common example. A few very high incomes raise the mean above the median.

For a symmetric dataset, the three measures coincide, or nearly so. Remember this lean as a clue: if the mean is bigger than the median, the data probably have a long right tail.

Linear transformations

Suppose each value is changed by Y = aX + b. Shifting by b moves the data but does not change how spread out they are. Multiplying by a stretches them. So:

mean of Y = a times mean of X + b
Var(Y) = a^2 times Var(X), so SD(Y) = |a| times SD(X)

Take the raw data again, with a = 3 and b = 4. The new values are 10, 13, 13, 19, 25, 34. Their mean is 3 times 5 + 4 = 19, and the population SD is 3 times 2.769 = 8.307. The shift of 4 changed nothing about the spread.

Key ideaAdding a constant shifts the mean but leaves the SD unchanged. Multiplying by a scales both the mean and the SD by |a|, and the variance by a squared.

TrapStudents often think adding b changes the SD. It does not. Only the multiplier a affects spread.

Coefficient of variation

The SD is in the units of the data, so comparing SD across datasets with different scales can mislead. The coefficient of variation removes the units by dividing by the mean:

CV = SD / mean

For the raw data, the population CV is 2.769 / 5 = 0.554, or 55.4 per cent. A dataset of heights in centimetres and one of weights in kilograms can then be compared on the same scale.

Self-check: if the SD of X is 2, what is the SD of Y = 5X - 1?

SD(Y) = |5| times 2 = 10. The minus 1 shifts the data but does not change the spread.

Self-check: why is CV useful?

It is unitless, so you can compare the relative spread of two datasets measured in different units.

Solved GATE-style problems

Mean of four values

Find the mean of 4, 8, 6 and 10.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The mean of four numbers.
  2. Which idea applies. Mean is the sum divided by n.
  3. Work it out. Sum = 4 + 8 + 6 + 10 = 28. Mean = 28 / 4 = 7.
  4. Check. The mean must lie between 4 and 10, and 7 does.

Answer: 7

Which value is the mode?

Which of the following is the mode of the data 1, 2, 2, 3, 5, 5, 5?

  • (A) 2
  • (B) 3
  • (C) 5
  • (D) 1

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The most frequent value.
  2. Which idea applies. Mode counts how often each value occurs.
  3. Work it out. 5 occurs three times, 2 occurs twice, and 1 and 3 occur once.
  4. Check. 5 has the largest count, so it is the mode.

Answer: (C) 5

Median of a grouped table

A grouped table has classes 0 to 5 with frequency 3, 5 to 10 with frequency 7, 10 to 15 with frequency 5, and 15 to 20 with frequency 5. Find the median. (Give a whole number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The median of grouped data.
  2. Which idea applies. The grouped median formula, using N / 2.
  3. Work it out. N = 20, so N / 2 = 10. Cumulative frequencies are 3, 10, 15, 20. The median class is 5 to 10, with L = 5, CF = 3, f = 7 and h = 5. Median = 5 + ((10 - 3) / 7) times 5 = 5 + 5 = 10.
  4. Check. The cumulative frequency reaches exactly 10 at the end of the class 5 to 10, so the median is at its upper limit, 10.

Answer: 10

Which statements about spread are true?

Select all that apply.

  • (A) Adding 10 to every value leaves the SD unchanged.
  • (B) For a right-skewed dataset, the mean is usually larger than the median.
  • (C) The sample variance divides the sum of squared deviations by n.
  • (D) The coefficient of variation has the same units as the data.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Judge each statement.
  2. Which idea applies. Shifting, skewness, the n - 1 correction, and the definition of CV.
  3. Work it out. (A) True: a shift does not change spread. (B) True: a long right tail pulls the mean up. (C) False: the sample variance divides by n - 1. (D) False: CV = SD / mean has no units.
  4. Check. Test (A) with 1, 2, 3: SD is 0.816. Add 10 and get 11, 12, 13, with the same SD.

Answer: (A) and (B). Select all that apply.

Sample standard deviation

The data 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 are a sample. Find the sample standard deviation, correct to 3 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The sample SD, with n - 1 in the denominator.
  2. Which idea applies. Sample variance divides by n - 1, then take the square root.
  3. Work it out. Mean = 40 / 8 = 5. Squared deviations: 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, 16, summing to 32. Sample variance = 32 / 7 = 4.571. SD = sqrt(4.571) = 2.138.
  4. Check. The population SD would be sqrt(4) = 2, which is smaller, as expected.

Answer: 2.138

Population SD from midpoints

Ten values are grouped as 0 to 10 with frequency 2, 10 to 20 with frequency 5, and 20 to 30 with frequency 3. Using class midpoints, find the population standard deviation. (Give a whole number.)

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The population SD, approximated from the grouped table.
  2. Which idea applies. Midpoints, then the grouped variance formula.
  3. Work it out. Midpoints are 5, 15, 25. Mean = (2 times 5 + 5 times 15 + 3 times 25) / 10 = 160 / 10 = 16. Deviations are -11, -1, 9. Sum of f times squared deviation = 2 times 121 + 5 times 1 + 3 times 81 = 242 + 5 + 243 = 490. Population variance = 490 / 10 = 49. SD = sqrt(49) = 7.
  4. Check. The mean 16 lies inside the middle class, as it should.

Answer: 7

Which statements survive a linear transformation?

Select all that apply. Data set A is 2, 4, 6, 8. Data set B is obtained by multiplying each value of A by 3 and adding 10.

  • (A) The mean of B is 25.
  • (B) The population variance of B is 45.
  • (C) The SD of B is 3 times the SD of A.
  • (D) The CV of B is larger than the CV of A.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. Which statements hold after the transformation Y = 3X + 10.
  2. Which idea applies. The mean scales and shifts, the variance scales by a squared, and CV is SD over mean.
  3. Work it out. Mean of A = 20 / 4 = 5, so mean of B = 3 times 5 + 10 = 25. Population variance of A: squared deviations 9, 1, 1, 9 sum to 20, so 20 / 4 = 5. Variance of B = 9 times 5 = 45. SD of A = sqrt(5), SD of B = sqrt(45) = 3 times sqrt(5). CV of A = sqrt(5) / 5 = 0.447. CV of B = 3 sqrt(5) / 25 = 0.268.
  4. Check. The CV of B is smaller, because the shift of 10 raises the mean but not the spread.

Answer: (A), (B) and (C). Select all that apply.

Exam-level 1: Median and mean from grouped marks (NAT, 2 marks)

The table gives the marks of 59 students, grouped into class intervals. The class 0 to under 10 has 5 students, 10 to under 20 has 12 students, 20 to under 30 has 20 students, 30 to under 40 has 9 students and 40 to under 50 has 13 students. Estimate the mean by using the midpoint of each class. Find the median using the standard grouped-data formula. Then give the mean minus the median, to 2 decimal places.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The difference between two averages of grouped data.
  2. Which idea applies. Midpoints for the mean. For the median, find the median class and interpolate inside it.
  3. Work it out. The midpoints are 5, 15, 25, 35 and 45. The sum of frequency times midpoint is 25 + 180 + 500 + 315 + 585 = 1605, so the mean is 1605 / 59 = 27.20. The median position is N / 2 = 29.5. The cumulative frequencies are 5, 17 and 37, so the median class is 20 to 30, with lower limit 20, cumulative frequency before it 17, frequency 20 and width 10. Median = 20 + (29.5 - 17) / 20 times 10 = 26.25. Mean minus median = 27.20 - 26.25 = 0.95.
  4. Check. The mean is above the median, which fits the longer tail on the right, where the 40 to 50 class holds 13 students.

Answer: 0.95 (mean 27.20, median 26.25)

classes = [(0, 10, 5), (10, 20, 12), (20, 30, 20), (30, 40, 9), (40, 50, 13)]
N = sum(f for _, _, f in classes)
mean = sum(((a + b) / 2) * f for a, b, f in classes) / N
cum = 0
for a, b, f in classes:
    if cum + f >= N / 2:
        median = a + (N / 2 - cum) / f * (b - a)
        break
    cum += f
print(f"N = {N}")
print(f"Estimated mean = {mean:.2f}")
print(f"Median = {median:.2f}")
print(f"Mean minus median = {mean - median:.2f}")

Estimated mean = 27.20
Median = 26.25
Mean minus median = 0.95

Exam-level 2: Spread after a linear change (NAT, 2 marks)

An engineer records five temperatures, x = 10, 12, 14, 16 and 18. She converts each reading to a new scale with y = 2x - 5. She then calculates the sample standard deviation of the new values y, which divides the sum of squared deviations by n - 1 rather than by n. Find this sample standard deviation to 3 decimal places. Remember how a linear change of scale affects the spread, and that the population and sample versions use different divisors.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The sample standard deviation of y, not of x.
  2. Which idea applies. Under y = 2x - 5, the spread is multiplied by 2 and the shift of -5 does not change it. The sample version divides by n - 1.
  3. Work it out. The mean of x is 14, with deviations -4, -2, 0, 2 and 4. Their squares add to 40, so the sample variance of x is 40 / 4 = 10 and its SD is the square root of 10. Therefore SD(y) = 2 times the square root of 10, which is 6.325. The population SD of y would instead be 2 times the square root of 8, which is 5.657.
  4. Check. The new values are 15, 19, 23, 27 and 31. Neighbouring values differ by 4, which is double the spacing of 2 in x, so the spread doubles.

Answer: 6.325

import statistics as st
x = [10, 12, 14, 16, 18]
y = [2 * v - 5 for v in x]
print(f"mean of y = {st.mean(y)}")
print(f"population SD of y = {round(st.pstdev(y), 3)}")
print(f"sample SD of y = {round(st.stdev(y), 3)}")
mean of y = 23
population SD of y = 5.657
sample SD of y = 6.325

Exam-level 3: Mode, median and mean ordering (MCQ, 2 marks)

A small data set of six values is 1, 2, 2, 3, 4 and 10. Which of the following orderings of the mode, the median and the mean is correct for this data set? Choose one option.

  • (A) mean less than median less than mode
  • (B) mode less than median less than mean
  • (C) median less than mode less than mean
  • (D) mode, median and mean are all equal

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The correct order of three averages for this one data set.
  2. Which idea applies. The mode is the most common value. The median is the middle value. The mean is the sum divided by the count. One very large value pulls the mean up.
  3. Work it out. The mode is 2, because it appears twice. Sorted, the values are 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 10, so the middle two are 2 and 3 and the median is 2.5. The mean is 22 / 6 = 3.67. So 2 is less than 2.5, which is less than 3.67, which is option (B).
  4. Check. Option (A) reverses the order. Option (C) fails because the median 2.5 is larger than the mode 2. Option (D) fails because the three averages are different here.

Answer: (B) mode less than median less than mean

import statistics as st
data = [1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 10]
print(f"mode = {st.mode(data)}")
print(f"median = {st.median(data)}")
print(f"mean = {round(st.mean(data), 2)}")
mode = 2
median = 2.5
mean = 3.67

Exam-level 4: Spread of two combined groups (NAT, 2 marks)

Group A has 10 measurements with mean 20 and standard deviation 3. Group B has 10 measurements with mean 30 and standard deviation 4. Treat each standard deviation as a population SD, which divides by n. Combine the two groups into one set of 20 values. Find the standard deviation of the combined set, to 3 decimal places. The combined spread includes the gap between the two group means, not only the spread inside each group.

How to think about it

  1. What is asked. The SD of one combined set built from two groups.
  2. Which idea applies. Total variance = average within-group variance + between-group variance, measured around the combined mean.
  3. Work it out. The combined mean is (10 times 20 + 10 times 30) / 20 = 25. Group A contributes 10 times (3^2 + (20 - 25)^2) = 10 times 34 = 340 to the sum of squares, and group B contributes 10 times (4^2 + (30 - 25)^2) = 10 times 41 = 410. The total is 750, and dividing by 20 gives a variance of 37.5. The SD is the square root of 37.5, which is 6.124.
  4. Check. The within-group part is (9 + 16) / 2 = 12.5, and the between-group part is 5^2 = 25. Their sum is 37.5, as the working gives.

Answer: 6.124

import math
n1, m1, s1 = 10, 20, 3
n2, m2, s2 = 10, 30, 4
M = (n1 * m1 + n2 * m2) / (n1 + n2)
var = (n1 * (s1 ** 2 + (m1 - M) ** 2) + n2 * (s2 ** 2 + (m2 - M) ** 2)) / (n1 + n2)
print(f"combined mean = {M}")
print(f"combined variance = {var}")
print(f"combined SD = {round(math.sqrt(var), 3)}")
combined mean = 25.0
combined variance = 37.5
combined SD = 6.124
